## What changes

- **Proposition 29.** In an anisotropic system with t = 7 and dim V = 9, alpha(V) is irreducible,
  every element has rank at most 4, and some element has rank 3 or 4. An element u of rank 4 is in
  the tight case k = 3 of Proposition 26, in Case A or in Case B. If no element has rank 4, every
  element of rank 3 is in one of three configurations, (A5), (B6) and (B5) below, as at t = 6
  (Corollary 39 of [a10.md](a10.md)).
- **Lemma 77 (Case A at rank 4).** The step of Lemma 52 ([a5.md](a5.md)) that handled x0 in Im A_u
  carries over: **x0 is not in Im A_u**. Then ker A_u n Im A_u is a plane D, and D lies in the kernel
  of every element over the coset x0 + Rad of the form C of [case-a-radical.md](case-a-radical.md).
  Every such element has rank exactly 4. The values of sigma over x0 + D all lie in D. A_u^2 has rank
  2, and phi^(-1)(ker A_u), of dimension 5, carries an anisotropic system with values in
  ker A_u^2, also of dimension 5. The step of Lemma 52 for x0 outside Im A_u stops at this last
  point: at t = 5 D was a line, and two values could not fit in it.
- **Lemma 78 (Case B at rank 4).** No element n != 0 of U+ with sigma(n) in Z_N = span beta(U+, U+)
  has A_n = 0. Such an n has rank 1, span(sigma(n)) + beta(n, V) = Im A_u, and
  dim U+(n) = 6 = 1 + A(5). So shape
  (d2) of Proposition 27 survives only in this rigid form. Shape (d1) was already ruled out by
  Lemma 42 of case-b-shapes.md.
- **Corollary 51.** In shape (c) at rank 4, n -> A_n is one-to-one on U+. This is Corollary 35 of
  [a8.md](a8.md) at (t, rank) = (7, 4).
- **Lemma 79 ((A5) at rank 3).** The count of Lemma 62 is tight in (A5). So **Im A_u lies in
  ker A_u** (A_u^2 = 0), ker A_u = Im A_u + span(x0) lies in phi(V), and for every v with
  phi(v) = x0, ker A_v n ker A_(u + v) = Im A_u, with A_v and A_(u + v) of rank exactly 3. Every
  element of ker phi kills ker A_u.

## Setting

As in [a10.md](a10.md) and [a23.md](a23.md): X = F_2^t, sigma : V -> X quadratic with polar form
beta, h -> A_h linear, and

    A_(h') sigma(h) = (I + A_h) beta(h, h')                                    (*)
    A_(h') beta(h_1, h_2) = A_(h_1) beta(h_2, h') + A_(h_2) beta(h_1, h').      (Q)

For u in V put x0 = sigma(u), phi = beta(u, .), P = phi(V), X_1 = ker A_u, R = Im A_u,
rho = rank A_u, k = t - rho, d = dim(X_1 n R) and U+ = {h : phi(h) in span(x0)}. We use (1) to (3)
of a10.md:

    A_h x0 = (I + A_u) phi(h)                         for all h,                (1)
    A_u beta(h_1, h_2) = A_(h_1) phi(h_2) + A_(h_2) phi(h_1)   for all h_1, h_2,  (2)
    A_h x0 = phi(h) and A_u sigma(h) = 0              for h in U+,              (3)

and also (*) with h' = u, which reads A_u sigma(h) = (I + A_h) phi(h). "h is over p" means
phi(h) = p. Throughout, t = 7 and dim V = 9, and the system is anisotropic. The bounds used are
A(s) = M(s) = s for s <= 6 (Theorems 26, 27 and 31). Only A(6) <= 7 is needed in place of
Theorem 31 (Theorem 28 also gives it), and only in Lemma 78 and Corollary 51.

## Proposition 29 (the reduction)

**Proposition 29.** Let an anisotropic system have t = 7 and dim V = 9. Then:

(a) alpha(V) is irreducible, every non-zero element has rank 3 or 4 or at most 2, and some element
    has rank 3 or 4;

(b) an element u of rank 4 has k = 3 and dim V = t + k - 1. Every A_h has rank at most 4,
    dim U+ = 3, and u is in Case A (phi onto X) or in Case B (P a hyperplane missing x0,
    U+ = ker phi);

(c) if no element has rank 4, then every element u of rank 3 (k = 4) is in one of

    (A5) x0 is in P, P has dimension 6, U+ has dimension 4 and ker phi dimension 3;
    (B6) x0 is not in P, P is a hyperplane, and U+ = ker phi has dimension 3;
    (B5) x0 is not in P, P has dimension 5, and U+ = ker phi has dimension 4.

    In (B6) and (B5), every n in ker phi has A_n x0 = 0, sigma(n) in X_1, and A_n maps P + span(x0)
    (all of X in (B6)) into R.

*Proof.* (a) An invariant subspace of dimension d, 0 < d < 7, would give
dim V <= M(7 - d) + A(d) = 7 (Lemma 27(c)). If every element had rank <= 2, Lemmas 28 and 29 would
give dim V <= 7. An element of rank s gives dim V <= 7 + A(6 - s) by Lemma 30: that is 8 for s = 5
and 7 for s = 6, and rank 7 does not occur, since A_h sigma(h) = 0 with sigma(h) != 0 for h != 0.

(b) Lemma 30 for u reads 9 <= 7 + A(2) = 9, so this is the tight case dim V = t + k - 1 with k = 3.
Proposition 26 needs A(s) = s for s <= 2. Its part (d) allows Case A, since 2 rho = 8 >= t + 1.

(c) Now the largest rank is 3, so Corollary 38 of a10.md applies to every element u of rank 3 and
every v with phi(v) = x0. Lemma 30 gives dim U+ <= 1 + A(3) = 4. If x0 is in P, Corollary 38 gives
c = 7 - dim P >= ceil((8 - 6) / 2) = 1. So codim U+ = dim P - 1 <= 5, dim U+ >= 4, and hence
dim U+ = 4 and dim P = 6. This is (A5). If x0 is not in P, then U+ = ker phi has dimension
9 - dim P <= 4, so dim P >= 5, and dim P <= 6 as P != X. These are (B6) and (B5). The last sentence
is the argument of Corollary 39: by (3), A_n x0 = 0 and A_u sigma(n) = 0, and by (2) with h_1 = n,
A_n phi(h) = A_u beta(n, h) lies in R. QED

## Lemma 77 (Case A at rank 4)

Let u have rank 4 and be in Case A. Then ker phi has dimension 9 - 7 = 2; write ker phi = span(u, n).
Fix v over x0, put w = u + v, K_h = ker A_h, and s = sigma(v). Then U+ = span(u, n, v).

By (3), every n' in ker phi has A_(n') x0 = 0 and sigma(n') in X_1. By (2) with h_1 = n',
A_(n') phi(h) = A_u beta(n', h) lies in R, and phi is onto, so **Im A_(n') lies in R**. So, as in
case-a-radical.md (where ker phi was span(u)), the class of A_h modulo R depends only on p = phi(h),
and

    C(p, q) = [A_h q] in X/R,   for any h over p,

is a well-defined bilinear form. By (2) taken modulo R it is symmetric. By (*) with h' = u,
C(p, p) = [p], and by (1), C(p, x0) = [p]. Let Rad = {p : C(p, q) = 0 for all q}. The proofs of
Lemma 51(a), (b) and (d) use only these facts and (*), so they hold here word for word:
Rad lies in R; x0 is not in Rad (C(x0, .) = [.] != 0, as R != X); and for p in x0 + Rad and h over
p, I + A_h maps X into R and sigma(h) lies in Rad.

**Lemma 77.** In this setting:

(a) Lemma 33 holds with equality (t = 2k + 1), for every v' over x0: A_(v') and A_(u + v') have
    rank 4, their kernels meet in a plane of X_1 n R, and s lies in that plane, outside span(x0).
    s lies in Rad.

(b) For p in x0 + Rad and h over p, A_h maps R into R, ker A_h lies in R, and the restriction P_h of
    A_h to R has rank at most 1. Also sigma(h) lies in ker P_h, and P_h + P_(h + u) = a, the
    restriction of A_u to R.

(c) x0 is not in R.

(d) D = X_1 n R is a plane, X_1 = span(x0) + D, and a has rank 2 and kernel D. For p in x0 + Rad
    and h over p, P_h and P_(h + u) have rank exactly 1, ker P_h n ker P_(h + u) = D, and A_h has
    rank exactly 4 with D inside its kernel. D lies in Rad, and Rad != R.

(e) For r in D and h over x0 + r, sigma(h) lies in D and is neither 0 nor r. In particular
    D = span(s, s'), where s' = sigma(h) for any h over x0 + s.

(f) Every A_(n') with n' in ker phi kills X_1.

(g) For r in D and every h over r, A_u beta(v, h) = r. A_u^2 has rank 2, and X_2 = ker A_u^2 has
    dimension 5.

(h) V' = phi^(-1)(X_1), of dimension 5, has all its values of sigma and beta in X_2, and they span
    X_2. So V' carries an anisotropic system with values in X_2 (Lemma 26(a)), with
    dim V' = dim X_2 = 5 = A(5).

*Proof.* (a) Proposition 26(d) applies to every v' over x0 (Lemma 62 allows any element of
v + ker phi), and t = 7 = 2k + 1 is the equality case of Lemma 33. Its proof puts K_(v') and
K_(u + v') inside R, so their intersection, of dimension k - 1 = 2, lies in X_1 n R. By Lemma 62(c),
s lies in X_1 n K_v n K_w and not in span(x0). s = sigma(v) with v over x0, so s lies in Rad.

(b) A_h = I + N_h with N_h mapping X into R. So A_h maps R into R, and A_h x = 0 gives x = N_h x,
which lies in R. Hence ker A_h = ker P_h, of dimension at least 7 - 4 = 3 inside the 4-dimensional
R, so rank P_h <= 1. A_h sigma(h) = 0 (Lemma 21(b)). h + u is over the same p, and
A_(h + u) = A_h + A_u.

(c) This is Step 5 of the proof of Lemma 52. Suppose x0 lies in R. For r in Rad n X_1, p = x0 + r and
h over p, (1) gives P_h x0 = A_h x0 = (I + A_u) p = p, as p lies in X_1. As p != 0 and rank P_h <= 1,
P_h has image span(p), and so has P_(h + u). So a = P_h + P_(h + u) has its image in span(p). Take
r = 0 and r = s (s is in Rad n X_1 by (a)). Then Im a lies in span(x0) n span(x0 + s) = 0. So
A_u R = 0, that is, R lies in X_1, but dim R = 4 > 3 = dim X_1. Contradiction.

(d) By (a) X_1 n R contains a plane, and by (c) it does not contain x0, so it is a plane, D, and
X_1 = span(x0) + D. Then ker a = X_1 n R = D and rank a = 2. For p in x0 + Rad and h over p, the two
maps P_h and P_(h + u) have rank at most 1 and sum a of rank 2. So each has rank 1, their images are
independent, and a x = 0 gives P_h x = P_(h + u) x in Im P_h n Im P_(h + u) = 0. So
ker P_h n ker P_(h + u) = ker a = D. Then dim ker A_h = dim ker P_h = 3. By (a) and (e) below
(which uses only what is proved so far), s and s' are independent vectors of Rad that span D, so
D lies in Rad. If Rad = R, R would be invariant (Lemma 51(b)), against (a) of Proposition 29.

(e) Let h be over x0 + r. Then sigma(h + u) = sigma(h) + x0 + phi(h) = sigma(h) + r. sigma(h) lies in
ker P_h and sigma(h + u) in ker P_(h + u). r is in D, which lies in both kernels by (d), so sigma(h)
lies in ker P_h n ker P_(h + u) = D. Anisotropy gives sigma(h) != 0 and sigma(h + u) != 0, that is,
sigma(h) != r. For r = s: s' lies in D and outside span(s), so D = span(s, s').

(The order of (d) and (e) is safe. For p in x0 + Rad, (d) proves ker P_h n ker P_(h + u) = D
without using D inside Rad. (e) for r = s needs only x0 + s in x0 + Rad, which holds by (a). It gives
s' in D, and s' lies in Rad (Lemma 51(d)), so D = span(s, s') lies in Rad. Only then does (e) follow
for every r in D, because x0 + r now lies in x0 + Rad.)

(f) A_(n') x0 = 0 by (3). v and v + n' are both over x0, and both kernels contain D by (d). So
A_(n') = A_(v + n') + A_v kills D, and hence X_1.

(g) Let h be over r in D. By (2), A_u beta(v, h) = A_v phi(h) + A_h phi(v) = A_v r + A_h x0. Here
A_v r = 0 (D lies in K_v), and A_h x0 = (I + A_u) r = r by (1). So A_u beta(v, h) = r. A_u^2 maps X
onto A_u R = Im a, of dimension 2. So X_2 has dimension 5, and it equals
{x : A_u x in X_1}, since dim X_1 + dim(X_1 n R) = 5.

(h) Take the basis u, n, v, g_1, g_2 of V', with phi(g_1) = s and phi(g_2) = s', and let G be the
span of g_1 and g_2. Then sigma(u) = x0, sigma(v) = s, sigma(n) lies in X_1 by (3), and
beta(u, .) = phi takes values in X_1 on V'. By (2), A_u beta(n, v) = A_n x0 + A_v phi(n) = 0, so
beta(n, v) lies in X_1. For g in G, every element v + g + lambda u + mu n is over x0 + phi(g), with
phi(g) in D, so its sigma lies in D by (e). From sigma(v + g) in D:
sigma(g) + beta(v, g) lies in D. From sigma(v + n) and sigma(v + g + n) in D: sigma(n) + beta(v, n)
and beta(g, n) lie in D. From sigma(v + g_1 + g_2) in D: beta(g_1, g_2) lies in D. By (g),
A_u beta(v, g) = phi(g) lies in X_1, so beta(v, g) lies in X_2, and then so does sigma(g). So every
value on the basis, and hence every value of sigma and beta on V', lies in X_2. They span it: x0, s
and s' = sigma(v + g_1) span X_1, and A_u beta(v, g_1) = s and A_u beta(v, g_2) = s' are
independent, so beta(v, g_1) and beta(v, g_2) are independent modulo X_1. QED

**What stops here.** At t = 5 (Lemma 52, Step 4), D was the line span(z), and the two values s' and
s' + z of Step (e) could not both be non-zero in it. At t = 7, D is a plane and they fit. So the
contradiction has to come from elsewhere, for example from the four elements of V outside V' (over
a complement of X_1). (h) says that V' is as large as A(5) = 5 allows, but that alone is not a
contradiction.

## Lemma 78 (Case B at rank 4: values of beta on U+)

Let u have rank 4 and be in Case B, with N = U+ = ker phi of dimension 3 and Z_N = span beta(N, N).
Proposition 27 applies (A(s) = s for s <= k = 3). For n in N put z = sigma(n),
C = span(z) + beta(n, V), c = dim C - 1 and U+(n) = {h : beta(n, h) in span(z)}.

**Lemma 78.** Let n != 0 in N have z = sigma(n) in Z_N. Then rank A_n = 1, C = R, and
dim U+(n) = 6 = 1 + A(5). In particular n != u, and shape (d2) of Proposition 27 occurs only with
rank A_(n_2) = 1 and beta(n_2, V) = R.

*Proof.* Step 1 of the proof of Lemma 57 ([a7.md](a7.md)) uses only (Z) of case-b-shapes.md and
Proposition 26(e), and puts C inside R, of dimension 4. So c <= 3, and by Step 2 there,
dim U+(n) = 9 - c >= 6. Let s = rank A_n. Lemma 30 for n gives dim U+(n) <= 1 + A(6 - s).

- If s >= 2: dim U+(n) <= 1 + A(4) = 5 < 6. Impossible. (This includes n = u, of rank 4.)
- If s = 0: by Lemma 60(a) of [a8.md](a8.md), C lies in X_1, so in X_1 n R, and c <= 2. If c <= 1,
  Steps 4 and 5 of the proof of Lemma 57 give an anisotropic system of dimension 8 - c with values
  in F_2^6 and an invariant subspace of dimension c. So 8 <= A(6) if c = 0, which is false as
  A(6) <= 7, or 7 <= M(5) + A(1) = 6 if c = 1, which is false. If c = 2, C has dimension 3 = dim X_1, so C = X_1,
  which contains x0. Lemma 60(c) then gives X_1 n R = 0, but C = X_1 lies in R. Contradiction.
- So s = 1, dim U+(n) <= 1 + A(5) = 6, hence c = 3 and C = R. In shape (d2), z = delta lies in
  beta(n_2, V) (delta = beta(n_1, n_2)), so beta(n_2, V) = C = R. In general, C = R has dimension 4,
  and beta(n, V) has codimension at most 1 in it.

For the last sentence: in shape (d2), sigma(n_2) = delta = beta(n_1, n_2) lies in Z_N. QED

Lemma 44 of case-b-shapes.md gives, at (t, k) = (7, 3), only 9 <= 4 + A(6 - s), that is, s <= 1.
Lemma 78 adds that s = 0 is impossible, using Lemma 60(c). It also says that n is then an element
of rank 1 for which Lemma 30 is tight: U+(n) / span(n) carries an anisotropic system of dimension 5
with values in ker A_n / span(z), of dimension 5.

## Corollary 51 (shape (c) at rank 4)

**Corollary 51.** Let u have rank 4 in Case B at t = 7 and dim V = 9, in shape (c) (beta = 0 on N).
Then d = dim(X_1 n R) >= 2, sigma maps N linearly onto X_1, every A_n (n in N) kills X_1, and
A_n != 0 for every n != 0 in N.

*Proof.* The first three facts are Proposition 27(c1) and (c4) with k = 3. For the last, Corollary 35
of a8.md (which needs A(s) = s for s <= k = 3 and shape (c)) puts x0 outside C, so c <= k - 2 = 1.
U+(n) / span(n) then carries an anisotropic system of dimension t + k - 2 - c = 8 - c with values
in F_2^6 and an invariant subspace of dimension c. Both 8 <= A(6) and 7 <= M(5) + A(1) = 6 are
false. QED

So with Lemma 42 of case-b-shapes.md (no shape (d1)) and Lemma 78: an element of rank 4 in Case B is
in shape (c) with n -> A_n one-to-one on N, or in shape (d2) with rank A_(n_2) = 1 and
beta(n_2, V) = R. In shape (c), the matrices M_n of Lemma 38 (shape-c.md) are 4 x 4 and commute.
Schur's bound allows a commutative space of dimension floor(16 / 4) + 1 = 5, and dim N = 3, so the
argument of Theorem 29 (which needed a zero M_n) does not apply.

## Lemma 79 ((A5) at rank 3)

**Lemma 79.** Let the system have no element of rank 4, and let u of rank 3 be in (A5). Let v be over
x0, w = u + v, K_v = ker A_v and K_w = ker A_w. Then:

(a) K_v n P = K_w n P = K_v n K_w = R; so R lies in X_1 (A_u^2 = 0), x0 is not in R, and
    X_1 = R + span(x0) lies in P;

(b) K_v and K_w have dimension 4, so A_v and A_w have rank exactly 3, and neither kernel lies in P;

(c) s = sigma(v) lies in R and is not 0;

(d) every A_(n') with n' in ker phi kills X_1 and maps P into R.

*Proof.* (a), (b) Lemma 62(d) applies (ranks are at most 3), with rho = 3 and c = 1:
2 rho + 2c = 8 = t + 1, so every inequality in its proof is an equality. dim K_v >= 4 and P is a
hyperplane, so dim(K_v n P) >= 3. K_v n P lies in R (Lemma 62(b)), of dimension 3, so
K_v n P = R. The same holds for K_w. So R lies in K_v n K_w, which lies in X_1 and misses x0
(Lemma 62(b)). As dim X_1 = 4, dim(K_v n K_w) <= 3, so K_v n K_w = R. Then R lies in X_1, x0 is not
in R, and X_1 = R + span(x0). R lies in P (it is K_v n P), and x0 is in P, so X_1 lies in P.
dim K_v <= dim(K_v n P) + 1 = 4, so dim K_v = 4, and K_v is not inside P. The same for K_w.

(c) Lemma 62(c) puts s in X_1 n K_v n K_w = R, outside span(x0); s != 0 by anisotropy.

(d) Every v' over x0 is a possible v, so R lies in K_(v') for every v' in v + ker phi. Then
A_(n') = A_(v + n') + A_v kills R. By (3), A_(n') x0 = phi(n') = 0. So A_(n') kills X_1. By (2) with
h_1 = n', A_(n') phi(h) = A_u beta(n', h) lies in R. QED

At t = 6 the configuration (A4) had slack 1 in Lemma 62 (6 + 2 >= 7). At t = 7, (A5) has none. This
is the shape that Theorem 29 met at dim V = 10 (Corollary 31: A_u^2 = 0), now one dimension lower
and outside the tight case.

## What this leaves

A(7) <= 8 holds if and only if none of the following exists in an anisotropic system with t = 7
and dim V = 9:

    rank 4, Case A           Lemma 77: x0 outside R, D = X_1 n R a plane, A_u^2 of rank 2
    rank 4, Case B, (c)      Corollary 51: d >= 2, n -> A_n one-to-one, sigma: N -> X_1 linear
    rank 4, Case B, (d2)     Lemma 78: rank A_(n_2) = 1, beta(n_2, V) = R
    rank 3 only, (A5)        Lemma 79: A_u^2 = 0, X_1 = R + span(x0) inside P
    rank 3 only, (B6), (B5)  Proposition 29(c)

The t = 6 method (fix A_u up to the stabiliser of its kernel and image, Lemma 70 of
[a17.md](a17.md), then search each class) is the planned next step. For each row, the next notes
will count the classes by hand first. Then the jobs and their predictions will be written down before
anything runs. Case A is the natural first target. Lemma 77 fixes x0, D and the restriction of A_u
to R up to small choices, and at t = 5 and t = 6 the Case A searches were the fastest (jobs m15 and
m24, seconds per instance).

## What does not follow

- A(7) <= 8 is open: 7 <= A(7) <= 9. No row of the table above is ruled out.
- Lemma 77 does not exclude Case A at rank 4. Its parts (e) and (h) are consistent with every
  check tried by hand.
- (B6) and (B5) have no new constraints here beyond those of Corollary 39, which a10.md proved for
  t = 6 and which hold at t = 7 by the same argument.
