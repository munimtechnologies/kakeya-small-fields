## Corollary 54 (no shape (d2) at rank 4 for t = 7)

**Corollary 54 (computer-assisted).** In an anisotropic system with t = 7 and dim V = 9, no element
of rank 4 is in Case B, shape (d2). So, with Corollary 52 (no Case A) and Lemma 42 (no shape (d1)),
every element of rank 4 is in Case B, shape (c), with the properties of Corollary 51.

*Proof.* Let u have rank 4 in Case B, shape (d2). By Lemma 37 (with A(s) = s for s <= 3, Theorem 27)
the system has, in one of the two forms d2c0 and d2c1, the normal form of [a28.md](a28.md):
x0 = e_0, P = span(e_1, ..., e_6), ker A_u = W = span(e_0, e_1, e_2), N = span(u, v_1, v_2),
sigma(v_1) = e_1, sigma(v_2) = beta(v_1, v_2) = delta with delta = e_2 (d2c0) or e_0 + e_2 (d2c1),
A_(v_1) : e_0, e_1 -> 0, e_2 -> delta, A_(v_2) kills W, and phi(v_(2+j)) = e_j. By Lemma 78, delta
lies in R = Im A_u, A_(v_2) has rank 1, and beta(v_2, V) = R; by (2) and (3) of a10.md, A_(v_1) and
A_(v_2) map X = P + span(x0) into R. Every g in K keeps all of this (a28.md), so R may be replaced by
one of the 7 or 5 representatives of Lemma 84 (confirmed by machine, "selfcheck_hand_r_orbits"),
and then A_u by any conjugate under Stab_K(R). The 20160 matrices with kernel W and image R fall
into classes under 24 random units of Stab_K(R); every unit was checked to lie in Stab_K(R), so each
computed class lies inside one true class and the 119 + 91 classes cover every A_u. Each class
gives one CNF (the encoding of case_b_shapes.encode_shape(7, 3, form) plus the unit clauses for A_u
and the linear conditions above), and a model of the system would be a model of the CNF of its
class. Jobs m43a to m43d found all 210 UNSAT (CaDiCaL 1.9.5, no proof certificates saved). The last
sentence is Lemma 42, Corollary 52 and Corollary 51 together. QED

What it rests on: Lemma 37 and Lemma 78 (by hand; Lemma 78 uses Lemma 60(c) and A(6) <= 7), Lemma
84 (by hand, matched by machine), the encoding of case_b.py and case_b_shapes.py (whose selfchecks
and controls held in this job and in jobs m23, m25, m40), and 210 solver answers. It does not rest
on Lemma 87: the class count is the machine's, and a missed generator would only have made more
classes, each still solved.

So "no element of rank 4 at t = 7 and dim V = 9" is now exactly "shape (c) is empty": the 54 classes
of Lemma 86, job m44. And with it Corollary 53's hypothesis, and (A5), fall.

## What the pattern suggests, and what it does not prove

1. **Shape (d2) by hand (Lemma 88 target).** 210 instances of 48,626 variables and 286,340 clauses,
   all but one refuted in under 5 s, means the solver finds a short refutation from the fixed data:
   sigma and A on u, v_1, v_2 (Lemma 37), A_u on e_3, ..., e_6, the images of A_(v_1) and A_(v_2) in R,
   beta(v_2, V) = R and rank A_(v_2) = 1 (Lemma 78). Lemma 78 already says that n = v_2 is an element
   of rank 1 for which Lemma 30 is tight: U+(v_2) / span(v_2) carries an anisotropic system of
   dimension 5 with values in ker A_(v_2) / span(delta), of dimension 5, and such a system is itself
   in a tight case. The hand argument to look for is the one the solver finds: start from (*) and (Q)
   at the pairs (u, v_2), (v_1, v_2) and (v_2, v_(2+j)), with A_(v_2) = lambda(.) y of rank 1, and
   derive a value of sigma that must be 0. If found, it replaces Corollary 54 by a hand lemma and
   removes 210 solver answers from what A(7) <= 8 rests on. The one slow class (68 s) is the place
   to look for what makes the others easy.
2. **(B5) against (B6).** The two shapes differ in dim ker phi: 4 in (B5), 3 in (B6). Lemma 30 for u
   gives dim U+ <= 1 + A(3) = 4, so in (B5) the bound is tight and U+ / span(u) carries an
   anisotropic system of dimension 3 with values in X_1 / span(x0), of dimension 3 = A(3); in (B6)
   that quotient system has dimension 2 inside a 3-dimensional space and is not tight. The solver's
   extra speed in (B5) (80 percent of the first half in a 40 s pass, against 13 percent of (B6) at
   250 s) and the kind-L pattern, which holds in (B5) and not so far in (B6), both point at this
   tightness as the hand fact to use, in the way Lemma 78 used the tightness of Lemma 30 at n for
   shape (d2). The statement to aim for: in (B5), x0 is not in Im A_u, or at least not when
   d <= 2. It would close 9 pair orbits of (B5) (97 classes). The analogous statement in (B6) has no
   such support and should not be assumed.
3. **d = 3 (R inside ker A_u, A_u^2 = 0), corrected count.** There are 13 such classes, not 10: 3 in
   (B6) (pair orbits 0, 1, 2) and 10 in (B5) (pair orbits 0 with 3 classes, 2 with 2, and 1, 20,
   31, 32, 33 with 1 each). Eight have been tried (3 in (B6), 5 in (B5)) and all eight stayed open;
   five are m42d's (two of (B5)'s pair 0, and pairs 1, 31, 33). a33.md counted "7 tried plus 3
   untried", missing the two untried classes of (B5)'s pair 0 and m42b's class of (B6)'s pair 1.

Nothing above is a lemma. The decision rule stands: a class counts only when the solver answers.
