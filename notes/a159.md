## The question, restated as information loss

Theorem 2 of this repository says that the Kakeya growth base of F_q is
beta_q = lim K(n, q)^(1/n) = inf_k 2^(h_k / k), where h_k = min over maps P : F_q^k -> F_q^k of
h(P) = (1/q) sum_t H(P(X) + tX), with X uniform on F_q^k. Since Y_t = P(X) + tX is a function of X,
H(Y_t) = k log2 q - H(X | Y_t). So everything is governed by the **information loss**

    Lambda(P) = sum over t in F_q of H(X | P(X) + tX)      (bits),

with h(P) = k log2 q - Lambda(P)/q and S(P) = q^k Lambda(P) (S is the fibre score used in a157).

**Conjecture C_q(k).** Lambda(P) <= k (q - 1) for every map P of F_q^k.

Theorem 1 is C_q(1): the q lines through two points of F_q meet in exactly one slope, which gives
Lambda <= q - 1. Products of k one-dimensional optima give Lambda = k (q - 1) exactly, so C_q(k)
says precisely that h_k = k h_1. "Blocks never help" for every k is therefore equivalent to

    beta_q = q * 2^(-(q-1)/q)      for this q,

i.e. it would make Theorem 1's bound **the exact Kakeya growth constant of F_q**. For comparison,
the best lower bound we know of comes from Bukh and Chao (arXiv:2108.00074),
|K| >= q^n / (2 - 1/q)^(n - 1), which gives beta_q >= q^2/(2q - 1). For q = 3 the gap is
1.800 <= beta_3 <= 1.890; for q = 4 it is 2.286 <= beta_4 <= 2.378.

What is known about C_q(k):
- **q = 2: true for every k.** With A = P(X), H(A) + H(A + X) >= H(A, A + X) = H(X) = k, so
  Lambda <= k. (Equivalently beta_2 = sqrt 2, which also follows from K + K covering F_2^n.)
- q = 3, k = 2: true, by the earlier exhaustive search in this repository (216 maps attain the
  maximum).
- Ruled out as counterexamples, for every q and k: maps respecting a proper subspace (Theorem 4)
  and additive maps (Theorem 34, a157).
- q = 4, 5 with k = 2: annealing is pre-registered (K2, K3 in a157), not yet run.

## Theorem 35 (one-coordinate reduction)

Let P : V -> V with V = F_q^k, k >= 2. Let l : V -> F_q be a nonzero linear functional, v a vector
with l(v) = 1, W = ker l, and pi(x) = x - l(x) v the projection onto W along v. For a in F_q let
Q_a : W -> W be Q_a(w) = pi(P(a v + w)). Then

    Lambda(P)  <=  Lambda_l(P) + (1/q) sum over a of Lambda(Q_a),
    where Lambda_l(P) = sum over t of H(l(X) | P(X) + tX)

and Lambda(Q_a) is the loss of Q_a on W (with X' uniform on W, a copy of F_q^(k-1)).

*Proof.* By the chain rule, H(X | Y_t) = H(l(X) | Y_t) + H(X | Y_t, l(X)). The map
x -> (l(x), pi(x)) is a bijection V -> F_q x W, so given l(X) = a we have X = a v + X' with
X' = pi(X) uniform on W, and H(X | Y_t, l(X)) is the average over a of H(X' | Y_t, l(X) = a).
Conditioning on less can only increase entropy, and pi(Y_t) is a function of Y_t, so
H(X' | Y_t, l(X) = a) <= H(X' | pi(Y_t), l(X) = a). On the event l(X) = a,

    pi(Y_t) = pi(P(a v + X')) + t pi(a v) + t pi(X') = Q_a(X') + t X',

because pi(v) = 0 and pi(X') = X'. So H(X' | pi(Y_t), l(X) = a) = H(X' | Q_a(X') + t X'). Summing
over t gives the claim. QED

The bound is tight on products: for P = f x g with l the first coordinate and v the first basis
vector, Lambda_l(P) = Lambda(f), every Q_a equals g, and the right side is Lambda(f) + Lambda(g) =
Lambda(P).

**Corollary 125.** Suppose C_q(k - 1) holds, and

> **Lemma L_q(k) (open).** Every map P of F_q^k has a nonzero functional l with
> Lambda_l(P) <= q - 1.

Then C_q(k) holds. Hence L_q(2) for all q would prove that blocks of size two never help
(h_2 = 2 h_1 for every q), and L_q(k) for all k would determine beta_q exactly.

L asks for **one direction in which the map loses no more information than a one-dimensional
map can**. It holds with equality for products, along a coordinate. A weaker sufficient condition
is

> **L'_q(k).** Every P has some (l, v) whose right side in Theorem 35 is at most k (q - 1).

L' follows from L plus C_q(k - 1), and L' alone implies C_q(k). If L' fails for some map at
(q, k) = (3, 2), where C_3(2) is true, then this one-coordinate route cannot prove C even where it
holds, and a different idea is needed.

Why L is not obvious: summing Lambda_l over all (q^k - 1)/(q - 1) directions does not help. For
q = 4, k = 2 and the coordinatewise square map, the five directions give 3, 3, 3, 6 and 6 bits.
The average is 4.2, above q - 1 = 3. Only the best direction works. A minimum over directions is
what has to be controlled.

## Census (job kakeya-lemmal-v1; code in this commit, not yet run)

`lemma_l.py` computes Lambda, every Lambda_l, and the best chain bound of Theorem 35:
- **exhaustive**: every map with P(0) = 0, using numpy. Constants change nothing. This covers
  (q, k) = (2, 2) (64 maps), (2, 3) (2,097,152) and (3, 2) (43,046,721).
- **additive**: every F_2-linear map at (4, 2) and (2, 4) (65,536 each).
- **anneal**: an adversarial search that maximises min_l Lambda_l. It runs at (4, 2) with two
  seeds, and at (5, 2), (3, 3) and (2, 4). Every map that breaks L gets its best chain bound
  checked.

Any map with Lambda > k (q - 1) would be a counterexample to C, which means a new Kakeya bound. It
is decided exactly in integers (prod s^s > 2^(k(q-1)q^k)).

`test_lemma_l.py` checks the following:
- the field axioms;
- the conditional entropies against the joint law;
- C_q(1) exhaustively for q <= 5;
- C_2(k) on random maps;
- that products are tight in Theorem 35 for seven (q, k);
- Theorem 35 itself for every (l, v) on about 190 random maps (half of them with deliberately
  large fibres);
- the numpy evaluator against the pure-Python one.

This commit was published only if those tests passed.

**Pre-registered predictions** (written before any census run):

- **E1.** No census map has Lambda > k (q - 1). The (3, 2) maximum is exactly 4 and the (2, 3)
  maximum exactly 3. This re-confirms C_3(2) with independent code.
- **E2.** For q = 2, L holds for every map at (2, 2) and (2, 3), and for every F_2-linear map at
  (2, 4).
- **E3.** At (3, 2), L **fails** for at least one map, but L' holds for every map (largest chain
  excess <= 0). This is a guess. The average over directions can already exceed q - 1 (the
  square-map example above), and nothing we can prove forces a good direction once Lambda is well
  above q - 1 and the loss is spread over many directions.
- **E4.** Every F_2-linear map at (4, 2) satisfies L.
- **E5.** No anneal finds a counterexample to C or a map that breaks L'.

How to read the outcomes:
- If L survives everywhere, L_q(2) becomes the target. It is a statement about one map on F_q^2,
  and proving it would settle the k = 2 block question for every q.
- If only L' survives, the reduction lives on in its chain form.
- If L' fails at (3, 2), the route is closed, and that negative result gets published too.
