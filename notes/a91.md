## Proposition 37 (type (N4) at (8, 11))

**Proposition 37 (computer-assisted).** In an anisotropic system with t = 8 and dim V = 11, no
element u of rank 4 has A_u of type (N4).

*Proof.* Let u be such an element. By Corollary 121 ([a84.md](a84.md)) u is in Case B in shape (c),
and by Lemma 94(a) type (N4) means d = 4. By Lemmas 95 and 96 ([a85.md](a85.md),
[a86.md](a86.md)) the space W = span{M_n : n in N_Y} lies in one of the 23 orbits of m93 that meet
the condition of (N4), and the matrices M_(v_1), M_(v_2), M_(v_3) may be fixed to the recorded
basis of its representative. Then:

    orbits 0, 1, 4, 5, 6, 7, 11, 14, 19, 25,   Lemma 100 (by hand): W contains a non-zero
      28, 34, 48, 57 (45360 spaces)            idempotent, which Lemma 99(c) forbids
    orbits 20, 21, 30, 35, 39, 41              the reduced system of Lemma 98 has no solution,
      (2100 spaces)                            even without anisotropy (job m98a, a complete
                                               search over the choices of G, with the
                                               invertible S searched over the whole solution
                                               space)
    orbits 18, 29, 40 (135 spaces)             the CNF of m95a is UNSAT, with a DRAT proof
                                               VERIFIED by drat-trim

In every case there is no such system. QED

For the last three orbits the CNF of [a87.md](a87.md) is the only route. Two facts make its answer
count: the encoder has a positive control at t = 8 (without anisotropy, orbits 0, 1, 6, 14 and 29
have models, each checked against the hypotheses independently of the encoder, a90.md), and the
reduced system read off those models agrees with the hand lemmas (m98a above). For the other 20
orbits, m95a's certified CNFs give a second, independent route.

## Corollary 122 (no element of rank 4 at (8, 11))

**Corollary 122 (computer-assisted).** No anisotropic system with t = 8 and dim V = 11 has an
element of rank 4.

*Proof.* Let u have rank 4. By Corollary 121, d = dim(ker A_u n Im A_u) is 3 or 4. If d = 4, A_u is
of type (N4) (Lemma 94(a)), which Proposition 37 rules out. If d = 3 (types (N3) and (F3)),
Lemma 97 ([a87.md](a87.md)) puts the system in one of the CNFs of m95b, one per orbit of W with an
invariant hyperplane and per row vector f with f B_i in {0, f}: 159 CNFs. All 159 are UNSAT (job
m95b, and again in job m98b with DRAT proofs VERIFIED by drat-trim). QED

The control for the d = 3 CNFs is the one above: without anisotropy, 14 of them have models that
pass every independent check. So the encoder at d = 3 does not refute everything regardless of
anisotropy.

## What A(8) <= 10 needs now

Proposition 36 ([a83.md](a83.md)): a system with t = 8 and dim V = 11 has an element of rank 3 or
4, and if none has rank 4, every element u of rank 3 (k = 5) has x0 = sigma(u) outside P = phi(V)
and is in

    (B7)_8  P is a hyperplane, and ker phi has dimension 4;
    (B6)_8  P has dimension 6, and ker phi has dimension 5.

By Corollary 122 the hypothesis "none has rank 4" now always holds. So **A(8) <= 10 holds if
(B7)_8 and (B6)_8 are empty**. This would settle the pairs (11, 2) and (14, 10) (Corollary 120's
count, [a83.md](a83.md)). At t = 7 the analogous step, (B6) and (B5), took 68 + 420 classes of A_u
and about 90,000 split instances. At t = 8 the instances are larger, and nothing yet predicts
whether the solver will decide them.

## Lemma 101 (the pair orbits of (B7)_8 and (B6)_8)

Setting: t = 8, X = F_2^8, u of rank 3, x0 = sigma(u) = e_0 outside P, P = span(e_1, ..., e_p),
X_1 = ker A_u of dimension 5 with e_0 in X_1, R = Im A_u of dimension 3. The normal form of Lemma 67
([a14.md](a14.md)) is kept by G = {g in GL(X) : g e_0 = e_0, g P = P}, of order |GL_7(F_2)| for
p = 7 and 2^7 |GL_6(F_2)| for p = 6. L = span(e_0), H0 = L + P, pi : H0 -> P the projection along
L. For p = 6, X = H0 + span(e_7), and g in G sends e_7 to e_7 + h with h in H0.

**Lemma 101.** The G-orbits of pairs (X_1, R) number 13 for p = 7 ((B7)_8) and 51 for p = 6
((B6)_8). For p = 6, 20 of them have X_1 inside H0 (651 such X_1) and 31 do not (11160 such X_1).

*Proof.* Steps 1 and 2 of Lemma 83 ([a27.md](a27.md)) use nothing about t: for U a subspace of P of
dimension u and R of dimension k inside H0, the orbits of the stabiliser of U are

    kind P      one for each value of dim(U n R),  from max(0, u + k - p) to min(u, k)
    kind L      one for each value of dim(U n R'), from max(0, u + k - 1 - p) to min(u, k - 1)
    kind graph  for each j from max(0, u + k - p) to min(u, k): 1 orbit if j = 0 or j = k, else 2

*p = 7.* H0 = X and X_1 = L + Y with Y = X_1 n P of dimension 4; GL(P) is transitive on such Y, so
there is one orbit of X_1, of [7 choose 4]_2 = 11811. With u = 4, k = 3, p = 7: kind P 4 (0 to 3),
kind L 3 (0 to 2), kind graph 1 + 2 + 2 + 1 = 6 (j = 0 to 3). 13 in all.

*p = 6, the orbits of X_1.* As in Step 4 of Lemma 83: (alpha) X_1 = L + Y inside H0 with dim Y = 4,
one orbit of [6 choose 4]_2 = 651, stabiliser g_P keeps Y and h in H0 arbitrary; (beta) X_1 not
inside H0, X_1 n H0 = L + Y with dim Y = 3, X_1 = L + Y + span(e_7 + h), one orbit of 11811 - 651 =
11160 (the element with g_P = I and g e_7 = e_7 + h maps L + Y + span(e_7) to X_1), stabiliser g_P
keeps Y and h in L + Y.

*p = 6, the orbits of R.* As in Step 5 of Lemma 83: if R lies in H0, Step 2 counts its orbits with
U = Y. If not, R0 = R n H0 is a plane and R = R0 + span(e_7 + h0). In (alpha) the orbit of R is
fixed by that of R0. In (beta) it is fixed by the orbit of R0 and by whether the class of h0 in
P / (Y + pi(R0)) is 0; both values occur, and since dim(Y + pi(R0)) <= 5 < 6 the elements g_P that
fix Y + pi(R0) pointwise act transitively on the non-zero classes. So

    (alpha), u = 4:  R in H0 (k = 3): 3 (kind P: 1 to 3) + 3 (kind L: 0 to 2) + 5 (graph: 2 + 2 + 1) = 11
                     R0 (k = 2):      3 (kind P: 0 to 2) + 2 (kind L: 0 to 1) + 4 (graph: 1 + 2 + 1) = 9
    (beta),  u = 3:  R in H0 (k = 3): 4 (kind P: 0 to 3) + 3 (kind L: 0 to 2) + 6 (graph: 1 + 2 + 2 + 1) = 13
                     R0 (k = 2):      3 (kind P: 0 to 2) + 2 (kind L: 0 to 1) + 4 (graph: 1 + 2 + 1),
                                      each orbit of R0 twice                                = 18

So (alpha) has 20 orbits, (beta) 31, and p = 6 has 51. QED

The same formula, written as a function (m99.hand_pairs), gives 3 at (t, p) = (2, 1), 7 and 19 at
(4, 3) and (4, 2), 11 and 39 at (6, 5) and (6, 4), and 13 and 49 at (7, 6) and (7, 5). These are the
seven counts that jobs m27, m30 and m42 found by machine, and m99's unit tests check the formula
against them. Lemma 101 counts pairs only. The classes of A_u inside each pair (168 matrices with
the given kernel and image, up to the stabiliser of the pair) are not counted by hand. Job m99
computes them by machine, as m42 did at t = 7.
