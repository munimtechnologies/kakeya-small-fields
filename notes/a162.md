## Where this sits

The notation is that of a159 to a161. X is uniform on F_q^k, Y_t = P(X) + tX, and

    Lambda(P) = sum_t H(X | Y_t) = q^(-k) sum_t sum_{fibres F of Y_t} |F| log2 |F|    (bits).

The block conjecture C_q(k) says Lambda(P) <= k(q - 1). By Theorem 2, C_q(k) for every k is
equivalent to beta_q = q 2^(-(q-1)/q) being the exact Kakeya growth constant of F_q. That is
1.890 for q = 3, against the best known lower bound of 1.800 (Bukh-Chao, arXiv:2108.00074).
Corollary 126 (a161) reduced the chain route to a structural question: what do maps with
Lambda(P) = 2(q - 1), or close to it, look like? This note answers it in the two cases where an
answer can be checked completely, and states the conjecture that the rest of the route needs.

## Lemma 112 (pointwise form of the loss)

For x in F_q^k and t in F_q, let z_t(x) be the size of the fibre of Y_t through x, and let
phi(x) = sum_t log2 z_t(x). Then

    Lambda(P) = q^(-k) sum_x phi(x),      sum_t (z_t(x) - 1) = N(x) := #{u != 0 : P(x + u) - P(x) in F_q u}.

*Proof.* The first identity regroups sum_F |F| log2 |F| by points. For the second, x and x + u
(u != 0) collide at t exactly when P(x + u) - P(x) = -t u. That happens for at most one t, and
for some t exactly when P(x + u) - P(x) lies in F_q u. QED

**Corollary 128 (C_q(1) and its equality case).** For k = 1, every u is parallel to every vector,
so N(x) = q - 1. Since log2 z <= z - 1, with equality exactly for z in {1, 2}, we get
phi(x) <= q - 1 at every point. So Lambda(P) <= q - 1, with equality iff no Y_t has a fibre of
size 3 or more.

## Proposition 42 (one-dimensional maximisers are ovals)

For f : F_q -> F_q these are equivalent:

(a) Lambda(f) = q - 1;
(b) no three points of the graph {(x, f(x))} are collinear;
(c) the graph together with the vertical point at infinity (0:1:0) is an oval (a (q+1)-arc) of
    PG(2, q). The line at infinity is then its tangent at (0:1:0).

For odd q, the maximisers are therefore exactly f(x) = a x^2 + b x + c with a != 0, which is
q(q - 1) maps with f(0) = 0. For even q, they are the ovals tangent to the line at infinity at
(0:1:0), whose nucleus lies on the line at infinity. Equivalently, they are the hyperovals that
meet the line at infinity in two points, one of which is removed. They are as varied as the
hyperovals of PG(2, q), whose classification is open.

*Proof.* (a) <=> (b): a fibre of f(x) + tx with three points is three graph points on the line
y = -tx + c. Every non-vertical line has this form. (b) <=> (c): vertical lines contain exactly
one graph point each, and the line at infinity contains no graph point, so adding (0:1:0)
creates no collinear triple. For odd q, Segre's theorem (every oval is a conic) and the tangency
condition at (0:1:0) leave the parabolas y = a x^2 + b x + c with a != 0. In homogeneous form the
conic is A x^2 + D xz + E yz + F z^2 = 0 with A E != 0. For even q, all tangents of an oval meet
at its nucleus. QED

This is a reformulation of classical finite geometry. It is recorded because it is the equality
case of Theorem 1. The tests confirm (a) <=> (b) by enumerating every f with f(0) = 0 for
q = 2, 3, 5, 7, and they confirm the quadratic list for q = 3, 5, 7.

## Proposition 43 (rigidity at (3, 2): every maximiser is a frame product)

Call P a **frame product** if there is a basis (e1, e2) of F_q^2 with dual basis (l1, l2) and
1D maximisers f, g with

    P(x) = f(l1(x)) e1 + g(l2(x)) e2 + const.

Equivalently, P is a GL_2-conjugate A (f x g) A^(-1) of a product. Every frame product has
Lambda = Lambda(f) + Lambda(g) = 2(q - 1). Conjugation maps Y_t to A Y_t, and the two coordinates
of a product are independent.

**Proposition 43.** At (q, k) = (3, 2) the maximisers with P(0) = 0 are exactly the 216 frame
products. As a result, every maximiser has a zero-slack frame in the sense of Corollary 126,
which proves the pre-registered **G1 (a161)**.

*Proof.* Over F_3, every non-affine f with f(0) = 0 has the form a y^2 + b y with a != 0, which
gives 6 such f. Proposition 42 shows these are the 1D maximisers. The unordered pairs of
distinct lines through 0 in F_3^2 number C(4, 2) = 6. So there are at most 6 * 6 * 6 = 216
frame products with P(0) = 0, and they are pairwise distinct for the following reason. In odd
characteristic, the quadratic part of P is a l1^2 e1 + c l2^2 e2 with a c != 0. Composing it
with functionals gives the span of l1^2 and l2^2. The rank-one forms in that span are exactly
the multiples of l1^2 and of l2^2, and these recover {l1, l2} up to scalars, and with it the
frame. The tests confirm that there are 216 distinct maps and that each one attains
Lambda = 4. The exhaustive branch-and-bound search of this repository (README section 9,
`exact_h2.py`, with P(0) = 0) found exactly 216 maps at the maximum S = 36, i.e. Lambda = 4. So
the two sets are equal. For a frame product, take l = l1 and v = e1. Then
l(Y_t) = f(l1(X)) + t l1(X) is a function of l(X), so every term in Lemma 111 is zero. QED

The proof relies on the published census count. The new census in job kakeya-lemmal-v1
(written independently, in a159) recounts the maximisers at (3, 2). The tests also confirm that
the triangular map (x1^2 + x2, x2^2), which respects a line but is not a frame product, falls
strictly short. By hand: at t = 0 its first coordinate still carries information about x2.

## Conjecture R_q(2) (rigidity), and why the route needs it

> **R_q(2), q >= 3.** If Lambda(P) = 2(q - 1), then P is a frame product.

(For q = 2 it fails as stated: every map of F_2 is a 1D maximiser, affine maps included, so
the equality case is degenerate.) R_q(2) predicts the following:

- For odd q, every maximiser is a quadratic map. With P(0) = 0 there are exactly
  C(q + 1, 2) (q (q - 1))^2 of them: 216 for q = 3, 6,000 for q = 5 and 49,392 for q = 7. The
  tests build each of these sets and confirm the counts. For q = 3 and 5 they also confirm that
  every member is a maximiser; for q = 7 they spot-check 1 map in 997.
- For even q, the maximisers are frame products of oval functions, e.g. f(x) = x^2 or x^4 on F_16.

R is not C. It describes who attains the bound, and it is only meaningful if C holds. Its value
for the route is this: by Lemma 111 and Corollary 126, a map near the maximum passes the chain
bound only if it has a frame of nearly zero slack, and frame products have exactly zero slack.
A **stability** version of R ("Lambda >= 2(q - 1) - delta forces P to be close to a frame
product") is the form the chain route can use. R itself is the first statement in that
direction that can be tested. At (3, 2), Proposition 43 proves it.

### Lemma 113 (quadratic maps, odd q: fibres through a point)

Let q be odd and P(x) = B(x, x) + Lx + c with B symmetric bilinear. Put
M_x = -(2B(x, .) + L) and S(M) = {u : B(u, u) = M u}. Then the fibre of Y_t through x is
x + S(M_x - t). For a direction w != 0 and c != 0, cw lies in S(M) iff c B(w, w) = M w, so:

- if w and B(w, w) are independent, the line F_q w adds exactly one point to exactly one
  S(M - s) when w is not an eigenvector of M, and nothing otherwise;
- if B(w, w) = lambda w with lambda != 0, it adds one point to S(M - s) for every s other than
  the eigenvalue, provided w is an eigenvector of M, and nothing otherwise;
- if B(w, w) = 0, it adds q - 1 points to S(M - mu) when w is an eigenvector of M with
  eigenvalue mu, and nothing otherwise.

(Each case is a two-line computation in the basis (w, B(w, w)).) For the square map
(x1^2, x2^2) at a point with x1 != x2, both eigen-directions of M_x are of the second kind.
That gives N(x) = 2(q - 1) + (q - 2) = 3q - 4, and phi(x) = 2(q - 1) at every point.

**Proposition 44.** A quadratic map (odd q) is a frame product iff, for some fixed basis, every
M_x is diagonal up to a scalar and the diagonal quadratic coefficients are nonzero. *Proof.* In
the coordinates of that basis, the off-diagonal entries of the Jacobian, d P1/d y2 = b y1 +
2c y2 + e and d P2/d y1, vanish identically. So P1 depends only on y1 and P2 only on y2. QED

*Heuristic (not a proof).* By Lemma 113, phi(x) can approach 2(q - 1) only at points where both
eigen-directions of M_x are of the second kind, and there are at most three such directions.
For any fixed pair, the set of x with M_x diagonal in that pair is a line or the whole plane.
So unless it is the whole plane (Proposition 44: a frame product), only O(q) of the q^2 points
are high, and the average falls below 2(q - 1) for large q. The census below tests this for
q = 3, 5 and 7.

## Pre-registered: job kakeya-quad-v1 (code in this commit; not yet run)

`quad_census.py` enumerates every quadratic map of F_q^2 for q = 3, 5 and 7, with constants
dropped and normalised by P -> P + cI so that P1 has no x1 term. That is q^9 maps: 19,683,
1,953,125 and 40,353,607. It computes Lambda by a vectorised fibre count. Every map within
1e-6 of the threshold is re-checked exactly in integers. The frame products are rebuilt
independently with `oval_rigidity.py`. Predictions, written before any run:

- **Q1.** No quadratic map has Lambda > 2(q - 1) for q = 3, 5 or 7. This is C_q(2) for all
  quadratic blocks. q = 5 and q = 7 have never been searched; a failure would be a new record
  upper bound for Kakeya sets over F_5 or F_7.
- **Q2.** The quadratic maps with Lambda = 2(q - 1) are exactly the frame products: 72, 1,200 and
  7,056 maps under this normalisation, which are the counts above divided by q. This is R_q(2)
  within quadratic maps. q = 3 is a control already implied by Proposition 43.
- Reported without a prediction: the second-largest Lambda among quadratic maps, i.e. the gap
  a stability version of R would have to explain.

## What the "wow" would be

A proof of C_q(2) for every q would show that blocks of size two never beat products, and an
induction on k along Corollary 126 would give the exact Kakeya constant of F_q. That would close
the gap between 1.800 and 1.890 at q = 3. A map with Lambda > 2(q - 1) would instead give a new
record Kakeya upper bound. This note adds three things. The equality cases are known completely
in dimension one (ovals) and at (3, 2) (frame products). The rigidity conjecture R that the
chain route needs is stated. Its first independent test, all quadratic maps at q = 5 and 7, is
pre-registered.
