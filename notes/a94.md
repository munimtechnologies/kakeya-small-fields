## Lemma 106 (the split of the rank-0 case by T(N))

**Lemma 106.** Let u be in (B7)_8, in the rank-0 case, in m42's normal form, and let
n -> T_n be the map of Lemma 104 (a) from N to End(Q).

(a) T(N) is a space of pairwise commuting matrices that contains I, of dimension 1, 2 or 3. If its
    dimension is 3, it is a commutative subalgebra of End(Q).

(b) The basis v_1, v_2, v_3 of N modulo u can be chosen, with A_(v_1) = 0 as in Corollary 123, in
    one of these forms, where M, M_1, M_2 are any fixed matrices that span T(N) together with I:

        t1   dim T(N) = 1:   T_(v_1) = T_(v_2) = T_(v_3) = 0
        t2   dim T(N) = 2:   T_(v_1) = T_(v_2) = 0,  T_(v_3) = M
        t3   dim T(N) = 3:   T_(v_1) = 0,  T_(v_2) = M_1,  T_(v_3) = M_2

    In each case every A_n with n in N is fixed (A_n = A_u-bar T_n pi), and Lemma 105 applies to
    every w != 0 in N_0 = span of the v_i with T_(v_i) = 0.

(c) Let g in GL(X) satisfy g e_0 = e_0, g P = P, g X_1 = X_1 and g A_u = A_u g, and let g_Q be the
    map it induces on Q. Then the system conjugated by g, with the complement of N chosen again so
    that phi keeps its normal form, is a system of the same class, in normal form and in the
    rank-0 case, with T(N) replaced by g_Q T(N) g_Q^(-1).

So for each class of A_u it is enough to take t1, and t2 and t3 once for each orbit of the group
Kbar = {g_Q} on the spaces of each dimension.

*Proof.* (a) T_u = I, and Lemma 104 (b), (c) give commuting and dimension at most 3. If the
dimension is 3, the algebra generated by T(N) is commutative, so it has dimension at most 3 by
Schur's bound, and it contains T(N). So it equals T(N).

(b) n -> T_n is linear from N onto T(N), with kernel N_0 of dimension 4 - dim T(N). Choose a basis
of N_0 that starts with the element v_1 of Corollary 123 (any w != 0 in N_0 will do), and choose
the remaining v_i as preimages of the given matrices. Their images (I for u, and the given
matrices) are independent, and N_0 is the kernel, so u, v_1, v_2, v_3 is a basis of N. As in the
proof of Corollary 123, this keeps A_u, sigma(u) and the values of phi on the basis, so the normal
form and m42's hints are unchanged. The conditions of m100 and m101 hold for every element of N and
every w in N_0.

(c) Put A'_h = g A_h g^(-1), sigma' = g sigma and beta' = g beta on the same V. These satisfy (*)
and are anisotropic. A'_u = A_u, sigma'(u) = e_0, phi' = g phi has image g P = P, and ker A'_u = X_1,
so ker phi' = N and the system is still in the rank-0 case because g X_1 = X_1. For each j choose
h_j in V with phi'(h_j) = e_j. The h_j are independent modulo N, so u, v_1, v_2, v_3, h_1, ..., h_7
is a basis in m42's normal form. From g A_u = A_u g and A_u = A_u-bar pi we get
g A_u-bar = A_u-bar g_Q, and pi g^(-1) = g_Q^(-1) pi. So
A'_n = g A_u-bar T_n pi g^(-1) = A_u-bar (g_Q T_n g_Q^(-1)) pi. QED

Kbar is found by machine in job m102. For each of the 168 g_Q in GL(Q), the conditions on g are
linear in its 64 entries (g e_0 = e_0, g P in P, g X_1 in X_1, pi g = g_Q pi, g A_u = A_u g). The
job looks for an invertible g in the solution space and checks every g it finds again, directly. A
g_Q for which no g is found is left out, which can only make more instances. By hand, for o000_c000
(R = span(e_0, e_1, e_2) inside X_1): g must fix e_0 and keep R n P = span(e_1, e_2), so Kbar is

## Lemma 107 (the commuting spaces of M_3(F_2))

**Lemma 107.** (a) M_3(F_2) has 255 spaces span(I, M) of dimension 2. Under conjugation by
GL_3(F_2) they form 6 orbits, of sizes 21, 24, 28, 42, 56 and 84.

(b) It has 183 three-dimensional commutative subalgebras (equivalently, by Lemma 106 (a),
three-dimensional spaces of commuting matrices that contain I). They form 7 orbits: F_8 (8),
F_2 x F_4 (28), F_2^3 (28), F_2 x F_2[x]/(x^2) (84), F_2[x]/(x^3) (21), and two types with radical
of square 0 (7 and 7).

*Proof.* (a) A space span(I, M) is the pair {M, M + I} with M not scalar: (512 - 2) / 2 = 255.
g M g^(-1) = M + I is impossible: the characteristic polynomial p would satisfy p(x + 1) = p(x),
but for a monic cubic the coefficients of x^2 in p(x + 1) and p(x) differ by 3 = 1. So the
stabiliser of span(I, M) is the centraliser of M, and the orbit has the size of the similarity
class of M. The 12 non-scalar classes (14 in all, q^3 + q^2 + q for q = 2) have sizes 24 and 24
(irreducible characteristic polynomial, centraliser F_8^*), 56 and 56 (x(x^2 + x + 1) and
(x + 1)(x^2 + x + 1), centraliser of order 3), 42 and 42 (x^3 and (x + 1)^3 with one Jordan
block, centraliser of order 4), 84 and 84 (x^2(x + 1) and x(x + 1)^2, cyclic, centraliser of
order 2), 21 and 21 (the nilpotents of rank 1, 7 lines times 3 functionals that vanish on them, and
I plus them), and 28 and 28 (the projections of rank 1 and 2, 7 lines times 4 complementary
planes). These add up to 510, and M -> M + I swaps the two classes of each pair.

(b) A three-dimensional commutative subalgebra A is a maximal one (Schur's bound), and it is a
product of local algebras acting on the summands of a decomposition of F_2^3. Counting each type
by its decomposition and its local factors gives: F_8, 48 matrices with irreducible
characteristic polynomial, 6 in each copy: 8. F_2 x F_4: a line and a complementary plane, with
the unique F_4 in M_2(F_2): 7 x 4 = 28. F_2^3: unordered triples of independent lines,
168 / 6 = 28. F_2 x F_2[x]/(x^2): a line, a complementary plane, and one of 3 non-zero
nilpotents on the plane: 84. F_2[x]/(x^3): 42 nilpotents of rank 2, two in each algebra
(N and N + N^2): 21. Radical of square 0, local: the radical is a 2-dimensional space of nilpotents
of rank 1 with all products 0, which either share an image line (7) or share a kernel plane (7).
Sum: 183. QED

Lemma 107 is not used for soundness: job m102 lists the spaces by brute force over all pairs of
matrices, and checks these counts against them.
