## Lemma 96 (the tuple of matrices can be fixed)

Setting of Lemma 95 ([a85.md](a85.md)): t = 8, dim V = 11, u of rank 4 in Case B, shape (c), in
the normal form of Lemma 37 with sigma(v_i) = e_i (i = 1, 2, 3), X_1 = span(e_0, ..., e_3) and the
matrices M_i = M_(v_i) of Lemma 38 on X/X_1 in the basis [e_4], ..., [e_7].

**Lemma 96.** Let W_0 be a space in the orbit of W = span(M_1, M_2, M_3) under conjugation, and
(B_1, B_2, B_3) any basis of W_0. Then the system has a normal form of the same kind for the same u
in which M_i = B_i for i = 1, 2, 3, and A_u has the same Jordan type.

*Proof.* Lemma 39 ([shape-c.md](shape-c.md)) with k = 4 and t - k = 4 gives, for every g in
GL_4(F_2) and c in GL_3(F_2), a normal form of the same kind for u with matrices
M'_a = g^(-1) (sum over b of c_ab M_b) g. Pick g with g^(-1) W g = W_0. Then (g^(-1) M_b g) is a
basis of W_0, and as GL_3(F_2) acts transitively on the ordered bases of a 3-dimensional space,
some c turns it into (B_1, B_2, B_3); conjugation by g is linear, so M'_a = B_a. The new normal
form comes from a change of basis of X, which conjugates A_u, so its Jordan type is unchanged.
QED

What it uses: Lemma 39 and Lemma 95(a) (so Corollary 121, Lemmas 38 and 94 and, through them,
Theorem 31). It does not use Theorem 33. With Lemma 96 a search for (type, orbit) may fix all 48
entries of the three matrices; what stays free is A_u within its type, and the rest of the system.

## Job m94: the pilot

Job m94 (cube_groups/m94.py, to be committed with its results; its predictions are committed now)
is job m44's shape (c) search moved to (t, k) = (8, 4), dim V = 11,
for one (type, orbit): (N4), with (M_1, M_2, M_3) = (E_00, E_12, E_11 + E_22), the basis m93

dimension 3), the largest orbit that meets (N4). The CNF is case_b_shapes.encode_shape(8, 4, 'c')
with

    (N4)  rows 4 to 7 of A_u e_c are 0 for c >= 4 (16 unit clauses), and T = A_u on
          [e_4], ..., [e_7] one-to-one (15 clauses); the 16 entries of T stay free;
    (S)   A_(v_i) e_(4+j) = A_u (sum over l of M_i[l][j] e_(4+l)), linear once the M_i are fixed
          (96 conditions);
    (a)   rows 4 to 7 of beta(v_i, v_(3+j)) are 0 for j <= 3 and column j - 4 of M_i for j >= 4
          (84 unit clauses).

By Lemma 96, a system of type (N4) whose W lies in this orbit gives a model of this CNF; an UNSAT
answer would close (N4) for this one orbit. Controls: case_b's (2, 1) example SAT, and the same
encoder at (6, 3) UNSAT (A(6) = 6); the unit tests check the new clauses on hand-made data. The
predictions and the decision rule were written before the job file: the pilot is guessed UNSAT
within its 75-minute cap at about 35%. If it closes within 10 minutes, the other 22 orbits of (N4)
follow in one job; if it is UNKNOWN, hand work on Lemma 94(c) comes first.

## Limits

- A VERIFIED certificate covers one CNF only. The encoding (m57.py on sat_aniso.py), the hints,
  Lemma 93's unit clauses, the orbit representatives of Propositions 30 to 34 and the reductions
  are code and hand arguments that no human has checked.
- Theorem 33 stays computer-assisted. It rests on many thousands of solver answers, of which 2226
  now carry checked certificates.
- Lemmas 95 and 96 are proofs by hand with no computer check of their own; job m93's list of orbits
  is a computation with independent checks. None of this proves anything about A(8) yet; job m94
  is a measurement, and one UNSAT answer would close one of 117 (type, orbit) pairs.
- No human mathematician has checked Theorem 33 or any lemma it uses. Until someone does, read it
  as a claim with its evidence, not as an established result.

## Prior work

As for the earlier notes, searches on 2026-10-15 to 2026-10-23 found nothing on A(t), M(t) or
Question R other than MV2's own posts. CaDiCaL (A. Biere et al.) is a standard SAT solver; DRAT
proofs and the drat-trim checker are due to M. Heule, W. Hunt and N. Wetzler. The count of commuting
pairs is W. Feit and N. J. Fine (1960); commuting tuples over a finite field up to simultaneous
similarity are counted in arXiv 1409.2698, which counts ordered tuples, not subspaces; commutative
matrix algebras are classical (D. A. Suprunenko and R. I. Tyshkevich, "Commutative matrices",
1968); spaces of singular matrices and compression spaces go back to J. Dieudonne (1949) and
M. D. Atkinson and S. Lloyd (1980), and over F_2 to C. de Seguins Pazzis (arXiv 1405.1575). No
published list of the 79 orbits was found. Please open an issue at
https://github.com/munimtechnologies/kakeya-small-fields if you find an error.
