## Corollary 52 (no Case A at rank 4 for t = 7)

**Corollary 52.** In an anisotropic system with t = 7 and dim V = 9, no element of rank 4 is in
Case A. So every element of rank 4 is in Case B, in shape (c) or (d2) (Lemma 42 rules out (d1)).

*Proof.* Let u have rank 4. By Proposition 29(b), k = 3 and u is in Case A or in Case B. In Case A,
Lemma 77 (a), (c) and the first sentence of (d) give x0 outside R, D = X_1 n R a plane and
X_1 = span(x0) + D. Lemma 80 then gives bases in which x0 = e_0, D = span(e_1, e_2),
R = span(e_1, ..., e_4), beta(u, .) takes the values of the normal form, and A_u is the
representative of one of six classes. So the plain CNF of that class (a25.md, "Job m39") is
satisfiable. Job m39 found all six unsatisfiable. QED

What it rests on: Proposition 29(b); Lemma 77 (a), (c), (d) and the lemmas they use (Lemmas 33, 51,
62 and Proposition 26, see a24.md); Lemma 80 (by hand, and checked by the orbit closure of the
selfcheck); and the six plain solver answers of m39a and m39b, saved without proof certificates. The
controls checked the encoder (the t = 5 CNFs are exactly those of case_a.py) and that a known system
is found (the planted system at t = 6). The bounds are those of the Setting of a24.md.

At t = 5 the same row was closed by hand (Lemma 52). At t = 6 it was closed by 21 solver answers
(Corollary 42, job m24). At t = 7 it took 6 answers, because Lemma 77 already fixes most of A_u.

## Lemma 81 (shape (c) at rank 4: the plane in ker A_u n Im A_u)

Setting as in [case-b.md](case-b.md) and case-b-shapes.md, at (t, k) = (7, 3): u of rank 4 in
Case B, P = beta(u, V) a hyperplane missing x0, N = U+ = ker phi of dimension 3, X_1 = ker A_u,
R = Im A_u, Y = P n X_1. In the normal form of Lemma 37 for shape (c), x0 = e_0,
P = span(e_1, ..., e_6), sigma(v_1) = e_1 and sigma(v_2) = e_2 span Y, X_1 = span(e_0, e_1, e_2),
and phi(v_(2+j)) = e_j for 1 <= j <= 6.

**Lemma 81.** Let u be in shape (c). Then the normal form of Lemma 37 can be chosen so that, in
addition, (A_u e_3, A_u e_4) is one of

    cY   (e_1, e_2)          Q = Y                       (1 plane)
    cX   (e_0, e_1)          Q = span(e_0, e_1)          (3 planes through e_0)
    cG   (e_0 + e_1, e_2)    Q = span(e_0 + e_1, e_2)    (3 planes, neither Y nor through e_0)

where Q is a plane of X_1 n R.

*Proof.* By Proposition 27(c4) (Corollary 51), d = dim(X_1 n R) >= 2, so X_1 n R contains a plane
Q. Replacing (v_1, v_2) by (v_1, v_2) g for g in GL_2(F_2) keeps every value of the form of shape
(c): beta = 0 on N, so sigma is linear on N and the new values sigma(v_i) are again a basis of Y,
which we take as the new e_1, e_2. The new v_3, v_4 are chosen over them. This acts on X_1 by
fixing e_0 and acting on Y by g. The 7 planes of X_1 fall into 3 orbits: Y; the 3 planes
span(e_0, y), y in Y, y != 0 (GL_2 is transitive on y); and the 3 graphs {y + f(y) e_0 : y in Y} of a
non-zero linear form f on Y (GL_2 is transitive on f). So we may take Q to be the representative of
its orbit, with the basis q_1, q_2 given in the table. A_u x0 = 0 and X = P + span(x0), so
A_u P = R contains q_1, q_2, and there are p_1, p_2 in P with A_u p_i = q_i. If
c_1 p_1 + c_2 p_2 lies in Y, applying A_u gives c_1 q_1 + c_2 q_2 = 0, so c_1 = c_2 = 0. So e_1, e_2,
p_1, p_2 are independent in P, and Lemma 35 lets us take e_3 = p_1, e_4 = p_2 and extend to a basis
of P. QED

If d = 3, Q can be any of the three representatives, so the classes overlap. They still cover every
case, which is all the search needs.

## Lemma 82 ((A5) at rank 3: one class of A_u)

Setting as in a24.md, Lemma 79: the system has no element of rank 4, u has rank 3 and is in (A5),
so x0 lies in P, dim P = 6 and ker phi has dimension 3. By Lemma 79 (a), R lies in X_1, x0 is not
in R, and X_1 = R + span(x0) lies in P.

**Lemma 82.** There are bases e_0, ..., e_6 of X and v_0, ..., v_8 of V with x0 = e_0,
R = span(e_1, e_2, e_3), P = span(e_0, ..., e_5), v_0 = u, phi(v_1) = phi(v_2) = 0,
phi(v_(3+j)) = e_j for 0 <= j <= 5, and

    A_u e_0 = ... = A_u e_3 = 0,   A_u e_4 = e_1,   A_u e_5 = e_2,   A_u e_6 = e_3.

*Proof.* Take e_1, e_2, e_3 a basis of R, e_4, e_5 completing e_0, ..., e_3 (a basis of X_1) to a
basis of P, and e_6 completing it to a basis of X. As in Lemma 80 (a), ker phi = span(u, v_1, v_2)
and any v_(3+j) over e_j give a basis of V, and a base change g of X keeps this form exactly when
g e_0 = e_0, g R = R and g P = P; then a basis of V can be chosen again. A_u kills X_1 and maps
X onto R, so it is i L pi with pi : X -> W = X / X_1 and L : W -> R an isomorphism: 168
candidates. An element g of G turns L into a' L b^(-1), where a' is the map g induces on R and b
the one it induces on W. Take g equal to a' on R and the identity on e_0, e_4, e_5, e_6: it lies in
G, and b = I. As a' runs over GL(R), a' L runs over all isomorphisms W -> R. So all 168 candidates
form one orbit, and A_u can be taken to be the one above. QED

The selfcheck of m41.py recomputes the orbit by closure under 22 transvections that generate G.

Compare (A4) at t = 6, where Lemma 70 left 141 classes of A_u. Here Lemma 79 (a) puts R inside X_1
and X_1 inside P, and that leaves no choice.
