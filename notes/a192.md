## 1. Block length 3: no quadratic map beats KLSS (h3scan-v1)

Notation as in a164 and README Theorem 2: for P: F_3^3 -> F_3^3 put
S(P) = sum_t sum_(fibres F of x -> P(x) + t x) |F| log2 |F|. The smallest Kakeya sets in F_3^n
grow with base at most 3 * 2^(-S(P)/243), and S = 162 is the KLSS base (27/4)^(1/3) = 1.889882.

**Proposition 86 (exhaustive computation).** For every quadratic map P: F_3^3 -> F_3^3,
S(P) <= 162. Equality holds for exactly 50,544 such maps with P(0) = 0 (1,364,688 with
constants), and only when the linear part of P is diagonalisable over F_3. If S(P) < 162, then
S(P) <= 150.793987519.

*Proof (computer-verified).* S is unchanged by P -> A P(A^-1 x) (A in GL_3(F_3)), by
P -> P + lam x and by adding constants. So it suffices to take L = DP(0) from one representative
of each of the 15 classes of 3x3 matrices under L -> A L A^-1 + lam I (class sizes add up to
3^9) and all 3^18 quadratic parts Q. The C scanner computed S for all 15 * 3^18 maps. Every map
within 1e-7 of 162 was classified exactly through the prime factorisation of prod |F|^|F|,
compared with 2^162. The top 8 values of every class were re-scored in Python with integers,
and C and Python agree on all of them. Equality maps occur in exactly the four diagonalisable
classes (scalar; eigenvalues (1,0,0); (2,0,0); (0,1,2)), with 1,872, 48, 48 and 8 maps per
representative. Weighting by class sizes 3, 351, 351 and 1,404 gives 50,544. No class has a
value in (150.7940, 162). []

Prop 86 closes the case q = 3 that a164 left open for degree 2: C_3(3) holds for all quadratic
maps. The same job also found that no S_3-equivariant map of any degree (3^18 maps, second
best 157.832) and no cyclic map of degree <= 3 (3^16 maps, second best 143.651) beats 162.
Together with the October annealing over all maps and the sd3 search (no counterexample to R3
in about 370,000 maps), the evidence now points one way. Over F_3, maps of this kind do not
beat KLSS. Beating the 1.889882 bound needs either a non-quadratic map without these
symmetries at length 3 or a longer block.

Open question raised by Prop 86: is there a conceptual proof that S(P) = 162 forces DP(0)
to be diagonalisable over F_3? A nilpotent part seems to merge fibres across slices. We have
no proof.

## 2. Erdos #1097: the record law is stable under two-dimensional correlation (sdz-search-v1)

The Katz-Tao sum-difference constant C_3b (Erdos problem #1097) has lower bound 1.7789888414
(Mosaic Intelligence 2026) and upper bound 1.8263484 (Farnamfar, Nov 2026). Every known
lower-bound law is one-dimensional. The pre-registered question asked whether a law on Z^2
near the product p x p of two optimal staircase laws can do better.

**Observation 86.1 (numerical).** On the optimal staircase laws of 7, 9, 11 and 13 points,
the second-order coefficient is kappa = 0.90641 < 1 (power iteration, two seeds agreeing to
1e-15). So p x p is a strict second-order local maximum against correlated perturbations.
Direct optimisation from random starts on S x S found non-local laws that beat every 1-D
staircase law (best 1.7789888274, a gain of 1.29e-7). These laws are still 1.4e-8 below the
record, and annealing over supports in Z^2 reached only 1.7789535.

Grade: NO_GAIN (pre-registered). The weak prediction "STABLE" held for kappa on all four
staircases. As a formal verdict it held only for 7 points; 9, 11 and 13 points are UNCLEAR
because of the non-local gains. We close the two-dimensional line for Erdos #1097. Another
copy of the record law does not help to second order, and the remaining slack lies in the
eighth decimal.
