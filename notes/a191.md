## The question

Note a180 (Theorem 68) proved that every Kakeya set in F_3^n has at least (3^n - 1)^(6/11) points,
base 3^(6/11) = 1.82074, using only the Katz-Tao sum-difference inequality. A Kakeya pair over F_3
has two properties that proof ignores: every relation is F_3-linear (3-torsion), and the direction
D is uniform on the whole group, so every group-valued variable has entropy at most log|G|
(the "Kakeya restriction"). The pre-registered question: can Shannon inequalities, copy-lemma
gluing, F_3-linear closures and the Kakeya restriction, in configurations of at most 14 atoms,
certify an average-entropy constant above 6/11? Any checked certificate would be a new record
lower bound for Kakeya sets over F_3.

kakeya-lp-v1 ran on 2026-11-19 from 20:22 to 22:56 UTC (1 CPU, 2 GB, peak 0.36 GB), 9,077 s of
solving. Unit tests passed. 4,524 LPs were solved, none skipped, none invalid.

| family | what it is | LPs |

| kt-* and n-* | the Katz-Tao word with extra atoms (with and without the Kakeya restriction), and its one-change neighbourhood | 189 |
| r<n>, r<n>-u | random chains of 3-4 page copies with random glues (seed 20261118); -u adds cross atoms and the Kakeya restriction | 4,335 |

2,200 of the 4,524 LPs included the Kakeya restriction rows.

## Grading against the pre-registration

- **H0 (self-test): PASS.** The Katz-Tao word reproduced 6/11 and the independent Fraction-only
  checker accepted the exact certificate (`cert_kt-base.json`, `kt_base_checked = "6/11"`).
- **H1 (3-torsion alone beats 6/11; prior ~20%): NO.** No LP exceeded 6/11 + 1e-7.
- **H2 (the Kakeya restriction beats 6/11; prior ~15%): NO.** Among the 2,200 LPs with the
  restriction, the best value is again exactly 6/11 (best tag kt-cross1-u).
- No hit, so no certificate other than the self-test was extracted. Verdict: **NO GAIN at <= 14
  atoms.** The record base stays 3^(6/11) = 1.82074 (a180).

Every LP value falls on a short list. The fractions below are identified from the floating-point
values (agreement to 1e-15); only 6/11 has an exact checked certificate.

| 0.523810 | 11/21 | 5 |
| 0.500000 | 1/2 | 4,183 |

Of the 137 words reaching 6/11, 26 are Katz-Tao variants and 111 are random chains. 202 of the 204
values strictly between 1/2 and 6/11 have the form k/(2k-1) for k = 7, ..., 11; the Katz-Tao value
6/11 is the k = 6 member, and no word reached k = 5 (5/9). Since k/(2k-1) = 1/(2 - 1/k), these are
exactly the averages that a sum-difference exponent 2 - 1/k gives (Katz-Tao's 11/6 = 2 - 1/6;
Bourgain's 13/7 = 2 - 1/7 is the k = 7 line). This suggests that small chain words
reproduce a single family of "k-step" sum-difference bounds, of which Katz-Tao is the strongest
that fits in 14 atoms. We have not proved this.

## What it means

At this size, neither the F_3-linear structure nor the uniformity of the direction adds anything
to the Katz-Tao argument: every certificate that reaches 6/11 is, in value, the Katz-Tao bound. The
pre-registration warned that the 14-atom cap excludes the large stacked configurations behind the
November 2026 sum-difference records (Farnamfar, arXiv:2611.19594), so this null says nothing about
larger configurations. The companion job kakeya-sparse-v1 (pre-registration
`notes/prereg-kakeya-sparse.md`) tests exactly those larger configurations with the Kakeya
restriction and is still waiting for compute.

## Limits

Sampled search over a finite family of configurations, not a proof that no small certificate
exists. LP values are floating point (HiGHS); only hits would have been certified exactly. The work
was carried out with AI agents; no human mathematician has checked it.
