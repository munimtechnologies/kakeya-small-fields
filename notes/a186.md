## Summary

Let U*(n) be the least size of a sticky Kakeya set in F_3^n (a182: in some basis, coordinate j
of the base point P(d) of the line in direction d depends only on d_1, ..., d_j). The classical
set {0,1}^n ∪ {0,2}^n is sticky and has 2^(n+1) - 1 points. Until now the only lower bounds were
asymptotic: 1.889882^n (a182) and 1.90499^n - 1 (a183). At n = 4 these give 12 points.

1. **Theorem 74 (exact split).** Cut a sticky Kakeya set by the three planes y_1 = u. One plane
   holds a sticky Kakeya set of dimension n - 1. Each of the three planes holds one *slice* of
   each of two full subtrees. This gives an exact formula for |K|. Its minimum is attained when
   the two subtrees are copies of each other, so

   U*(n) = min over (K', T, h) of |K' ∪ S_h(T)| + sum_{t != h} |S_t(T)|,

   where K' is a sticky Kakeya set in F_3^(n-1), T is a full sticky tree of height n - 1 with
   slices S_0, S_1, S_2, and h is a slice index.
2. **Corollary 74.1.** U*(n) >= min over game vectors a in A_(n-1) of
   a_0 + a_1 + a_2 + max(0, U*(n-1) - max a).
3. **Theorem 75.** U*(1), U*(2), U*(3) = 3, 7, 15 = 2^(n+1) - 1, and 29 <= U*(4) <= 31.
   For n <= 3 the classical set is a smallest sticky Kakeya set.
4. **Corollary 75.1.** A Kakeya set in F_3^3 with at most 14 points, or one in F_3^4 with at
   most 28, is not sticky in any basis. The smallest Kakeya set in F_3^3 has 13 points, and
   F_3^4 has Kakeya sets with 27 (munimtechnologies/kakeya-conjecture, release v1.1.0). So **the
   smallest Kakeya sets in dimensions 3 and 4 are not sticky.** In dimension 2 they are: both
   minima are 7. Corollary 72.1 (a183) proved non-stickiness only for unspecified large n.

## 1. Full trees and slices

A *full tree* of height k is a sticky family P on all of F_3^k, including d = 0, given by node
maps g_a: F_3 -> F_3 for the prefixes a of length < k, with P(d)_j = g_{d_<j}(d_j). Its slices
are S_t = {P(d) + t d : d ∈ F_3^k}. Grouping by the first coordinate gives, exactly,

  S_t(T) = ⊔_v {v} × ∪_{x : g(x) + t x = v} S_t(T_x),                     (1)

where g is the root map and T_x is the subtree of directions with d_1 = x.

**Lemma 158 (a183, Lemma 149, for full trees).** |S_t(T)| >= L_t(T), where L(T) ∈ A_k is the
game vector of the same tree. *Proof.* Induction on (1): |∪| >= max. ∎

**Lemma 159 (relabelling slices).** For c ∈ F_3, the full trees P(d) + c d and P(-d) - c d have
slices S_(t+c)(T) and S_(c-t)(T). Every permutation of F_3 is t -> ±t + c, so the three slices
of a full tree can be permuted at will, as sets. Adding a constant w to P translates all three
slices by w. *Proof.* P(-d) - c d + t d = P(-d) + (c - t)(-d). ∎

Lines of a full tree in directions d != 0 lie in S_0 ∪ S_1 ∪ S_2, so the union of the three
slices contains a sticky Kakeya set.

## 2. Theorem 74

Let K be the union of the lines of a sticky family in F_3^n, with root map g and c0 = g(0).

- Directions (0, d''), d'' != 0, have lines inside the plane y_1 = c0. Their last n - 1
  coordinates form a sticky Kakeya set K^(0) in F_3^(n-1).
- For x = 1, 2, the directions (x, d''), d'' ∈ F_3^(n-1), form a full tree T_x of height n - 1.
  The point P(d) + t d lies in the plane u = g(x) + t x, so the plane u receives exactly the
  slice t_x(u) = x(u - g(x)) of T_x (x^(-1) = x in F_3). As u runs over F_3, t_x(u) runs over F_3.

Hence, exactly,

  |K| = |K^(0) ∪ S_(t_1(c0))(T_1) ∪ S_(t_2(c0))(T_2)| + sum_{u != c0} |S_(t_1(u))(T_1) ∪ S_(t_2(u))(T_2)|.   (2)

**Theorem 74.** U*(n) = Ψ(n - 1) := min over K', T, h of |K' ∪ S_h(T)| + sum_{t != h} |S_t(T)|,
with K' a sticky Kakeya set in F_3^(n-1) and T a full tree of height n - 1.

*Proof.* (>=) Drop T_2 from (2). As u runs over the two planes u != c0, t_1(u) runs over the two
slices other than h = t_1(c0). Also |K^(0) ∪ S_h(T_1)| >= |K' ∪ S_h(T_1)| for some sticky K' (the
lines of K^(0)). (<=) Given K', T (translated by any w), and h, put g = (0, -h, 0), use K' on the
directions (0, d''), use T + w on (1, d''), and use P_2(d'') = P_T(-d'') + w - h d'' on (2, d'').
By Lemma 159 the plane u receives S_(u+h)(T) + w from both x = 1 and x = 2. So the plane 0 holds
K' ∪ (S_h(T) + w), and the planes 1 and 2 hold the other two slices. The family is sticky:
every coordinate of every base point is a function of a prefix. ∎

The tests check (2) on 960 random sticky families in dimensions 1 to 4. They also check the
construction on 440 random triples (K', T, h, w) in dimensions 2 to 4.

**Corollary 74.1.** Since |K' ∪ S_h| >= max(U*(n-1), |S_h|), Lemma 158 gives
U*(n) >= min over a ∈ A_(n-1) and h of max(U*(n-1), a_h) + sum_{t != h} a_t, and the best h hides
the largest coordinate. Define G(0) = 0 and G(k+1) = min over a ∈ A_k of
a_0 + a_1 + a_2 + max(0, G(k) - max a). Then U*(n) >= G(n). The right side is nondecreasing in a,
and every node is monotone. So A_k may be replaced by node outputs over the Pareto front of A_(k-1).

## 3. Theorem 75: the values

**G(1..4) = 3, 7, 15, 29** (`test_G_values`). G(4) runs over all 27,436 node outputs of the 19
front vectors of height 2 (a184, Proposition 80). By hand: G(3) = 15 because every one of the six
front shapes at height 2 gives exactly 15 or more. For example, (3,3,9) hides 9 and gives
9 + 3 + 3, and (4,4,4) gives 7 + 4 + 4. For G(4) the case analysis has two cases.

- *Triangle root* (iso = identity). If the two smallest coordinates sum to at most 13, then two
  children carry (3,3,9)- or (3,4,8)-type pairs in those slots. That forces the third slot to
  at least 17 and the total to at least 30. Otherwise, either the maximum is at least 15 and
  the total is at least 14 + 15, or the bound is 15 + (at least 14).
- *Affine root* (constant slice s). Each child's pair outside s sums to 8 or more, except for
  (8,3,4)-type children (sum 7, but then M_s >= 8) and (9,3,3) (sum 6, but then M_s = 9). The
  bound is at least 29 in every case.

Equality holds at the triangles over ((3,3,9), (3,3,9), (5,3,5)) and ((3,3,9), (3,3,9), (4,4,4)),
with vectors (8,6,14) and (7,7,13), and at the affine node over three copies of (5,3,5), with
vector (5,9,15).

With the classical upper bound 2^(n+1) - 1 this gives **U* = 3, 7, 15 for n = 1, 2, 3 and
29 <= U*(4) <= 31**. U*(2) = 7 is also checked by exhausting all 19,683 relevant families.

**Remark (where the game is loose).** The two n = 4 triangle witnesses need real nesting, and
it can fail. Every 3-point slice of a (3,3,9) tree is a full row {u} × F_3 or a graph
{(i, φ(i))}, while the 3-point slice of the (3,5,5)-type tree has exactly two rows. So when the
children have exactly these sizes, the union in slice 1 of the (8,6,14) witness has at least 4
points, not 3, and the vector becomes (8,7,14), which gives at least 30. We have not excluded
every realisation of 29 or 30. The pre-registered CP-SAT job
`sticky-exact-v1` computes U*(4) exactly.

## 4. What this means

- **Small dimensions.** The game bound gave 1.90499^n - 1, i.e. 5.9 at n = 3 and 12.2 at n = 4.
  The split gives the exact value 15 at n = 3 and 29 at n = 4. Together with K(3,3) = 13 and the
  27-point sets in F_3^4, this makes the separation of a183 concrete: from dimension 3 on, the
  smallest Kakeya sets over F_3 are not sticky.
- **The real question now has two objects.** Theorem 74 turns "is the classical set the smallest
  sticky set?" into a question about one full tree T and one smaller sticky set K' at each step:
  two slices of T must be small, and the third must hide K' cheaply. The classical set balances
  all three at 2^(n-1). Theorem 73 says the game alone has trees whose largest coordinate is
  below 2^k, but it does not say whether real slices can be nested that well.
- **Question S5.** Is U*(n) = 2^(n+1) - 1 for every n? It holds for n <= 3. At n = 4, either
  U*(4) = 31, or a sticky set below the classical construction exists. The pre-registered job
  decides which.

## Checks (`test_sticky_planes.py`, run before anything is published)

- The height-2 front equals a184's 19 vectors. The closed form and the monotonicity of the
  plane bound hold. G = [0, 3, 7, 15, 29]. The witnesses come from front children and give 29.
- The general node rule matches the affine and triangle rules. Real slices dominate the game
  vector (450 random full trees). The union of the slices contains the Kakeya set.
- The exact split (2) holds on random families in dimensions 1 to 4. The construction of
  Theorem 74 gives the formula's size, contains a map for every prefix, and is a Kakeya set.
- The classical set has 2^(n+1) - 1 points and is Kakeya. The exhaustive minimum in dimension 2
  is 7. Random families respect G. The results file matches the code.

## Prior art

As in a182-a185: we know of no earlier definition or study of sticky Kakeya sets over finite
fields beyond the classical product construction. The exact value K(3,3) = 13 and the sets of 27
points in F_3^4 are MV2's own earlier computations (munimtechnologies/kakeya-conjecture, v1.1.0,
October 2026, checked by two ILP solvers and an exhaustive search). Pointers are welcome at
