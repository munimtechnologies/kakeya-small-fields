## What changes

- After job m30 ([a17.md](a17.md)), A(6) = 6 needs only the 2 open classes of (B4) and the 49
  open classes of (B5) = ("B", 5). Jobs m32 to m34 re-run them with 10 to 20 times the budget;
  they were queued at about 08:50Z on 2026-10-19 and have not run yet (the shared compute queue
  stalled). Their decision rule (`m32/predictions.md`) says: if more than 10 classes of (B5) stay
  open, split each by a second fixed matrix, designed in a note first.
- **Lemma 71** is that design. In (B5) the kernel of phi = beta(u, .) is span(u, h_1), and the
  element h_1 has a vector sigma(h_1) in ker A_u and a matrix A_(h_1) with kernel containing x0 and
  sigma(h_1) and image inside Im A_u. Both are fixed up to the exact stabiliser H_A of A_u, which
  is computed by linear algebra (not by Schreier generators), and checked against brute force at
  t = 4.
- The split is large: 12288 candidate pairs per class before symmetry, and the stabiliser orders
  implied by job m30's class sizes run from 6 to 1008, so the whole of (B5) would be at least
  33,000 instances. Whether that is affordable depends on the time per instance, which no earlier
  job measures. **Job m35** is a probe: three open classes with the pairs fixed, after controls,
  with the decision rule for the full split written down in `m35/predictions.md` before it runs.
- A by-product of the proof, (iv) below, is an identity that says what A_(h_1) is on P: it is
  A_u composed with the polar form of h_1 read through phi. It is linear and already implied by
  the (Q) equations of the encoding, so it is recorded here for the hand analysis, not added to the
  solver.

## Setting

As in [a14.md](a14.md), [a16.md](a16.md) and [a17.md](a17.md): X = F_2^t, sigma : V -> X quadratic
with polar form beta, h -> A_h linear, and A_(h') sigma(h) = (I + A_h) beta(h, h') (*) for all
h, h'. For u in V, x0 = sigma(u), phi = beta(u, .), P = phi(V), p = dim P, X_1 = ker A_u,
R = Im A_u, rho = rank A_u. The normal form of Lemma 67: sigma(u) = e_0, a basis h_0 = u, h_1, ...,
h_(d-1) of V with phi(h_i) = 0 for i < d - p and phi(h_(d-p+j)) = b_j; G = {g in GL(X) :
g e_0 = e_0, g P = P}; for a pair (X_1, R), H = {g in G : g X_1 = X_1, g R = R} (Lemma 70). The
cyclic identity (Q) of [a10.md](a10.md) is A_a beta(b, c) + A_b beta(c, a) + A_c beta(a, b) = 0; it
follows from (*) by polarising in h.

In (B5) at t = 6: d = 7, p = 5, so d - p = 2 and ker phi = span(h_0, h_1) = U+; X = P + span(x0);
and X_1 has dimension 3 with x0 in X_1, so X_1 n P has dimension 2 and, G containing GL(P), every
pair orbit of job m27 has X_1 = span(e_0, e_1, e_2) (job m30's class files confirm this for all 11
pair orbits).

## Lemma 71 (the second matrix)

**Lemma 71.** Let (sigma, alpha) be anisotropic and in the normal form of Lemma 67 for u, with
d - p >= 2 (so h_1 lies in ker phi), X_1 = ker A_u, R = Im A_u, A_u = A. Put s = sigma(h_1) and
N = A_(h_1). Then:

(i) s lies in X_1 and not in span(x0). Replacing h_1 by h_1 + h_0 keeps the normal form and
replaces (s, N) by (s + x0, N + A). So one may assume that the e_0-coordinate of s is 0.

(ii) N x0 = 0, N s = 0, and N P lies in R. When X = P + span(x0) (shape B with p = t - 1, as in
(B5)), Im N lies in R.

(iii) Let H_A = {g in H : g A g^(-1) = A}. For every g in H_A there is an anisotropic system in the
normal form of Lemma 67 for u, with the same shape and dimensions, with A_u = A, and with
(sigma(h_1), A_(h_1)) = (g s, g N g^(-1)), normalised as in (i). Consequently every anisotropic
system in the normal form for u with A_u = A is conjugate, by an element of H_A followed by at most
one replacement h_1 -> h_1 + h_0, to one whose pair (sigma(h_1), A_(h_1)) is a chosen
representative of one of the H_A-orbits on the normalised pairs satisfying (ii).

(iv) For every h in V, N phi(h) = A beta(h_1, h). So N restricted to P is A composed with the map
psi : P -> X, psi(phi(h)) = beta(h_1, h), which is well defined because ker phi = span(h_0, h_1)
lies in ker beta(h_1, .).

*Proof.* (i) (*) with h = h_1 and h' = u gives A_u sigma(h_1) = (I + A_(h_1)) beta(h_1, u) = 0,
since beta(u, h_1) = phi(h_1) = 0; so s is in X_1. s != 0 by anisotropy, and s != x0 because
sigma(h_0 + h_1) = x0 + s + beta(h_0, h_1) = x0 + s is not 0. The vector h_1 + h_0 lies in ker phi,
h_0 = u is untouched, and the other basis vectors are unchanged, so the normal form is kept;
sigma(h_1 + h_0) = s + x0 as just computed, and A_(h_1 + h_0) = N + A by linearity of h -> A_h.
Exactly one of s, s + x0 has e_0-coordinate 0.

(ii) N x0 = 0 is Lemma 69 (a) (phi(h_1) = 0). N s = 0 is (*) with h = h' = h_1, as beta(h_1, h_1)
= 0. N P in R is Lemma 69 (b). If X = P + span(x0), then Im N = N P + N span(x0) = N P lies in R.

(iii) As in the proofs of Lemmas 67 and 70: for g in G the system (g sigma, g A_h g^(-1)) satisfies
(*), is anisotropic, has the same u with g sigma(u) = e_0, and its phi is g phi, with the same
kernel span(h_0, h_1) and image g P = P. Replacing h_(d-p+j) by the combinations of the h_(d-p+l)
with g phi(h_(d-p+j)) = b_j restores the normal form without touching h_0 or h_1. The new A_u is
g A g^(-1) = A for g in H_A, the new sigma(h_1) is g s, and the new A_(h_1) is g N g^(-1). The
normalisation of (i) commutes with this action, because g x0 = x0 and g A g^(-1) = A. For the
consequence: by Lemma 70 the system may be taken in the normal form with A_u = A; by (i) and (ii)
its normalised pair is one of the candidates; it lies in the H_A-orbit of a representative
(s', N'), that is, (s, N) = g (s', N') for some g in H_A up to normalisation, and conjugating by
g^(-1) (and renormalising) gives a system in the normal form with A_u = A and pair (s', N').

(iv) (Q) with (a, b, c) = (u, h_1, h): A_u beta(h_1, h) + A_(h_1) beta(h, u) + A_h beta(u, h_1) = 0.
The last term is 0 since beta(u, h_1) = 0, and beta(h, u) = phi(h), so N phi(h) = A beta(h_1, h).
If phi(h) = phi(h'), then h + h' is in ker phi = span(h_0, h_1), and beta(h_1, h_0) = beta(h_1, h_1)
= 0, so beta(h_1, h) = beta(h_1, h'). QED

**Counting.** The normalised pairs: (2^(t - rho) - 2) / 2 choices of s, and for each s the
matrices N with N x0 = N s = 0, N P in R and the rest free: |R|^(dim(P + span(x0, s)) - 2) times
2^(t (t - dim(P + span(x0, s)))). In (B5) at t = 6: 3 times 8^4 = 12288 per class. The stabiliser
H_A is found exactly: g = I + D with D x0 = 0, D P in P, D X_1 in X_1, D R in R and D A = A D are
linear conditions on the 36 entries of D; the solution space is enumerated and the invertible
elements kept (g X_1 in X_1 with g invertible gives g X_1 = X_1). By hand, if job m30's Schreier
sample reached all of H (no late merges were seen), |H_A| = |H| / (class size) = |G| / (pair orbit
size) / (class size):

    orbit size              155  6510  1085  6510  1085 17360 13020 52080 39060  9920  69440

With 12288 pairs per class, the H_A-orbits number at least 12288 / |H_A|: from 13 (orbit 9, class
5) to 2048 (orbit 10, the classes with |H_A| = 6). Over the 49 open classes that is at least
33,000 instances. The split is only affordable if the instances are fast.

**What is not known by hand.** Whether the instances with A_u, s and N all fixed decide quickly.
Job m30 showed that fixing A_u took ("A", 5) from 0 of 22 decided (m27, m29) to 141 of 141 in
under 16 seconds each, but ("B", 5) stayed at 17 of 66 at one minute. Nothing says the second
matrix helps as much as the first.

   There h_1 = a_1 has sigma(a_1) = E_1 = e_0 + e_4 + e_5 and A_(a_1) = E_11 (diag), so the instance

## Candidates for a further hand fact in (B5)

If the probe shows the split is too slow, these are the next things to try by hand, in order:

- The rank of h_1. By Corollary 46 (and the two (B4) classes, once closed), an element of rank
  exactly 3 is in (B5), so if rank A_(h_1) = 3 then h_1 is itself in (B5) with ker beta(h_1, .) =
  span(u, h_1) and sigma(h_1) outside its P. The cases rank A_(h_1) in {0, 1, 2} are the shapes of
  Lemma 68 ([a15.md](a15.md)).
- (iv) applied twice: A_(h_1) phi = A_u psi and, by symmetry, A_u beta(h_1, .) = A_(h_1) phi. So
  A_u psi(P) = A_(h_1) P lies in R n Im A_(h_1), and psi(P) + X_1 is constrained by the rank of
  A_(h_1).
- The two-dimensional restriction to span(u, h_1): sigma takes the values x0, s, x0 + s there, all
  of A_u, A_(h_1), A_(u + h_1) kill span(x0, s), and these three matrices have ranks at most 3 with
  images in R, R' and R + R'.
