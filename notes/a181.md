## Summary

Let beta_3 = lim_n K(n,3)^(1/n), where K(n,3) is the least size of a Kakeya set in F_3^n.
After a180, 1.82074 <= beta_3 <= (27/4)^(1/3) = 1.88988.

1. **Reformulation.** Over F_3, the lower-bound route of a180 is exactly the *injective
   sums-differences problem in F_3^N*. Call its constant C(F_3). Then
   3^(1/C(F_3)) <= beta_3 <= 3^(1/R0), where R0 = log 27 / log(27/4) = 1.725982 is the ratio of
   Ruzsa's example. Ruzsa's example is also realised in F_3: it is the frame map d -> d^2, i.e. the
   KLSS construction. So **Conjecture R3 (C(F_3) = R0) would give the exact value
   beta_3 = (27/4)^(1/3).**
2. Over the integers R3 is false: Lemm (2015) beat Ruzsa (C_3b >= 1.77898). R3 says that
   characteristic 3 behaves differently.
3. **Theorem 69.** In characteristic 3, Ruzsa's product rho^n is a *strict local maximum* of the
   ratio in every F_3^N, N >= n. This holds under reweighting and under adding new points.
4. **Proposition 76.** Over Z it is not. Adding mass eps/2 at (2,0) and (0,2) to Ruzsa's triangle
   beats it for all small eps (1.726068 > 1.725982 at eps = 10^-4).
5. **Lemma 147.** Lemm's four-point and seven-point configurations collapse in every group of
   exponent 3: their differences form arithmetic progressions of length >= 4.
6. A search for global counterexamples to R3 in F_3^2 and F_3^3 is pre-registered (section 6). It has not run yet.

This is a structural result, not a new bound on beta_3.

## 1. Configurations and the constant C(F_3)

A *configuration* in F_3^N is a law mu of a direction D in F_3^N together with a base point P(d)
for each direction. Its slices are Y_t = P(D) + tD for t in F_3. Put X = Y_1 and Y = Y_0. Then
X - Y = D and X + Y = 2P + D = -(P + 2D) = -Y_2. So (X, Y) is a pair whose difference determines it,
with H(X) = H(Y_1), H(Y) = H(Y_0), H(X + Y) = H(Y_2) and H(X - Y) = H(D). Conversely every such pair
arises this way, with P(D) = Y. (Test: `test_is_sum_difference_instance`.)

Write R_max = H(D)/max_t H(Y_t) and R_avg = H(D)/avg_t H(Y_t). Define
C(F_3) = sup R_max over all N and all configurations.

*Max and average give the same supremum.* R_max <= R_avg always. Conversely, let (D1, D2, D3)
be i.i.d. copies with base point (P(D1), P(D2) + D2, P(D3) + 2D3). Its slice t is
(Y_t, Y_{t+1}, Y_{t+2}), whose entropy is the sum over all three slices for every t. So the
triple product has R_max equal to the R_avg of the original.

*Kakeya link (a180, proof of Theorem 68).* From a Kakeya set K in F_3^n, choose one line per
nonzero direction, let D be uniform on the nonzero directions and let P(D) be the line's first
point. This gives a configuration with H(D) = log(3^n - 1) and every H(Y_t) <= log|K|, so
|K| >= (3^n - 1)^(1/C(F_3)). Hence beta_3 >= 3^(1/C(F_3)). The a180 constants are valid upper
bounds for C(F_3): 11/6 (Katz-Tao), and 1.826348 (Farnamfar, arXiv:2611.19594, unrefereed, valid
without 2-torsion).

*Ruzsa = frame map.* rho on F_3 is mu uniform with P(d) = d^2. Its slices are d^2, d^2 + d and
d^2 + 2d, and slice t separates the direction d = t from the other two. Each slice has law
(1/3, 2/3), so R_max = R_avg = log 3 / h(1/3) = R0. The product rho^n has the same ratio. Since
3^(1/R0) = (27/4)^(1/3) is the KLSS upper bound for beta_3:

> **Conjecture R3.** Every configuration in every F_3^N has H(D) <= R0 * avg_t H(Y_t).
> Equivalently C(F_3) = log 27 / log(27/4). R3 implies beta_3 = (27/4)^(1/3) = 1.88988...

R3 implies the Kakeya-restricted conjecture C_3 of a180, where D must be uniform. R3 is the
stronger statement and makes no Kakeya assumption.

## 2. Three lemmas

**Lemma 144 (frame bound).** Let f: F_3^n -> R have mean 0 under the uniform law, and let Y_t
be the slices of rho^n. Then sum_t E[(E[f | Y_t])^2] <= (3/2) E[f^2]. Equality holds for every f
when n = 1, and the constant 3/2 is attained for every n >= 1.

*Proof.* Slice t of rho^n generates the same sigma-algebra as the indicators 1[d_j = t]
(j = 1..n). Split L^2(F_3) = R1 + V with V the mean-zero functions (dimension 2). The vectors
e_t = 1_t - 1/3 satisfy |e_t|^2 = 2/9 and <e_s, e_t> = -1/9. So their unit multiples u_0, u_1, u_2
are three unit vectors at 120 degrees in a plane, and sum_t u_t u_t^T = (3/2) I_V.
Expand f = sum over nonempty S of f_S (Efron-Stein, f_S in V^(tensor S)). Conditional
expectation on slice t is the tensor product of the projections onto span{1, e_t}. On
V^(tensor S) it is the projection onto the line spanned by w_t = u_t^(tensor S), so
E[(E[f|Y_t])^2] = sum_S <f_S, w_t>^2.
For |S| = s the w_t are unit vectors with Gram matrix (1 - a)I + aJ, a = (-1/2)^s. Its largest
eigenvalue is max(1 + 2a, 1 - a), which equals 3/2 for s = 1, 2 and is at most 9/8 for s >= 3.
So sum_t <f_S, w_t>^2 <= (3/2)|f_S|^2. Summing over S gives the bound. For n = 1 the frame
identity gives equality. For n >= 2, f = sum_t e_t (tensor) e_t attains 3/2.
Tests `test_gram`, `test_frame_bound_exact` and `test_frame_bound_tight_n2` check this exactly
in rationals for n = 1, 2, 3. ∎

**Lemma 145 (first-order cancellation).** For rho^n and every d,
prod_t P(Y_t = Y_t(d)) = (4/27)^n.

*Proof.* In each coordinate exactly one of the three slices isolates d_j (probability 1/3). The
other two put it in a block of probability 2/3, and the slice laws are product laws. Checked
exactly for n <= 3 (`test_first_order_cancellation`). ∎

**Lemma 146 (one join).** Let 1 <= n < N, let d be a direction of F_3^N outside F_3^n x 0, and
let b be in F_3^N. Then b + td lies in F_3^n x 0 for at most one t in F_3.

*Proof.* The last N - n coordinates b'' + t d'' (with d'' nonzero) take three different values
as t runs over F_3. Checked exhaustively for (n, N) = (1,2), (1,3), (2,3) (`test_one_join`). ∎

All slice values of rho^n lie in F_3^n x 0. So a new point can share its value with old points in
at most one of its three slices. Over Z the analogue fails (Proposition 76).

## 3. Theorem 69 (local optimality in characteristic 3)

**Theorem 69.** Let 1 <= n <= N and view rho^n as a configuration in F_3^N. There is
delta_N > 0 with the following property. Measure distance as total variation between the laws of
the point (D, P(D)). Every configuration at distance in (0, delta_N) from rho^n satisfies
H(D) < R0 * avg_t H(Y_t), and hence R_max <= R_avg < R0.

*Proof.* **Step 0.** rho^n puts mass 3^-n on each of the 3^n directions of F_3^n x 0. If
delta_N < 3^-n / 2, every old point keeps positive mass. Each direction has a single base point,
so P agrees with rho^n on all old directions, and every other point has a direction outside
F_3^n x 0. Write mu = (1 - eps) nu + eps lambda with nu on old directions (close to uniform) and
lambda on new ones.

**Step 1 (reweighting).** Let nu = u(1 + f) with u uniform, E f = 0 and g_t = E[f | Y_t]. Then
H(nu) = n log 3 - E[f^2]/2 + O(|f|_inf E f^2) and
H(nu_t) = H_* - E[f log p_t(Y_t)] - E[g_t^2]/2 + O(|f|_inf E f^2).
By Lemma 145, sum_t log p_t(Y_t) is the constant n log(4/27), so the first-order terms cancel in
the average. By Lemma 144,
H(nu) - R0 avg_t H(nu_t) <= -(1/2)(1 - R0/2) E[f^2] + O(|f|_inf E f^2).
Since 1 - R0/2 = 0.1370 > 0, there is eta_n > 0 such that H(nu) <= R0 avg_t H(nu_t) when
|f|_inf < eta_n, with equality only at f = 0.

**Step 2 (new points).** Let o_t be the lambda-mass of new points whose slice-t value lies outside
F_3^n x 0. By Lemma 146, sum_t o_t >= 2. Directions are disjoint, so
H(D) = h(eps) + (1 - eps)H(nu) + eps H(lambda) <= (1 - eps)H(nu) + eps log(1/eps) + eps(1 + N log 3).
Split each slice law into old and new values and use concavity. This gives
H(Y_t) >= (1 - eps)H(nu_t) + h(eps o_t) >= (1 - eps)H(nu_t) + eps o_t log(1/eps), and therefore
R0 avg_t H(Y_t) - H(D) >= (1 - eps)[R0 avg_t H(nu_t) - H(nu)] + eps[kappa log(1/eps) - 1 - N log 3],
with kappa = 2R0/3 - 1 = 0.15065. The first bracket is >= 0 by Step 1. The second is > 0 when
eps < exp(-(1 + N log 3)/kappa). If eps > 0 the inequality is strict. If eps = 0 then nu is not
uniform and Step 1 is strict. ∎

**Remark 69.1 (scope).** The result is local. delta_N shrinks with N, and the eps-threshold
in Step 2 is crude: one new point of mass up to 10^-2 already lowers the ratio
(`test_new_point_lowers_ratio`). Reweightings of rho^1 and rho^2 at size 10^-3 lower it too
(`test_reweighting_lowers_ratio`). The theorem does not prove R3. What it shows is that no
perturbative, Lemm-style improvement of Ruzsa exists in characteristic 3. Anything that beats
Ruzsa there must be far from rho^n in total variation.

**Remark 69.2 (the two margins).** Reweighting: the three slices together keep at most 3/2 of
the chi-square information of the direction, i.e. 1/2 on average. Beating Ruzsa would need
1/R0 = 0.5794. New points: in characteristic 3 a new point is visible in at least two of the
three slices. It therefore costs at least 2R0/3 = 1.1507 units of R0-weighted slice entropy per
unit of direction entropy. Over Z it can be visible in only one slice (Prop. 76).

**Remark 69.3 (dynamics).** For a fixed map P, F(mu) = H(mu) - (R0/3) sum_t H(Y_t) is concave
plus convex. The concave-convex step mu(d) proportional to prod_t p_t(Y_t(d))^(R0/3) never
decreases F (`sd3_search.cccp`; `test_cccp_monotone`). At rho^n it linearises to
f -> (R0/3) sum_t E[f|Y_t], which by Lemma 144 has norm <= R0/2 = 0.863. So rho^n is an attracting
fixed point (`test_cccp_returns_to_ruzsa`).

**Remark 69.4 (N = 1, numerical only).** In F_3 an injective configuration with three points is
either collinear in one form (R_avg <= 3/2) or a triangle. For a triangle, R_avg <= R0 is the
two-variable inequality sum_i [psi(1 - p_i) - log(9/4)/log 3 * psi(p_i)] >= 0, with
psi(x) = -x log x. It holds on a 1/240 grid of the simplex, with equality at the uniform point
(`test_n1_triangle_grid`). We have not written a proof.

## 4. Characteristic 0 is different

**Proposition 76.** In Z^2, give (0,1), (1,0), (1,1) mass (1 - eps)/3 each and (2,0), (0,2) mass
eps/2 each. Then X - Y is injective, and for all sufficiently small eps > 0,
H(X - Y) > R0 max(H(X), H(Y), H(X + Y)).

*Proof.* (2,0) shares its Y-value with (1,0) and its (X+Y)-value with (1,1). (0,2) shares its
X-value with (0,1) and its (X+Y)-value with (1,1). So each new point is new in only one form.
Then H(X - Y) = log 3 + eps log(1/eps) + O(eps), H(X) = H(Y) = H_* + (eps/2) log(1/eps) + O(eps)
and H(X + Y) = H_* + O(eps). The difference is (1 - R0/2) eps log(1/eps) + O(eps) > 0.
At eps = 10^-4 the ratio is 1.726068 > 1.725982 (`test_z_perturbation_beats_ruzsa`). ∎

So Ruzsa's example is a strict local maximum in characteristic 3 but not over Z. Lemm's
construction is the global version of this perturbation.

**Lemma 147 (Lemm's configurations collapse in exponent 3).** Let G be an abelian group with
3G = 0, and write S = X + Y and D = X - Y.
(a) Lemm's four-point pattern (p1 = (0,1), p2 = (1,0), p3 = (1,1), p4 = (2,0) over Z) has the
coincidences X(p2) = X(p3), Y(p1) = Y(p3), Y(p2) = Y(p4), S(p1) = S(p2) and S(p3) = S(p4). Any
four points of G^2 with these coincidences satisfy D(p1) = D(p4).
(b) Lemm's seven-point staircase has the coincidences Y(p1) = Y(p2), X(p2) = X(p3), and so on
alternately, with S constant on odd and on even indices. Any seven points of G^2 with these
coincidences satisfy D(p1) = D(p4).

*Proof.* (a) Put p2 = p1 + (a, -a), which uses S. The cycle p2 ~Y p4 ~S p3 ~Y p1 forces
p3 = p1 + (b, 0) and p4 = p1 + (a + b, -a), and X(p2) = X(p3) forces b = a. So the D-values are
d, d + 2a, d + a, d + 3a, and 3a = 0. (b) The steps alternate (s, 0) and (0, -s) for a single s
(the S-coincidences force this), so D(p_i) = D(p1) + (i - 1)s and D(p4) = D(p1) + 3s = D(p1).
Part (a) is also checked exhaustively in F_3 and F_3^2 (`test_lemm4_collapses_in_exponent_3`). ∎

In both of Lemm's configurations the differences form arithmetic progressions of length 4 or
more, which wrap around in exponent 3.

## 5. Where this leaves the Kakeya constant of F_3

| quantity | lower | upper |
|---|---|---|
| C(F_3) (char-3 sums-differences) | R0 = 1.725982 (Ruzsa) | 1.826348 (Farnamfar, unrefereed); 11/6 (Katz-Tao) |
| beta_3 (Kakeya growth base) | 1.82074 (a180); 1.82492 with Farnamfar | 1.88988 (KLSS) |
| C_3b over the reals | 1.77898 (Lemm; AlphaEvolve and Mosaic in later digits) | 1.826348 (Farnamfar) |

The wow, if R3 is proved, would be the exact Kakeya growth constant of a finite field,
beta_3 = (27/4)^(1/3). Two routes are open.

- **Upper bounds on C(F_3).** Use Shannon-type certificates that exploit 3 = 0. These are the
  F_3 closures in `kakeya_sparse.py` (job kakeya-sparse-v1, queued). Theorem 69 shows that any
  such certificate is tight to second order at rho^n, so it must be sharp there.
- **Refutation.** Find a configuration far from rho^n that beats R0 (section 6).

## 6. Pre-registered search (job sd3-search-v1, not yet run)

`sd3_search.py` samples maps P of F_3^N for N = 2 and N = 3: uniformly random maps, random
quadratic maps (frame maps are quadratic) and perturbations of rho^N. For each map it runs the
concave-convex procedure from the uniform law and from random laws. It records the largest
F = H(D) - R0 avg_t H(Y_t), and the largest R_avg among configurations with H(D) > 0.3 nats.

*Reading rule, fixed now.* If a configuration has F > 10^-9 and still has R_avg > R0 + 10^-9
when re-evaluated from its printed weights, it refutes R3 in that dimension. It would also show
that the a180 route via sums-differences cannot reach beta_3 = 1.88988, though the
Kakeya-restricted C_3 would remain open. Otherwise the run is evidence only. We will report the
number of maps tried and the best R_avg; sampling does not cover all maps.

## Prior art

We found no study of the sums-differences constant in groups of exponent 3 or in F_3^n.
- Lemm (arXiv:1404.3745) and Tao's optimisation-constants page (constant 3b) work over the
  reals; Lemm notes that the problem does not depend on the real vector space.
- Farnamfar (arXiv:2611.19594) proves upper bounds for groups without 2-torsion, which include
  F_3^n. He gives no lower bounds beyond the reals and does not mention finite fields.

Pointers to earlier work are welcome.
