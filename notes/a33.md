## What changes

- **Job m42c ran** (2026-10-20 02:13 to 03:46 UTC, 1 CPU, 89 minutes of solving). It is the
  "every second class" half of (B5): 210 of the 420 classes of A_u that m42a had counted
  ([a32.md](a32.md)). Its classification step gave the same 49 pair orbits and 420 classes as m42a
  (same code and seeds), in 12 s. The job had no selfcheck step of its own (that was m42a's), so
  its grade has no graded entries; the controls it relies on are m42a's 96.
- **168 classes UNSAT, 42 UNKNOWN, no SAT.** The exploratory guess for (B5) was "at least 70
  percent decided in this pass"; 80 percent were. 80 of the 168 UNSAT answers came in under 10 s,
  86 in 10 to 60 s, and 2 in about 4 minutes (pair orbit 19, classes 21 and 24: 257 and 247 s). The
  42 open classes had budgets of 40 to 63 s, except two that reached 107 s (pair 30, class 11) and
  475 s (pair 39, class 22). That is no evidence either way for them.
- **The solver's time follows the invariants of Lemma 83.** Sorting the 210 tried classes by the
  kind of R = Im A_u and by d = dim(X_1 n R) (table below):

      kind L (x0 in R) with d <= 2:   39 tried, 39 UNSAT (all within 43 s), 0 open
      d = 0 (any kind):               48 tried, 43 UNSAT, 5 open
      d = 1, kind P or graph:         89 tried, 80 UNSAT, 9 open
      d = 2, kind P or graph:         29 tried,  6 UNSAT, 23 open
      d = 3 (R inside ker A_u):        5 tried,  0 UNSAT, 5 open

  So in (B5) the hard core is **d = 2 or 3 with x0 outside Im A_u**: 28 of the 42 open classes.
  Part of this held in m42a for (B6): its four UNSAT answers were all in the one pair orbit with
  d = 0, and both tried classes with d = 3 stayed open. But (B6) is slower across the board: its
  kind-L pair 7 (d = 1, 2 classes tried) and its d = 1 pairs 8 to 10 (14 tried) were all still
  open at 200 s, where (B5) closed 39 of 39 and 80 of 89 such classes in under a minute. The
  instances differ in the size of ker phi (4 in (B5), 3 in (B6)), and so in the linear conditions
  m42 adds for it (111 against 79). The first pattern is (B5)'s so far, not (B6)'s.
- **Job m46 (designed, not queued)** gives the 42 open classes 7 to 8 minutes each, in two jobs of
  20 and 22 (by the orbit of X_1), as the decision rule of m42 asks (at least three times the
  budget). It was not queued because this field already had 12 jobs waiting in the shared queue
  (m42b, m42d, m43a to m43d, m44a to m44d, m45a, m45b); it is queued when m42d reports, so that
  m42d's leftovers can be added to it.

## What the pattern suggests, and what it does not prove

The solver is not a proof of anything but UNSAT, and a short solving time is only a hint that a short
argument exists. Still, two hints are worth writing down as targets for hand work, because each
would remove whole pair orbits from both (B5) and (B6) at once:

1. **Kind L (x0 in Im A_u) with d <= 2.** Every one of the 39 tried classes of (B5) was UNSAT
   within 43 s (in (B6), the two tried classes of this kind were still open at 200 s, so the hint
   is weaker there).
   In Case A at rank 4 the same statement, x0 outside Im A_u, is Lemma 77(c) (the step of Lemma 52
   for x0 in R); its proof uses elements over x0 + r, which Case B does not have (x0 is not in P).
   A proof here would have to come from (1) to (3) of a24.md for n in ker phi together with (*) at
   h' = u, or from Lemma 30 applied to an element of ker phi. If it exists, it closes 9 pair orbits
   of (B5) (3, 7, 11, 14, 21, 25, 34, 40, 43; 97 classes, 39 of them already UNSAT) and 2 of (B6)
   (pairs 3 and 7, 15 classes). At t = 6, Corollary 47 ([a20.md](a20.md)) proved by machine that
   sigma(w) lies outside Im A_w when rank A_w^2 = 2; that is the d = 1 case of the same statement,
   one dimension lower.
2. **d = 3, that is R inside X_1 (A_u^2 = 0).** None of the 7 tried classes (5 here, 2 in m42a) was
   decided, at budgets up to 200 s. This is the configuration of Lemma 79 for (A5), where it was
   forced; in (B6) and (B5) it is one option among four. It needs either a hand argument or a much
   longer budget: 7 classes here plus 3 untried (pairs 1, 31, 33 of (B5) and pair 1 of (B6)).

Nothing above is a lemma. The decision rule stands: a class counts only when the solver answers.

## Cost, updated

After m42a and m42c, A(7) <= 8 by this method still needs: 64 classes of (B6) (30 in m45, 34 in
m42b), 252 of (B5) (42 in m46, 210 in m42d), 210 of shape (d2) (jobs m43), 54 of shape (c) (jobs
m44): 580 classes, down from 748 in a32.md. (B5) turned out cheaper than (B6): 80 percent of its
first half closed in a 40 s pass, against 12 percent of (B6)'s at 200 s. If m42d behaves like m42c,
(B5) will have about 85 open classes after the first pass, and a 7-minute pass on each is about
10 CPU hours.

## What does not follow

- Nothing is decided for (B5): 168 of 420 classes are UNSAT, 42 are open after one short pass, and
  210 have not been tried. 7 <= A(7) <= 9 still.
- The kinds-and-time pattern is an observation about one solver on one encoding; it proves nothing
  and could change with the longer budgets of m46.
- The 168 UNSAT answers were saved without proof certificates. m42c ran no selfcheck of its own;
  its code and seeds are m42a's, which held all 96 graded checks, and its classification step
  reproduced m42a's 49 pair orbits and 420 classes.
