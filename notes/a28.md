## Setting

As in a24.md and a27.md: t = 7, dim V = 9, X = F_2^7, u of rank 4 in Case B with k = 3,
x0 = sigma(u), phi = beta(u, .), P = phi(V) a hyperplane missing x0, N = ker phi of dimension 3,
X_1 = ker A_u and R = Im A_u (dimension 4). The forms d2c0 and d2c1 fix

    x0 = e_0,  P = span(e_1, ..., e_6),  X_1 = W = span(e_0, e_1, e_2),  N = span(u, v_1, v_2),
    sigma(v_1) = e_1,  sigma(v_2) = beta(v_1, v_2) = delta  (e_2 in d2c0, e_0 + e_2 in d2c1),
    A_(v_1): e_0, e_1 -> 0, e_2 -> delta;  A_(v_2) kills W;  phi(v_(2+j)) = e_j for 1 <= j <= 6.

Write Y = span(e_1, e_2) = W n P and

    K = {g in GL(X) : g e_0 = e_0, g e_1 = e_1, g e_2 = e_2, g P = P}.

Every g in K keeps the form: replace the system by g sigma, g beta, g A_h g^(-1), and choose the new
v_(2+j) over g e_j, which lies in P = phi(V). The values on u, v_1, v_2 do not change, since g fixes
W pointwise and delta lies in W. So R can be replaced by any g R, and then A_u by its conjugate under
any element of K that keeps R.

By Lemma 78, delta lies in R: Step 1 of the proof of Lemma 57 puts C = span(sigma(v_2)) + beta(v_2, V)
inside R, and sigma(v_2) = delta.

## Lemma 84 (the orbits of R)

**Lemma 84.** The K-orbits of 4-dimensional subspaces R of X that contain delta number 7 for d2c0
and 5 for d2c1. Each orbit is fixed by R_W = R n W (which K does not move), and, when R_W lies in Y,
by whether R lies in P:

    R_W                      d2c0 (delta = e_2)                d2c1 (delta = e_0 + e_2)
    span(delta)              R in P: 120;  R not in P: 840     960
    each of the 3 planes     Y: R in P: 35, not: 105;          140 each
      containing delta       span(e_0, e_2), span(e_0 + e_1, e_2): 140 each
    W                        15                                15

The sizes add up to 1395 in both columns, the number of 4-dimensional subspaces of F_2^7 through a
given non-zero vector.

*Proof.* *Step 1 (K in blocks).* In the basis e_0, e_1, e_2 | e_3, ..., e_6, an element of K is

    g = [ I_3  M ]
        [  0   A ]      with A in GL_4(F_2) and M a 3 x 4 matrix whose row for e_0 is zero,

because g fixes W pointwise and maps each e_j (j >= 3) into P, so g e_j has no e_0 component. Every
such matrix keeps P and lies in K. So |K| = 2^8 |GL_4(F_2)| = 256 x 20160 = 5160960.

*Step 2 (the invariant R_W).* Since g fixes W pointwise, R n W is the same for every R in an
orbit. Let R_W = R n W, r = dim R_W (1 <= r <= 3, as delta is in R_W), and let Rbar be the image of R
in X / W, identified with F_2^4 through e_3, ..., e_6; dim Rbar = m = 4 - r. Then R is given by R_W,
Rbar and a linear map f : Rbar -> W / R_W (R is the set of w + x + f(x) with w in R_W and x in
Rbar), and different f give different R. So there are [4 choose m]_2 x 2^(m (3 - r)) subspaces R for
each R_W.

*Step 3 (orbits for a fixed R_W).* The elements with M = 0 act on Rbar through A, and GL_4 is
transitive on m-dimensional subspaces, with the stabiliser of Rbar inducing all of GL(Rbar). The
elements with A = I send f to f + M|Rbar modulo R_W, where M is any linear map from X / W into Y.
So once Rbar is fixed, what is left of f is its composite fbar with W / R_W -> W / (R_W + Y).
- If R_W does not lie in Y, then R_W + Y = W (Y is a plane of the 3-dimensional W), fbar = 0, and
  there is one orbit.
- If R_W lies in Y, then W / (R_W + Y) = W / Y is a line, fbar is a linear form on Rbar, and A acts on
  it by composing with an element of GL(Rbar). So there are two orbits, fbar = 0 and fbar != 0, as
  m >= 1. fbar = 0 means that R lies in Y + span(e_3, ..., e_6) = P.

*Step 4 (the count).* The subspaces of W through delta are span(delta), the three planes through
delta, and W. In d2c0, delta = e_2 lies in Y, and so do span(e_2) and the plane Y; the planes
span(e_0, e_2) and span(e_0 + e_1, e_2) do not. In d2c1, delta = e_0 + e_2 is not in Y, so no
R_W lies in Y. Step 3 then gives 2 + 2 + 1 + 1 + 1 = 7 orbits for d2c0 and 1 + 3 + 1 = 5 for d2c1.
The sizes: r = 1 gives 15 x 2^6 = 960 subspaces, of which 15 x 2^3 = 120 lie in P when R_W = span(e_2)
(f has values in Y / R_W); r = 2 gives 35 x 2 = 140, of which 35 lie in P when R_W = Y (f = 0); r = 3
gives 15. QED

**The same count at t = 4, 5, 6.** With A_u of rank t - 3, X / W has dimension t - 3 and the same
steps give |K| = 2^(2(t - 3)) |GL_(t-3)(F_2)| = 4, 96, 10752 and orbit sizes

    t = 6:  d2c0  1, 7, 7, 14, 14, 28, 84       d2c1  1, 14, 14, 14, 112
    t = 5:  d2c0  1, 1, 1, 6, 6                 d2c1  1, 1, 1, 12
    t = 4:  d2c0  1                             d2c1  1

(at t = 5 the plane Y is R itself, so it gives one orbit, not two). The selfcheck of m43.py
recomputes all of these by closure under the 20 transvections of K, which generate it, and at
t = 4, 5, 6 enumerates K in full.

## Job m43 (queued, not run)

`cube_groups/m43.py` uses case_b.py and case_b_shapes.py unchanged. For each orbit representative
R it computes the classes of A_u, the 20160 matrices with kernel W and image R, under conjugation by
24 random units of the algebra {g : g e_i in span(e_i) for i < 3, g P <= P, g R <= R}. The units of
this algebra are exactly the stabiliser of R in K. Every unit is checked to lie in it, so every
computed class lies inside one true class and the split is complete either way. At t = 4, 5 and 6
the computed classes must equal the exact orbits of the whole stabiliser.

Each instance is the CNF of case_b_shapes.encode_shape(7, 3, form) (sat_aniso.encode for (7, 9)
without its echelon form, with the 84 + 63 unit clauses of Lemmas 35 and 37), plus 28 unit clauses
that fix A_u on e_3, ..., e_6, plus conditions from facts already proved:

- for n = v_1, v_2 in ker phi, Im A_n lies in R: by (2) and (3) of [a10.md](a10.md), A_n maps
  P = phi(V) into R and A_n x0 = phi(n) = 0, and X = P + span(x0) (42 linear conditions);
- beta(v_2, v_j) lies in R for j >= 3 (Lemma 78, as C = R; 18 linear conditions);
- A_(v_2) has rank exactly 1 (Lemma 78): its columns on e_3, ..., e_6 are lambda_c y for new
  variables lambda_c and y, and one clause asks for a non-zero column.

The controls: case_b's (t, k) = (2, 1) example must be SAT; every class at t = 4, 5 and 6 (dim V =
t + 2) must be UNSAT, as A(t) = t there. There is no positive control in a d2 form: a satisfiable one
at t <= 6 would contradict A(t) = t. This is the same situation as in job m40.

    m43a   unit tests; selfcheck; controls (18 minutes); d2c0, every second class, 95 minutes
    m43b   d2c0, the other classes, 140 minutes
    m43c   d2c1, every second class, 140 minutes
    m43d   d2c1, the other classes, 140 minutes

Within a job each class gets an equal share of the time left, at least 20 seconds and at most 10
minutes. The predictions and the decision rule were written before the jobs were queued.

What a result would mean. If every class of both forms is UNSAT, shape (d2) does not occur at rank 4
for t = 7 and dim V = 9 (computer-assisted, resting on Lemmas 37 and 78). With Corollary 52 (Case A),
Lemma 42 (shape (d1)) and shape (c) (jobs m40b to m40d), no element would have rank 4. A verified SAT
would give A(7) = 9.

## What does not follow

- Nothing is decided until m40, m41, m42 and m43 run: 7 <= A(7) <= 9.
- Lemma 84 counts orbits of R. It does not count the classes of A_u, which m43 computes by machine.
  If the random units missed part of a stabiliser, there are only more classes, not fewer.
- The conditions of the search use Lemma 78, so an UNSAT answer rests on it as well as on the solver.
