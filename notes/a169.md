## 0. Summary

Program (a159-a168): for a map P of F_q^k let

    Lambda(P) = sum_{t in F_q} H(X | Y_t),   Y_t = P(X) + tX   (bits, X uniform on F_q^k).

By Theorem 2 (README), beta_q = q 2^(-lambda*/q), where lambda* = sup_k max_P Lambda(P)/k
and beta_q is the Kakeya growth constant of F_q. The conjecture C_q(k), Lambda <= k(q-1),
for all k says lambda* = q - 1. a168 (Theorem 47, peeling) reduced it to the
one-coordinate criterion

    (*)  every map P of every F_q^k has a graph slicing f with
         ell_P(f) = sum_t H(f(X) | Y_t) <= q - 1.

This note proves four things.

> **Theorem 48 (averaged slicing).** For every map P of F_q^k (any q, any k >= 1), the
> average of ell_P(psi) over the nonzero linear functionals psi is at most
> (1 - p_k) Lambda(P), where p_k = (q^(k-1) - 1)/(q^k - 1).
> **Corollary 48.1.** Every map with Lambda(P) <= q - q^(1-k) satisfies (*) with a linear
> functional. Squaring in F_(q^k) has Lambda = q - q^(1-k) exactly and ell = q - 1 for
> every psi, so it attains the threshold with no slack.
> **Corollary 48.2.** For odd q, every map whose fibres all have at most 2 points has
> Lambda <= q - q^(1-k), so it satisfies (*).

So a map that breaks (*) must lose more than the squaring map, and for odd q it must have
fibres of 3 or more points. This narrows the inverse problem left open in a168.

> **Lemma 123 (triangle lemma, every map, every k).** Call x != y *t-partners*
> (x ~_t y) if P(x) + tx = P(y) + ty. Every triangle of partners is either inside one fibre (all three
> times equal) or collinear with three distinct times. If y ~_t x ~_t' z with t != t' and
> x - z = mu (x - y), then y ~_s z with s = (t - mu t')/(1 - mu). On each line, being
> partners is an equivalence relation.

> **Proposition 56 (directional decomposition of the plane).** For every map of F_q^2 and
> every direction v, Lambda = ell(nu_v) + Lambda_v. Here nu_v is a functional with kernel
> span(v), and Lambda_v, the loss along lines parallel to v, is at most q - 1. Also
> sum_v Lambda_v >= Lambda, with equality when every fibre is collinear. Hence (*) with
> linear slicings in the plane is equivalent to max_v Lambda_v >= Lambda - (q - 1).

> **Theorem 49 (record slack).** Suppose that for some L < q log2(2 - 1/q), every map of
> every F_q^k (k >= 2) has a graph slicing with ell <= L. Then beta_q >= q 2^(-L/q) >
> q/(2 - 1/q), a new record lower bound for Kakeya sets over F_q (Bukh-Chao 2021 is the
> best published). The available slack, q log2(2 - 1/q) - (q - 1), is 0.211 bits at
> q = 3, 0.229 at q = 4, 0.240 at q = 5, and increases to 1 - 1/(2 ln 2) = 0.2787.

Theorem 49 changes what counts as a win for the next steps. The sharp (*) would give the
exact constant. A *weak* (*), off by up to 0.21 bits at q = 3 (0.27 for large q), would
already be a new record Kakeya bound. What is still missing is the hard part: (*), or weak
(*), for maps with large, spread-out fibres. Nothing here proves a new Kakeya bound yet.

## 1. Lemma 124 and Theorem 48

**Lemma 124.** Let F be a set of m >= 1 points of F_q^k and U uniform on F. Averaged over
the nonzero linear functionals psi, H(psi(U)) <= (1 - p_k) log2 m. Equality holds when
m <= 2.

*Proof.* H(psi(U)) = log2 m - (1/m) sum_{x in F} log2 |F ∩ (x + ker psi)|. Write
|F ∩ (x + ker psi)| = 1 + S_x(psi), where S_x(psi) counts the y in F \ {x} with
psi(y - x) = 0. A nonzero vector w is killed by exactly q^(k-1) - 1 of the q^k - 1 nonzero
functionals, so E_psi S_x = (m - 1) p_k. The function s -> log2(1 + s) is concave with
values 0 at s = 0 and log2 m at s = m - 1. So on {0, ..., m - 1} it lies above the chord:
log2(1 + s) >= s log2(m)/(m - 1). Hence E_psi log2(1 + S_x) >= p_k log2 m, and averaging
over x gives the claim. For m = 2, S_x is 0 or 1 and the chord is exact. ∎

*Proof of Theorem 48.* ell_P(psi) = sum_t sum_F (|F|/q^k) H(psi(U_F)), summed over the
fibres F of Y_t. Average over psi with Lemma 124 and compare with
Lambda(P) = sum_t sum_F (|F|/q^k) log2 |F|. ∎

*Proof of Corollary 48.1.* Some psi lies at or below the average. Also
(q - 1)/(1 - p_k) = (q^k - 1)/q^(k-1) = q - q^(1-k). For the squaring map z -> z^2 of
F_(q^k) (q odd), a168 Proposition 54 gives Lambda = q - q^(1-k) and ell(psi) = q - 1 for
every psi. The same holds for z -> a z^2 + b z with a in F_q^*, whose fibres are
{z, -(b+t)/a - z}. ∎

*Proof of Corollary 48.2.* q^k is odd, so every Y_t has a fibre of odd size. If all fibres
have at most 2 points, every Y_t has a singleton. So H(X | Y_t) <= (q^k - 1)/q^k for every
t, and Lambda <= q - q^(1-k). ∎

*Remark (the inverse problem after Theorem 48).* (*) can only fail at a map P with
Lambda(P) > q - q^(1-k). For odd q, such a map has a fibre with at least 3 points.
Quantitatively, it must have sum_t E_X E_psi log2(1 + S_X) < Lambda(P) - (q - 1). So most
pairs inside fibres must avoid every hyperplane direction about equally. Frame products
are the opposite case: their fibres are cubes, the average is about k(q-1), and a single
coordinate is cheap. Averaging cannot handle them, so a proof of (*) has to combine
Theorem 48 (for isotropic maps) with a structural step (for maps with a preferred
direction).

## 2. Lemma 123: the triangle lemma

*Proof.* Let a = x - y and b = x - z. Then P(x) - P(y) = -t a and P(x) - P(z) = -t' b. So
P(y) - P(z) = t a - t' b, while y - z = b - a. The pair y, z collides at s iff
t a - t' b = -s(b - a), i.e. (t - s) a = (t' - s) b. If a and b are independent this forces
s = t = t'. So when t != t', y and z never collide. If b = mu a, then mu != 0 (z != x) and
mu != 1 (z != y), and the condition becomes t - s = mu (t' - s), i.e.
s = (t - mu t')/(1 - mu). Conversely, this s satisfies the condition. If t = t', the three
points lie in one fibre of Y_t. ∎

*Lines.* On a line x0 + F_q v, two points collide iff P(x) - P(y) lies in span(v), i.e.
iff pi_v(P(x)) = pi_v(P(y)), where pi_v is the projection along v. So the line splits into
*clusters*: the level sets of pi_v ∘ P. Inside a cluster, the collision time of two points
is minus the slope of the remaining coordinate of P, as in the one-dimensional problem. For
t != t', the formula above gives three distinct times on every collinear rainbow triangle.

So the partner graph is a union of cliques, one for each (t, fibre). Two edges at a vertex
with different times close a triangle only along a line. This is the combinatorial shadow
of P(X) + tX that any inverse theorem has to use. The q parallel classes of an affine plane
would give Lambda = q log2 q, which is above 2(q - 1) for every q >= 3. The lemma rules this out, since
their non-collinear triangles would be rainbow.

## 3. Proposition 56: the plane

Let k = 2, v a direction, and nu_v = (v_1, -v_0). Let a_v(x, t) = |F_t(x) ∩ (x + F_q v)|.

*Proof.* H(X | Y_t) = H(nu_v(X) | Y_t) + H(X | Y_t, nu_v(X)). Given Y_t and the line, X is
uniform on F_t(X) ∩ line. So the second term is E log2 a_v(X, t), and summing over t gives
Lambda = ell(nu_v) + Lambda_v. Each y != x in the cluster of x on its v-line collides with x
at exactly one t, so sum_t (a_v(x, t) - 1) = c_v(x) - 1 <= q - 1, where c_v(x) is the size of
that cluster. Since log2 a <= a - 1, Lambda_v <= E(c_v(X) - 1) <= q - 1. The other points
of F_t(x) are split by their direction from x, so prod_v a_v(x, t) >=
1 + sum_v (a_v(x, t) - 1) = |F_t(x)|. Taking logs and summing gives
sum_v Lambda_v >= Lambda. This is an equality when every fibre lies on a line (for example
when all fibres have at most 2 points). For frame products it is strict: a point whose
fibre at time t is a 4-point rectangle sees its 3 partners in 3 directions, so it
contributes 3 bits to sum_v Lambda_v but only log2 4 = 2 bits to Lambda. ∎

Averaging Proposition 56 over the q + 1 directions recovers Theorem 48 for k = 2
(p_2 = 1/(q + 1)). Proposition 56 also shows why C_q(2) and linear (*)_2 stand or fall
together in the plane: if Lambda > 2(q - 1), every direction has ell(nu_v) > q - 1.

## 4. Theorem 49: the record slack

*Proof.* We may assume L >= q - 1, since raising L only weakens the hypothesis.
Theorem 47 (a168) gives Lambda(P) <= ell(f) + E_z Lambda(P'_z), where each P'_z is
a map of F_q^(k-1). By induction from C_q(1) (Lambda <= q - 1 <= L), max Lambda_k <=
(q - 1) + (k - 1) L <= kL. The README's Theorem 2 gives h_k = k log2 q - max Lambda_k/q and
beta_q = lim 2^(h_k/k), so beta_q >= q 2^(-L/q). This exceeds q/(2 - 1/q) iff
L < q log2(2 - 1/q). (README Corollary 2 is the converse: Bukh-Chao gives
lambda* <= q log2(2 - 1/q).) The slack is
q log2(2 - 1/q) - (q - 1) = 1 + q log2(1 - 1/(2q)) = 1 - (1/(2 ln 2))(1 + x/2 + x^2/3 + ...)
with x = 1/(2q). So it increases in q, toward 1 - 1/(2 ln 2). ∎

| q | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 7 | large |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| slack (bits) | 0.170 | 0.211 | 0.229 | 0.240 | 0.252 | 0.2787 |

With Theorem 48, weak (*) at constant L already holds for every map with
Lambda <= L (q^k - 1)/(q^k - q^(k-1)). The open cases are maps with large Lambda and
isotropic fibres.

## 5. Status

- **Proved here (every q unless noted):** Lemma 123, Lemma 124, Theorem 48 with
  Corollary 48.1, Corollary 48.2 (odd q), Proposition 56, Theorem 49.
- **Not proved:** (*) or weak (*) in general. No new Kakeya bound yet.
- **Next:** (i) the pre-registered search (a168 Section 6) for maps with m(P) > 0;
  Corollaries 48.1-48.2 say where to look: Lambda > q - q^(1-k) with fibres of 3 or more
  points. (ii) An inverse step for k = 2: if no direction has
  Lambda_v >= Lambda - (q - 1), use Lemma 123 to bound Lambda. (iii) Weak (*) at q = 3
  with L = 2.21 (Theorem 49).
