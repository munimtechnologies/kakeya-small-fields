## What changes

- **Lemma 68** (by hand). Let u be a Case B element of rank 4 in an anisotropic system with t = 6
  and dim V = 7. If n is an element of U+ whose matrix M_n has rank r = 1, 2 or 3, then n is itself
  an element of rank r, and its map phi_n = beta(n, .) puts it in one of exactly two shapes: x0 in
  phi_n(V) with dim phi_n(V) = r + 2, or x0 outside with dim phi_n(V) = r + 1.
- **Corollary 44.** Each of the four instances that job m25 left (Corollary 43 of a12.md) has
  such an element: q2_t6_i03 one of rank 3, q2_t6_i07 one of rank 1, and q2_t6_i119, q2_t6_i120
  one of rank 2. So q2_t6_i03 is satisfiable only if one of the shapes ("A", 5), ("B", 4) of job
  m27 has a satisfiable instance. Rank 1 and rank 2 lead to four more shapes.
- **Job m28** (`cube_groups/a6_r12.py`) searches those four shapes with the encoding and orbit split
  of job m27 (Lemma 67), unchanged. With job m27 it gives a route to "no Case B at rank 4 and
  t = 6" that does not depend on job m26 deciding the four instances. Where both routes decide an
  instance, they check each other.
- **Job m28 ran** (see "Results of job m28" below): every graded prediction held, but none of its
  102 instances at t = 6 was decided within 2 minutes. The four instances of Corollary 43 remain
  decided only by job m26 ([a13.md](a13.md), all UNSAT).

## Setting

As in [shape-c.md](shape-c.md), Lemma 40: t = 6, dim V = 7, u is in Case B with rank 4, so k = 2,
X_1 = ker A_u = span(x0, e_1), R = Im A_u has dimension 4, P = phi(V) is a hyperplane that misses
x0, Y = P n X_1 = span(e_1), and U+ = ker phi = N = span(u, v) has dimension 2. Since k = 2, beta
vanishes on N, sigma maps N one-to-one onto X_1 (Proposition 27(c)), and every n in N has a matrix
M_n on X/X_1 with A_n x = T M_n [x], where T[x] = A_u x and [x] is the class of x in X/X_1
(Lemma 38). M_u = I, and M = M_v is the matrix of the instance. Shapes are those of
[a14.md](a14.md): ("A", p) means sigma(n) lies in phi_n(V) and dim phi_n(V) = p; ("B", p) means it
does not.

## Lemma 68 (the shape of an element of U+ of rank 1 to 3)

**Lemma 68.** In this setting let n be in N with rank M_n = r, 1 <= r <= 3, and put
P_n = beta(n, V). Then rank A_n = r, and n is in shape ("A", r + 2) or in shape ("B", r + 1).

*Proof.* rank A_n = rank M_n by Lemma 38(a). Also by Lemma 38(a), [beta(n, h)] = M_n [phi(h)] for
every h. Since [P] = X/X_1, the classes of P_n fill Im M_n, which has dimension r, so

    dim P_n = r + dim(P_n n X_1).

Let H = {h : M_n [phi(h)] = 0}, so that P_n n X_1 = beta(n, H). The map h -> [phi(h)] goes from
V onto X/X_1 with kernel phi^(-1)(Y), which has dimension dim N + dim Y = 3; so dim H = 3 + (4 - r)
= 7 - r. beta(n, .) kills N, which lies in H, so dim(P_n n X_1) <= 5 - r. It is also at most
dim X_1 = 2. Hence dim P_n <= r + 2, and if dim P_n = r + 2, then P_n contains all of X_1, and so
it contains sigma(n), which lies in X_1.

Now the count of Lemma 30, as in the proof of Corollary 39 of [a10.md](a10.md) (it does not use
any hypothesis on ranks): n has rank r, so k_n = 6 - r, and dim U+(n) <= 1 + A(5 - r) = 6 - r
(A(s) = s for s <= 5, Theorem 27). Also dim V = codim U+(n) + dim U+(n), and codim U+(n) is p - 1
in shape ("A", p) and p in shape ("B", p). So 7 <= p - 1 + 6 - r in shape A, that is p >= r + 2,
and 7 <= p + 6 - r in shape B, that is p >= r + 1. In shape A this forces p = r + 2. In shape B,
p = r + 2 is impossible, because then sigma(n) lies in P_n, so p = r + 1. QED

For r = 3 the two shapes are ("A", 5) and ("B", 4), which are the configurations (A4) and (B4) of
Corollary 39. Job m27 searches them, and its encoding (Lemma 67) uses only sigma(u), phi and the
pair (ker A_u, Im A_u), not the hypothesis "no element of rank 4" of Corollary 39. For r = 4
(M_n invertible), Lemma 38(c) already says that n is in Case B, which is shape ("B", 5).

## Corollary 44 (the four instances of Corollary 43)

The bit masks of the instances are decoded as in shape_c.py (entry (r, c) of a 4 x 4 matrix at
bit 4r + c; ranks do not depend on whether rows or columns are meant):

    instance     M      M                      rank M   rank (I + M)   element of rank 1 to 3
    q2_t6_i03    1      single entry (0, 0)     1        3              v (rank 1), u + v (rank 3)
    q2_t6_i07    2      single entry (0, 1)     1        4              v (rank 1)
    q2_t6_i119   4686   rows 0111 0010 0100 1000  4      2              u + v (rank 2)
    q2_t6_i120   4686   (the same)              4        2              u + v (rank 2)

For 4686 the rows of I + M are 1111, 0110, 0110 and 1001. The second and third rows are equal, and
the first is the sum of the second and the fourth, so the rank is 2. M itself is invertible: its
last row is 1000, and the other three are independent on the last three coordinates.

**Corollary 44.** Suppose an anisotropic system with t = 6 and dim V = 7 has an element of rank 4
in Case B. Then it has an element of rank 3 in shape ("A", 5) or ("B", 4), or an element of rank 1
in shape ("A", 3) or ("B", 2), or an element of rank 2 in shape ("A", 4) or ("B", 3). More
precisely, the model of q2_t6_i03 has the first kind (n = u + v), and it also has the second
(n = v). q2_t6_i07 has the second (n = v), and q2_t6_i119 and q2_t6_i120 have the third (n = u + v).

*Proof.* By Corollary 43 one of the four instances is satisfiable. Apply Lemma 68 to the element
listed in the table: M_v = M and M_(u+v) = I + M (Lemma 38(a), linearity). QED

So, once job m27 finds every instance of ("A", 5) and ("B", 4) UNSAT and job m28 finds every
instance of its four shapes UNSAT, Case B at rank 4 and t = 6 is ruled out, whatever job m26
reports. It also works the other way: a SAT answer of m26 for q2_t6_i07 must come with a SAT
instance of ("A", 3) or ("B", 2) in m28, and so on. A mismatch would mean an error in Lemma 68 or in
the code. The same argument applies to every pair (M, q) of Lemma 65 in which M or I + M is
singular. For the 142 instances that job m25 already found UNSAT, it would give a second proof.

## Job m28

`cube_groups/a6_r12.py` imports `a6_r3.py` of job m27 and reuses its normal form, encoding, group,
child runner and brute-force check of any SAT answer, unchanged. Two things are new. The four
shapes and their rank come from Lemma 68: ("A", 3) and ("B", 2) at rank 1, ("A", 4) and ("B", 3) at
rank 2, all at t = 6 and dim V = 7. And the subspaces are listed by adding one vector at a time,
because a6_r3.subspaces would try about 7 million 5-tuples for the 5-dimensional kernels of rank 1.
The selfcheck compares the two listings on five cases. The pairs (ker A_u, Im A_u) number
31 x 63 = 1,953 at rank 1 and 155 x 651 = 100,905 at rank 2. The job runs, on one CPU:

1. the unit tests; the selfcheck (subspace counts, group orders 2,064,384, 516,096, 2,064,384 and
   258,048, the generator counts 19, 17, 19 and 16, orbit sizes adding up and dividing |G|, the
   hand orbit sizes of m27 at t = 2 and t = 4);
2. the controls of m27 at t = 2 (Lemma 63, both SAT and UNSAT known) and t = 4 (UNSAT, A(4) = 4),
   through this module's orbit code;
3. ("B", 2) and ("A", 3) with 15 minutes each, then ("B", 3) and ("A", 4) with 50 minutes each,
   2 minutes per instance;
4. the grade, which also reports which of q2_t6_i07, q2_t6_i119 and q2_t6_i120 are ruled out.

The predictions are in `m28/predictions.md`, written before the job was queued. Graded: the unit
tests, the selfcheck, the controls, the check of any SAT answer, and the cross-check with m26 above.
Exploratory: the orbit counts, and the guess that the rank-1 shapes are all UNSAT within 2 minutes
per instance.

## Results of job m28 (ran 2026-10-19, 03:18Z to 05:58Z, one CPU, 1 GB)

**Graded: 47 of 47 checks held.** The unit tests passed; the selfcheck was all true (subspace
counts, the two subspace listings equal on five cases, the four group orders, the generator counts,
orbit sizes adding up to 1,953 and 100,905 and dividing |G|, the hand orbit sizes of m27 at t = 2
and t = 4); the 5 control instances at t = 2 came out as Lemma 63 says (the 3 SAT models verified
by brute force and in normal form) and the 38 control instances at t = 4 were all UNSAT within
0.3 to 0.8 s each (A(4) = 4). No instance at t = 6 was SAT, so the cross-check with job m26 (all
four of its instances UNSAT) is consistent.

**Exploratory.** The orbit counts were inside the guessed ranges: 9 orbits for ("A", 3), 12 for
("B", 2) (guess: under 40 each), 32 for ("A", 4), 49 for ("B", 3) (guess: 30 to 150 each). The
guess that every rank-1 instance would be UNSAT within 2 minutes was **wrong**: nothing at t = 6
was decided.

So this job rules out none of q2_t6_i07, q2_t6_i119 and q2_t6_i120 on its own; they stay decided

**Two things worth recording.** First, time. Job m26 decided the whole instance q2_t6_i07 (the
shape_c2 encoding, with the rank-4 element u and M fixed; 14,724 variables, 84,175 clauses) as UNSAT
in 740 s. The rank-1 instances here fix much less (only sigma(n), phi_n and the pair
(ker A_n, Im A_n) of the rank-1 element, so they also cover systems with no rank-4 element at all)
and got 120 s each. The lesson of jobs m23 and m26, that leftover instances want ten times the
budget before a new split, applies again: job m31 ([a16.md](a16.md)) tries 12 minutes per instance
on the smallest rank-3 shape first. Second, wall clocks. Between about 05:15Z and 05:57Z the host
stalled: instances r2_t6_A4_o009, o011, o014 and o015 reported 537, 208, 179 and 2,313 s against a
120 s budget, which used up the ("A", 4) group budget and made 16 instances SKIPPED (job m29, which
ran at the same time, lost the same 37 minutes in its ("B", 4) group). The budgets in
`a6_r3.run_group` are wall-clock, so a stall counts against them; the results of the stalled
instances are UNKNOWN like the others and carry no information either way.
