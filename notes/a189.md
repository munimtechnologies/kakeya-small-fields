1. **Proposition 84.** pi_3 = 512 and sigma_3 = 24. So every full sticky tree of height <= 3 has

2. **Beam (heuristic).** Over 38 restarts and about 51,000 evaluated trees per height and restart,

## 1. Proof of Proposition 84 (computer-assisted, exhaustive)

*Height 2 is exact.* `sticky_pi3.py` builds all 27^4 = 531,441 full trees of height 2 and records
their slice-size vectors (|S_0|, |S_1|, |S_2|). There are 96 distinct vectors and 19
coordinatewise-minimal ones:

(1,9,9), (2,6,6), (3,3,9), (3,4,8), (3,5,5), (3,8,4), (3,9,3), (4,3,8), (4,4,4), (4,8,3),
(5,3,5), (5,5,3), (6,2,6), (6,6,2), (8,3,4), (8,4,3), (9,1,9), (9,3,3), (9,9,1).

*Height 3 by relaxation (Lemma 163).* A node of height 3 with root map g and children T_0, T_1,
T_2 has slice t equal, plane by plane, to the union of the children's slices t that land in
that plane (a188, Section 3). A union is at least as large as its largest member, so

  |S_t| >= sum over the values u of max { |S_t(T_x)| : g(x) + t x = u }.

The right side is monotone in the children's vectors, so it suffices to let each child run over
the 19 minimal vectors. Taking all 27 root maps and all 19^3 child triples, the least product is
512 and the least sum is 24. The classical tree reaches 512 and 24, so pi_3 = 512 and
sigma_3 = 24. ∎

The relaxation attains 512 not only at the classical shape. Root (0,1,1) with children
(2,6,6), (6,2,6), (6,6,2) also gives sizes (8,8,8). We have not checked whether that shape is
realised by real trees with the same sizes; it would give a non-classical tree of product 512.

| k | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| pi_k | 8 | 64 | **512** | open, in [3456, 4096] |
| sigma_k | 6 | 12 | **24** | open |
| 8^k | 8 | 64 | 512 | 4096 |

(The lower end 3456 = (27/4) · 512 is Lemma 162 with pi_3 = 512.)

## 2. What the beam saw

At every height from 1 to 10, every restart's best tree had slice sizes exactly (2^k, 2^k, 2^k),
the classical product. Lopsided base trees were kept alive by 28 weight vectors, but none of
them led to a cheaper tree at the next height. This matches a188's reading of Lemma 162: a
certificate needs quadratic nodes whose two plane-sharing children are nested (their union close
to their maximum) while having different shapes, and random or greedy recombination does not
find such pairs.

## 3. Next: pi_4 by the same relaxation (pre-registered, not yet run)

The same argument one level higher gives a proved lower bound for pi_4. Keep the real height-2
vectors, relax unions to maxima at heights 3 and 4, take the minimal relaxed height-3 vectors
(M3), and minimise over all ordered child triples from M3 under quadratic roots. Affine roots
cost at least 9 · 512 = 4608 > 4096 (Lemma 162). If the minimum is 4096, then pi_4 = 4096 and
certificates for S7 need height at least 5. If it is below 4096, the relaxation is too weak at
height 4, and its minimiser names the child shapes that a real certificate would have to
realise, which turns the blind beam into a targeted search. The rules are fixed in
`notes/prereg-sticky-pi4.md` before any run.

The relaxation must fail at some height, because the max-game has base below 2 (a185,
Theorem 73). So this method can push the "no certificate below height h" bound only so far.
Deciding S7 needs either a real tree or an argument that uses union structure.
