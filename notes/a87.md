## Lemma 97 (the instances with d = 3)

Setting of Lemmas 95 and 96 ([a85.md](a85.md), [a86.md](a86.md)): t = 8, dim V = 11, u of rank 4
in Case B, shape (c), in the normal form of Lemma 37 with X_1 = span(e_0, ..., e_3) = ker A_u, R =
Im A_u, T : X/X_1 -> R the isomorphism [x] -> A_u x, the matrices M_i = M_(v_i) on X/X_1 in the
basis [e_4], ..., [e_7], fixed by Lemma 96 to a basis (B_1, B_2, B_3) of a representative of the
orbit of W, and D = T^(-1)(X_1 n R) of dimension d in {3, 4}. Write vectors of X/X_1 as bit masks,
bit l for [e_(4+l)], and a row vector f as a mask the same way.

**Lemma 97.** If d = 3, then D = ker f for a non-zero f with f B_i in {0, f} for i = 1, 2, 3. With
l0 the lowest set bit of f, the vectors e_l + f_l e_l0 (l != l0) are a basis of D, e_l0 is not in
D, and

    (D)   rows 4 to 7 of A_u (sum over l of v_l e_(4+l)) are 0 for each of these three v,
    (O)   rows 4 to 7 of A_u e_(4+l0) are not all 0,
    (inv) no non-empty set of the columns A_u e_4, ..., A_u e_7 adds up to 0,

together with (S) and (a) of Lemma 38(a) as in job m94. Conversely, (D) and (O) imply
D = T^(-1)(X_1 n R).

*Proof.* d = 3 makes D a hyperplane, so D = ker f for one f != 0. By Lemma 95(d) every M in W maps D
into itself, so f M vanishes on D, that is f M is in span(f) = {0, f}; this holds for each B_i, as
they lie in W. Each e_l + f_l e_l0 is in ker f (f_l + f_l f_l0 = 0, as f_l0 = 1), the three are
independent, and f e_l0 = 1. A vector [x] is in D exactly when A_u x lies in X_1, that is when rows
4 to 7 of A_u x are 0: this gives (D) and, as e_l0 is not in D, (O). (inv) says T is one-to-one,
which holds since ker A_u = X_1. (S) and (a) are Lemma 38(a), A_n = T M_n T^(-1) A_u and
[beta(n, h)] = M_n [phi(h)], read in the normal form, exactly as for m94. For the converse,
T^(-1)(X_1 n R) is a subspace that contains D by (D) and misses e_l0 by (O), so it is D. QED

So the case d = 3 at (8, 11) is covered by one CNF per orbit of W with an invariant hyperplane (73
orbits, by m93) and per f with f B_i in {0, f}: m94's CNF with its 16 units of (N4) replaced by
(D) and (O), and its one-to-one clauses taken over all eight rows (in (N4) the columns of T lie in
X_1, so rows 0 to 3 were enough there; with d = 3 they need not). Types (N3) and (F3) are not told
apart; both have d = 3, so both are covered. The q(y) of Lemma 95(d) and the singularity of
Lemma 94(b) are not encoded; leaving out true conditions cannot turn a satisfiable case into an
UNSAT one.

What Lemma 97 uses: Lemmas 95 and 96 (so Corollary 121, Lemmas 38, 39 and 94 and, through them,
Theorem 31) and Lemma 38(a). It does not use Theorem 33.

## Limits

- A VERIFIED certificate covers one CNF only. The encoding (m57.py on sat_aniso.py), the hints,
  Lemma 93's unit clauses, the orbit representatives of Propositions 30 to 34 and the reductions
  are code and hand arguments that no human has checked.
- Theorem 33 stays computer-assisted. It rests on many thousands of solver answers, of which 2268
  now carry checked certificates.
- Job m94's UNSAT is one solver answer without a certificate, for one of the 23 orbits of (N4);
  it rests on Lemmas 38, 39, 94, 95 and 96 and on m93's orbit list. Nothing about A(8) is proved
  yet. Lemma 97 is a proof by hand with no computer check of its own.
- No human mathematician has checked Theorem 33 or any lemma it uses. Until someone does, read it
  as a claim with its evidence, not as an established result.
