## 0. Summary

Program (a159-a174): for a map P of F_q^k, Lambda(P) = sum_t H(X | P(X) + tX) (bits). By
Theorem 2 (README) the Kakeya growth constant of F_q is beta_q = q 2^(-lambda*/q) with
lambda* = sup_k max_P Lambda(P)/k. The conjecture C_q(k), Lambda <= k(q - 1), would give the
exact constant. For the **frame** algebra (the algebra of the extremal frame products) a173-a174
reduced this to

    SR* for frames:  H(M) := H(Y | Y o Y + M Y) <= k(1 - 1/q)   for every M in F_q^(k x k),

where (y o y)_i = y_i^2, Y is uniform on F_q^k and q is odd. a174 proved it when every strongly
connected component of M has at most K0(q) vertices (K0(3) = 14), and for M of rank one plus
a diagonal. This note works with any rank. Throughout, M = U V^T + (diagonal) with
U, V in F_q^(k x r), tau = V^T Y, S = Y o Y, theta = (q + 1)/(2q), and for a k x r matrix A

    W_A(x) = sum over c in F_q^r of x^(w(A c))     (w = Hamming weight),

which is the weight enumerator of the code A F_q^r when A has full column rank.

1. **An exact rank identity (Proposition 68).** For every map Q and every U, V,
   H(Y | Q(Y) + U V^T Y) = H(Y | Q(Y)) - [H(tau | Q(Y)) - H(tau | Q(Y) + U V^T Y)]. For frames
   this says H(M) = k(1 - 1/q) - Delta with Delta = H(tau | S) - H(tau | F). **SR* for M is
   exactly the statement that the r-dimensional statistic V^T Y is at least as uncertain given
   the squares as given the output.**
2. **Two-code criterion (Theorem 60).** Bounding H(tau | F) with a compatibility count
   (Lemma 133) and H(tau | S) with an exact sign-Fourier identity (Lemma 134,
   E_S |E[omega^(aY) | S]|^2 = 1/2 for every a != 0 and every odd q) gives

       H(M) <= k(1 - 1/q) - r log2 q + log2 W_U(theta) + log2 W_V(1/2).

   The column code may be **anything**: W_U(theta) <= a_q^r with a_q = (q^2 + 2q - 1)/(2q)
   (Corollary 60.1). So SR* holds whenever the row code of M satisfies
   W_V(1/2) <= (q/a_q)^r ((9/7)^r at q = 3). In particular it holds whenever r >= 2 and the
   row space of M has minimum distance at least r log2 a_q + 2 (1.23r + 2 at q = 3), in
   **every dimension** k (Corollary 60.2).
3. **A strongly connected example beyond a174 (Proposition 69).** An explicit rank-11 M on
   F_3^28 with a strongly connected digraph. Corollary 58.1 does not apply to it (k > 14), and
   Theorem 59 provably cannot certify it for any choice of J. Theorem 60 certifies it with at
   least one bit to spare.
4. **Matching lower bound and exact deficit (Theorem 61).** For every odd q (not only prime q),
   H(M) >= k(1 - 1/q) - H(tau | S) >= k(1 - 1/q) - r log2 q, which improves Proposition 66
   (q - 1, q prime) to log2 q for rank one. For the all-c family M = cJ the deficit is now known
   up to an exponentially small error: k(1 - 1/q) - H(cJ) lies between
   log2 q - log2(1 + (q-1)theta^k) - log2(1 + (q-1)2^(-k)) and log2 q. For the Kakeya map
   x o x + cJx this gives **Lambda = k(q - 1) - q log2 q + O(q theta^k)**. That proves the
   heuristic "deficit about q log2 q" stated in a174.

**Limits.** Theorem 60 is a criterion, not a proof of SR* for all frames. It is empty when the
rank is close to k (at r = k the identity is a tautology) and when both codes have many light
words. The open frame case is now **high rank (r close to k) with large strongly connected
components**. For random codes Theorem 59 is heuristically stronger than Theorem 60. The new
criterion's reach is different: column codes with many light words, as in Proposition 69. As
before, **this is not a new Kakeya bound**, and quadratic maps are only a test class for
beta_q.

## 1. The rank identity

> **Proposition 68 (rank identity).** Let Q : F_q^k -> F_q^k be any map, U, V in F_q^(k x r)
> any matrices, tau = V^T Y and F = Q(Y) + U tau. Then
>
>     H(Y | F) = H(Y | Q(Y)) + H(tau | F) - H(tau | Q(Y)).
>
> For frames (Q = S, H(Y | S) = k(1 - 1/q)) and M = U V^T + D with D diagonal:
>
>     H(M) = k(1 - 1/q) - Delta(M),   Delta(M) = H(tau | S) - H(tau | F),
>
> and SR* for M is equivalent to Delta(M) >= 0.

*Proof.* tau is a function of Y, so H(Y | F) = H(Y, tau | F) = H(tau | F) + H(Y | F, tau). The
pairs (F, tau) and (Q(Y), tau) determine each other (F = Q + U tau), so
H(Y | F, tau) = H(Y | Q, tau) = H(Y | Q) - H(tau | Q), again because tau is a function of Y.
For frames, H(Y | S) = sum_i P(Y_i != 0) = k(1 - 1/q). A diagonal changes nothing
(a174, Proposition 67(a)). []

For r = k and V = I the identity is a tautology. It has content when r < k: it compares the
uncertainty of the r linear statistics V^T Y under two observations, the squares and the
output. Equivalently H(F) >= H(S): adding M Y to the squares never loses information. This
form is what Theorems 60 and 61 use.

## 2. The two lemmas

> **Lemma 133 (compatibility count).** For every U, V,
>
>     H(tau | F) <= log2 E K(Y) <= log2 sum_{c in F_q^r} prod_{i : (Uc)_i != 0} (q + chi(-(Uc)_i))/(2q)
>                <= log2 W_U(theta),
>
> where K(y) = #{tau(z) : F(z) = F(y)} and chi is the quadratic character (chi(0) = 0).

*Proof.* H(tau | F = f) <= log2 of the support size, and Jensen. If tau' != tau(y) occurs in
the fibre of y, say tau' = tau(z), then S(z) = S(y) + U(tau(y) - tau'), so c = tau(y) - tau'
is nonzero and S(y) + U c has every coordinate a square or 0. The map tau' -> c is injective.
The coordinates of S(Y) are independent, and for a != 0,

    #{y in F_q : y^2 + a is a square or 0} = (q + chi(-a))/2,

because z^2 - y^2 = a has q - 1 solutions (z, y) (put u = z - y != 0, then z + y = a/u). A y
with y^2 + a a nonzero square gives two of them, and each of the 1 + chi(-a) values of y with
y^2 = -a gives one (z = 0). So the count is (q - 1 + 1 + chi(-a))/2. Each factor is at most
theta. []

> **Lemma 134 (signs are half-informative in Fourier).** For every odd q and every a != 0 in
> F_q, with Y uniform on F_q and omega = e^(2 pi i/q),
>
>     E_S |E[omega^(aY) | S]|^2 = 1/2,   S = Y^2.
>
> Consequently, for every V in F_q^(k x r), E_S sum_x P(tau = x | S)^2 = q^(-r) W_V(1/2) exactly, and
>
>     H(tau | S) >= r log2 q - log2 W_V(1/2).

*Proof.* Let Y' be a second root of S, chosen independently given S. Then
E_S |E[omega^(aY) | S]|^2 = E omega^(a(Y - Y')). With probability 1/q, Y = 0 and the term is 1.
Otherwise Y' = Y or Y' = -Y with probability 1/2 each. The first case gives 1. The second gives
E omega^(2aY) over Y uniform on F_q^*, which is -1/(q - 1). Total:
1/q + ((q-1)/q)(1/2 - 1/(2(q-1))) = 1/2. Given S, the coordinates of Y are independent, so
the Fourier coefficient of tau | S at xi is a product over i of these one-variable
coefficients at a = (V xi)_i. By Parseval, sum_x P(tau = x | S)^2 = q^(-r) sum_xi |coefficient|^2.
Averaging over the independent S_i gives prod_i 2^(-[(V xi)_i != 0]) = 2^(-w(V xi)). Finally
Shannon entropy is at least collision entropy, and -log2 is convex (Jensen over S). []

## 3. The criterion

> **Theorem 60 (two-code criterion).** For every odd q, every k, and every M = U V^T + D
> (U, V in F_q^(k x r), D diagonal),
>
>     H(M) <= k(1 - 1/q) - r log2 q + log2 W_U(theta) + log2 W_V(1/2).
>
> So SR* holds for M whenever W_U(theta) W_V(1/2) <= q^r.

*Proof.* Proposition 68 with Lemmas 133 and 134. []

No rank or support condition is needed. If U or V has dependent columns the bound is still
true, only weaker. When U and V have full column rank, W_U and W_V are the weight enumerators of
the **column code** C = col(U V^T) and the **row code** C' = row(U V^T), and the bound does not
depend on the factorization.

> **Corollary 60.1 (any column code).** If U has full column rank r, then
> W_U(theta) <= a_q^r with a_q = 1 + (q - 1) theta = (q^2 + 2q - 1)/(2q), with equality for the
> coordinate code. Hence SR* holds for M whenever W_V(1/2) <= (q/a_q)^r. Here
> q/a_q = 2q^2/(q^2 + 2q - 1) is 9/7 at q = 3, 25/17 at q = 5, and tends to 2.

*Proof.* Pick r rows I with U_I invertible. Then w(Uc) >= w(U_I c), theta <= 1, and
c -> U_I c is a bijection of F_q^r. So W_U(theta) <= sum_x theta^(w(x)) = (1 + (q-1)theta)^r. []

> **Corollary 60.2 (minimum distance, every dimension).** Let r >= 2, let V have full column
> rank, and let the row code have minimum distance d >= r log2 a_q + 2 (at q = 3:
> d >= 1.2224 r + 2). Then SR* holds for M = U V^T + D, for every U and every D, in every
> dimension k.

*Proof.* W_V(1/2) <= 1 + (q^r - 1) 2^(-d) < 1 + (q/a_q)^r / 4. This is at most (q/a_q)^r as soon
as (q/a_q)^r >= 4/3. That holds for r >= 2 because q/a_q is increasing in q and (9/7)^2 > 4/3.
Then use Corollary 60.1. []

By the Singleton bound this needs k >= r(1 + log2 a_q) + 1 (k >= 2.23r + 1 at q = 3). For a
random code (uniform random V) the expected value of W_V(1/2) - 1 is at most
(q^r - 1) theta^k, because a uniform vector of F_q^k has E 2^(-w) = ((1 + (q-1)/2)/q)^k = theta^k.
At q = 3 this is (3^r - 1)(2/3)^k, so the criterion is typically met up to r of about 0.48k.
This heuristic is not used anywhere.

> **Proposition 69 (a certificate beyond a174).** At q = 3, r = 11, k = 28, let U have rows
> e_0, ..., e_10 followed by 17 copies of e_0. Let V be the 28 x 11 matrix whose entries, row by
> row, are the outputs of the 64-bit LCG in `twocodes.lcg(seed)` reduced mod 3, for the first
> seed s >= 1 (s <= 10) with the properties below. Then M = U V^T has rank 11 and its digraph
> is strongly connected. Also W_U(theta) = (1 + 2 theta^18)(1 + 2 theta)^10 and
> 2 W_U(theta) W_V(1/2) <= 3^11, all in exact arithmetic. Hence
> **H(M) <= 28 * 2/3 - 1 = 17.67**, strictly below the SR* bound 18.67. Neither earlier tool
> applies: Corollary 58.1 needs k <= 14, and Theorem 59 (with this factorization) gives a
> bound above 18.70 for **every** admissible J.

*Proof.* The checks are exact computations in `test_twocodes.py`: rank, strong connectivity,
the enumeration of all 3^11 codewords of each code, and rational arithmetic. For Theorem 59:
the codewords U c with c_0 = 0 are supported on coordinates 1..10. Each nonzero entry
contributes a factor 1/q or theta >= 1/q, so B_J >= (2 - 1/q)^10 - 1 for every J. That makes
Theorem 59's bound at least 17 * 2/3 + 10 log2(5/3) = 18.703 > 18.667. []

The column code here has many light words (the coordinates 1..10 carry a copy of F_3^10),
which is exactly what Theorem 59 cannot absorb. The row code is a random [28, 11] ternary code.

## 4. The lower bound and the all-c family

> **Theorem 61 (low rank is near-extremal, every odd q).** For every M = U V^T + D,
>
>     k(1 - 1/q) - r log2 q  <=  k(1 - 1/q) - H(V^T Y | Y o Y)  <=  H(M).
>
> In particular, every frame of rank r (modulo the diagonal) is within r log2 q bits of the
> SR* bound, and every frame Kakeya map x o x + Lx whose slices have rank <= r has
> Lambda >= k(q - 1) - q r log2 q.

*Proof.* Proposition 68 and H(tau | F) >= 0, H(tau | S) <= log2 q^r. []

For rank one this replaces the q - 1 of Proposition 66 (Cauchy-Davenport, q prime) by log2 q
(1.585 instead of 2 at q = 3), for every odd q.

> **Corollary 61.1 (the all-c family).** For M = cJ (every entry c != 0), U = c 1, V = 1:
>
>     log2 q - log2(1 + (q-1) theta^k) - log2(1 + (q-1) 2^(-k))  <=  k(1 - 1/q) - H(cJ)  <=  log2 q.
>
> Every slice of P(x) = x o x + cJx equals cJ modulo the diagonal, so Lambda(P) = q H(cJ), and
>
>     Lambda(P) = k(q - 1) - q log2 q + O(q theta^k).

So P is never extremal, but its deficit tends to exactly q log2 q, not to a constant times q - 1.
a174 conjectured this heuristically from the zero-sum structure. Here it follows from the two
codes being the repetition code (weights k) on both sides.

## 5. What this says about the program

* SR* for frames can now be phrased as one inequality between two conditional entropies of
  V^T Y (Proposition 68). The difficulty is concentrated in high rank. The method of this note
  is exact on the diagonal (r = 0), sharp to O(theta^k) on the all-c family, and certifies
  strongly connected examples with k > K0 in which the column code has many light words.
* Tools now available for frames: chain rule over strongly connected components, the pair
  count (k <= K0(q)), Theorem 59 (good column code), Theorem 60 (good row code, any column
  code), and Theorem 61 (matching lower bound). **Still open:** M with r close to k and large
  strongly connected components (for example sparse M with many short cycles). Proposition 68
  with a non-diagonal base Q = S + M_0 Y (any Q is allowed) is the natural way to push
  further: it measures exactly what a rank-r perturbation of a known M_0 costs.
* Proposition 68 holds for **every** algebra (Q = y o y in any commutative algebra). The analogue
  of Lemma 134 for an algebra B needs the Fourier transform of the square-class structure of B.
  That is the route to low-rank linear parts for all unital algebras (SR*, a173).
