    G = {g in GL(X) : g e_0 = e_0, g P = P},   |G| = |GL_6(F_2)| = 20158709760 (p = 6),

## Lemma 83 (the pair orbits)

**Lemma 83.** The G-orbits of pairs (X_1, R) with e_0 in X_1, dim X_1 = 4 and dim R = 3 number 13
for p = 6 and 49 for p = 5. For p = 5, 20 of them have X_1 inside H0 (155 such X_1) and 29 do not
(1240 such X_1).

*Proof.* *Step 1 (three kinds of subspace of H0).* A subspace R of H0 lies in P (kind P), or
contains e_0 (kind L: R = L + R' with R' = R n P), or neither (kind graph). In the last case
R' = R n P is a hyperplane of R, S = pi(R) has dim S = dim R, and R = R' + span(e_0 + q) for any q in
S outside R'; so R is the same as the pair (S, R'), R' a hyperplane of S. An element of G that keeps
H0 acts on all three through g_P.

*Step 2 (orbits of a stabiliser).* Let U be a subspace of P of dimension u and Stab(U) its
stabiliser in GL(P). Its orbits on subspaces W of a fixed dimension are told apart by dim(U n W)
alone. The stabiliser of U and S induces on S every element of GL(S) that keeps U n S: write
U + S = (U n S) + U' + S' with U = (U n S) + U' and S = (U n S) + S', and extend such an element of
GL(S) by the identity on U' and on a complement of U + S. So the orbits of Stab(U) on pairs (S, R')
are told apart by j = dim(U n S) and dim(U n R'), which is j - 1 or j: only 0 when j = 0, only
j - 1 when S lies in U, and either value otherwise. For a subspace R of H0 of dimension k this gives,
with p = dim P:

    kind P      one orbit for each value of dim(U n R),  from max(0, u + k - p) to min(u, k)
    kind L      one orbit for each value of dim(U n R'), from max(0, u + k - 1 - p) to min(u, k - 1)
    kind graph  for each j from max(0, u + k - p) to min(u, k): 1 orbit if j = 0 or j = k, else 2

*Step 3 (p = 6).* Here X_1 = L + Y with Y = X_1 n P of dimension 3, and GL(P) is transitive on such
Y, so there is one orbit of X_1. With u = 3, k = 3, p = 6, Step 2 gives 4 orbits of kind P
(dim(Y n R) = 0 to 3), 3 of kind L (0 to 2) and 1 + 2 + 2 + 1 = 6 of kind graph (j = 0 to 3):
13 in all.

*Step 4 (p = 5, the orbits of X_1).* If X_1 lies in H0, then X_1 = L + Y with dim Y = 3, one orbit,
155 of them (the 3-dimensional subspaces of H0 / L). If not, X_1 n H0 = L + Y with dim Y = 2 and
X_1 = L + Y + span(e_6 + h) for some h in H0; the element of G with g_P = I and g e_6 = e_6 + h maps
L + Y + span(e_6) to X_1. So again one orbit, of 1395 - 155 = 1240. Their stabilisers are

    (alpha) X_1 = L + Y,  dim Y = 3:           g_P keeps Y, h in H0 arbitrary;
    (beta)  X_1 = L + Y + span(e_6), dim Y = 2: g_P keeps Y, h in L + Y.

*Step 5 (p = 5, the orbits of R).* If R lies in H0, h plays no part and Step 2 counts the orbits
with U = Y. If not, R0 = R n H0 is a plane and R = R0 + span(e_6 + h0), where h0 in H0 is fixed by R
up to R0. The element (g_P, h) sends R to g R0 + span(e_6 + h + g h0). In (alpha), h is arbitrary,
so the orbit of R is fixed by the orbit of R0. In (beta), h runs over L + Y, so the orbit is fixed by
the orbit of R0 and the class of h0 in H0 / (R0 + L + Y), which is P / (Y + pi(R0)). That class is
either 0 or non-zero, and every non-zero class is reached: the elements g_P that are the identity on
Y + pi(R0) fix R0 and Y pointwise and act on P / (Y + pi(R0)) as any element of its GL. Here
dim(Y + pi(R0)) <= 4 < 5, so each orbit of R0 gives exactly two orbits of R.

    (alpha), u = 3:  R in H0 (k = 3): 3 (kind P: 1 to 3) + 3 (kind L: 0 to 2) + 5 (graph: 2 + 2 + 1) = 11
                     R0 (k = 2):      3 (kind P: 0 to 2) + 2 (kind L: 0 to 1) + 4 (graph: 1 + 2 + 1) = 9
    (beta),  u = 2:  R in H0 (k = 3): 3 (kind P: 0 to 2) + 3 (kind L: 0 to 2) + 5 (graph: 1 + 2 + 2) = 11
                     R0 (k = 2):      3 (kind P: 0 to 2) + 2 (kind L: 0 to 1) + 4 (graph: 1 + 2 + 1),
                                      each orbit of R0 twice                                = 18

So (alpha) has 11 + 9 = 20 orbits, (beta) has 11 + 18 = 29, and p = 5 has 49. QED

**The same count at t = 6.** For p = 5 at t = 6 (dim X_1 = 3, so u = 2 in P of dimension 5) Step 3
gives 3 + 3 + 5 = 11. For p = 4 at t = 6, Steps 4 and 5 give (alpha) u = 2: 9 + 9 = 18, and (beta)
u = 1: 7 + 2 x 7 = 21, so 39. These are the numbers job m27 found by closing the pairs under
generators of G (a14.md), and the selfcheck of m42 recomputes all four.

## Job m42 (queued, not run)

`cube_groups/m42.py` computes, for each shape, the orbits of X_1 (closure under the elementary
transvections in G, which generate it), then for each X_1 the orbits of R under 64 random units of
the algebra of matrices that keep span(e_0), P and X_1, then for each pair the classes of the 168
matrices with kernel X_1 and image R under 64 random units of the algebra that also keeps R. Every
unit is checked to lie in its group, so every computed orbit lies inside a true orbit and the split
is complete either way. At t = 2 and t = 4 the whole group is enumerated and the computed orbits and
classes must equal the exact ones; at t = 6 the class counts must be job m30's 66 and 401.

Each instance is the CNF of sat_aniso.encode for (7, 9) without its echelon form, with 112 unit
clauses (sigma(u) = e_0, A_u = the class representative, phi on the basis) and the linear
conditions that (2) and (3) of [a10.md](a10.md) give for every n in ker phi: A_n x0 = 0, A_n maps
P + span(x0) into R, sigma(n) lies in X_1, and beta(n, n') lies in X_1 (79 conditions in (B6), 111
in (B5)). The controls: the planted system at t = 6 of jobs m32 and m41, with its own hint
conditions, must be SAT; at t = 2 the instance with R = span(e_1) must be SAT and the other two
UNSAT, as in job m30; every instance at t = 4 and dim V = 5 must be UNSAT.

    m42a   selfcheck; controls; (B6), every second class, 110 minutes
    m42b   (B6), the other classes, 140 minutes
    m42c   (B5), every second class, 140 minutes
    m42d   (B5), the other classes, 140 minutes

Within a job each class gets an equal share of the time left, at least 20 seconds and at most 8
minutes, taken round robin over the pair orbits. The predictions and the decision rule were written
before the jobs were queued.

What a result would mean. An instance fixes only u, so if every class of a shape is UNSAT, then no
anisotropic system with t = 7 and dim V = 9 has an element of rank exactly 3 in that shape, whether
or not it has elements of rank 4. With (A5) closed (job m41) and no element of rank 4 (Corollary 52
and job m40), Proposition 29 would then give A(7) <= 8, and with it (13, 8).

## Shape (d2) after m40a

The plain CNF of form d2 fixes sigma(u), phi, the columns of A_u on X_1 = span(e_0, e_1, e_2), and
sigma, beta and part of A on v_1, v_2 (Lemma 37), but leaves the image R of A_u free. That is the
situation in which the t = 6 searches stalled until A_u itself was fixed (jobs m27 and m29 against
m30). Lemma 78 adds what a model must satisfy once R is known: delta = sigma(v_2) lies in R,
beta(v_2, V) = R, and A_(v_2) has rank 1 with image in R. All of these are linear once A_u is fixed.
So the next step for (d2) is the method of m42: base changes g with g e_0 = e_0, g e_1 = e_1,
g e_2 = e_2 and g P = P keep the form, so R can be fixed up to them, and then A_u up to the
stabiliser of R, with Lemma 78 as linear conditions. The orbits of R will be counted by hand
first.

## What does not follow

- Nothing is decided until m40, m41 and m42 run: 7 <= A(7) <= 9.
- Lemma 83 counts orbits of pairs. It does not count the classes of A_u, which m42 computes by
  machine. If the random generators missed part of a stabiliser, there are only more classes, not
  fewer.
- (B6) is the analogue of (B5) at t = 6, which needed 10 to 24 minutes for some classes (jobs m33,
  m34, m36, m37). A first pass with a few minutes per class may leave many of them open.
