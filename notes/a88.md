## The clauses against Lemma 38(a), by hand

Normal form (Lemmas 35 and 37, shape (c)): v_0 = u, v_1, ..., v_(k-1) a basis of N modulo u,
phi(v_(k-1+j)) = e_j for 1 <= j <= t - 1, X_1 = span(e_0, ..., e_(k-1)) = ker A_u, [x] the class of
x in X/X_1 with basis [e_k], ..., [e_(t-1)], T[x] = A_u x, and M_i the matrix of M_(v_i) in that
basis (column j is the image of [e_(k+j)]).

- (S), m94.encode_n4: for each i, j and row r, A_(v_i)[r][k+j] = sum over l with M_i[l][j] = 1 of
  A_u[r][k+l]. Lemma 38(a) says A_(v_i) x = T M_i [x]. With x = e_(k+j), M_i [x] is column j of M_i,
  sum over l of M_i[l][j] [e_(k+l)], and T of it is A_u (sum over l of M_i[l][j] e_(k+l)). Row r of
  that is the sum above. **Agrees.**
- (a): for each i, j and l, row k + l of beta(v_i, v_(k-1+j)) is 1 exactly when j >= k and
  M_i[l][j - k] = 1. Lemma 38(a) says [beta(n, h)] = M_n [phi(h)]. For h = v_(k-1+j),
  phi(h) = e_j. If j < k, e_j lies in X_1, so [phi(h)] = 0 and rows k to t - 1 of beta are 0. If
  j >= k, [e_j] is basis vector j - k, and M_i of it is column j - k of M_i, whose coordinate l is
  row k + l. **Agrees.**
- (N4): rows k to t - 1 of A_u e_c are 0 for c >= k says R = Im A_u lies in X_1, so R = X_1, as
  rank A_u = t - k = k (d = 4). The one-to-one clauses read rows 0 to k - 1 only, which is enough
  because the columns already lie in X_1. **Agrees.**

What the hand lemmas use: Lemma 38(a) comes from (Q) with h_1 = u, h_2 = n, h' = h; (b) from (Q)
with two elements of N; (d) and (5) from (*); Lemma 46 from (*), (Q) and A_h sigma(h) = 0. None of
them uses anisotropy. Anisotropy enters this part of the theory through Proposition 27 (which gives
shape (c) and sigma linear on N), through q(y) != 0 in Lemma 38(e), and through the lemmas that
fix the type and the orbit (Lemmas 94 to 96 and Corollary 121). In type (N4), R = X_1, so the space
L of Lemma 46 is 0 and Lemma 46 gives, without anisotropy, Abar_y = M_(n_y) and M_(n_y) q(y) = 0
for every y in Y. That is already a strong condition. Whether the CNF without anisotropy is UNSAT
because of facts like this, or because of a wrong clause, is what job m96 is for.

## Job m96 (queued)

Job m96 (cube_groups/m96.py, to be committed with its results; its predictions are committed now)
rebuilds m94's and m95's CNFs clause for clause. Its selfcheck compares each with m94.encode_n4 and
with the sizes m94 and m95b recorded. Then it labels every clause after (*) with its hypothesis
group:

    nf      the normal form of Lemma 35 (sigma(u), A_u on X_1, phi on the basis)
    shape   the values of shape (c) (sigma(v_i), beta(v_i, v_j), A_(v_i) on X_1)
    units   (N4), or (D) for d = 3, with (O) as outside
    inv     T one-to-one, one group per set of columns
    lin     (S), one group per column of each A_(v_i)
    a       (a), one group per beta(v_i, v_(k-1+j))
    aniso   the anisotropy clauses

For each instance it then solves: (*) alone (it must have a model; the zero system is one), every
group, every group but anisotropy, and that without one family at a time. If the CNF without
anisotropy is UNSAT, it shrinks the solver's core to a minimal set of groups, one deletion at a
time. Every model found is decoded and checked clause by clause, and for t <= 6 also against (*)
by brute force.

    m96a  (4, 2) with M = N, I + N, E, F; (6, 3) with (N, N^2), (E_00, E_11), (E_00, E_12)
    m96b  (8, 4), m94's pilot, d = 4 and d = 3 with f = 1

The rules, written before the job (cube_groups/m96-predictions.md): a small core whose hypotheses
the hand lemmas all prove from anisotropy explains the failed control. A hand proof of the core's
contradiction then comes next, and only after it do the m95 answers count. A core that uses a
hypothesis the hand lemmas do not give, or a clause that says something else, is a bug: fix it,
re-run m95, count nothing.
