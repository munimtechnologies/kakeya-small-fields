# Which bound next: A(8) <= 10 settles the first open pair and needs three configurations

MV2 of Munim, Inc., 2026-10-23. Added to kakeya-small-fields next to [a82.md](a82.md). It was
researched and written with AI agents working in MV2's name. A planning note with one hand result,

## What changes

- **Three candidate bounds compared.** With Theorem 33 in hand, the next bounds that the count of
  Corollary 120 can use are A(7) = 7, A(8) <= 10 and A(9) <= 12. cube_groups/a83_targets.py runs
  that count (a80_bounds.settled) with each one added, over a <= 15 and b <= 12, and its tests
  check the lists below:

      A(7) = 7      settles (13, 7)                 E(11) drops from 8 to 7
      A(8) <= 10    settles (11, 2) and (14, 10)    E(8) drops from 3 to 2, E(12) from 11 to 10
      A(9) <= 12    settles (11, 3) and (15, 12)    E(9) drops from 4 to 3, E(13) from 13 to 12
      A(7) = 7 and A(8) <= 10 together: (11, 2), (13, 7), (14, 10)

  (11, 2) and (11, 3) are the first open pairs. A(7) = 7 alone settles one pair, and only through
  the count; the remark in the README that it "would carry the argument of Theorem 32 to t = 10"
  is a hope, not a computed gain, and is not counted here.
- **How big each one is.** The counting part of Proposition 29 ([a24.md](a24.md)) works for any
  (t, dim V): Lemma 27(c) makes alpha(V) irreducible, Lemma 30 bounds the ranks and dim U+, and
  Corollary 38 bounds dim P when x0 lies in P. a83_targets.reduction does this count, and gives
  back Proposition 29 at (7, 9) and the configurations behind Theorem 31 at (6, 7) as controls.
  For an element of the largest rank, a counterexample would need:

      A(7) = 7, (t, dim V) = (7, 8): 11 configurations
        rank 5 (tight, k = 2): Case A, Case B
        rank 4: x0 in P with dim P = 6 or 7; x0 outside P with dim P = 5 or 6
        rank 3: x0 in P with dim P = 5 or 6; x0 outside P with dim P = 4, 5 or 6
      A(8) <= 10, (t, dim V) = (8, 11): 3 configurations (Proposition 36)
        rank 4 (tight, k = 4): Case B only
        rank 3: x0 outside P with dim P = 7 or 6
      A(9) <= 12, (t, dim V) = (9, 13): 3 configurations
        rank 4 (tight, k = 5): Case B only
        rank 3: x0 outside P with dim P = 8 or 7

  The count is an upper bound on the work, not a proof: each configuration still has to be ruled
  out, as (A5), (B5) and (B6) were for Theorem 33.
- **The plan: A(8) <= 10 next.** It settles the first open pair, (11, 2), and it has the shape that
  the machinery of a24.md to a80.md was built for: one tight Case B at rank 4, and two rank-3
  configurations with x0 outside P, the analogues of (B6) and (B5). A(7) = 7 has more room at
  every rank (dim V is one below the bound, not at it), so nearly four times as many
  configurations, and it buys less.

## Proposition 36 (the reduction for A(8) <= 10)

**Proposition 36 (by hand).** Let an anisotropic system have t = 8 and dim V = 11. Then:

(a) alpha(V) is irreducible, every non-zero element has rank at most 4, and some element has rank
    3 or 4;

(b) an element u of rank 4 has k = 4 and dim V = t + k - 1. Every A_h has rank at most 4, and u is
    in Case B: P is a hyperplane that does not contain x0, U+ = ker phi has dimension 4, and every
    h in U+ has A_h x0 = 0, sigma(h) in X_1 and Im A_h in R;

(c) if no element has rank 4, then every element u of rank 3 (k = 5) has x0 outside P and is in
    one of

    (B7)_8  P is a hyperplane, and U+ = ker phi has dimension 4;
    (B6)_8  P has dimension 6, and U+ = ker phi has dimension 5.

    In both, every n in ker phi has A_n x0 = 0, sigma(n) in X_1, and A_n maps P + span(x0) into R.

The subscript 8 keeps these apart from (B6) and (B5) at t = 7; the number is codim U+, as there.

*Proof.* Notation as in [a24.md](a24.md): x0 = sigma(u), phi = beta(u, .), P = phi(V),
X_1 = ker A_u, R = Im A_u, rho = rank A_u, k = t - rho, U+ = {h : phi(h) in span(x0)}.

(a) An invariant subspace of dimension d, 0 < d < 8, would give dim V <= M(8 - d) + A(d)
(Lemma 27(c)). With the bounds of Corollary 119 and M(s) <= s + E(s), the largest of these is 9,
at d = 1 (M(7) + A(1) <= 8 + 1) and d = 7 (M(1) + A(7) <= 1 + 8). That is below 11. If every element
had rank <= 2, Lemmas 28 and 29 would give dim V <= 8. An element of rank s gives
dim V <= 8 + A(7 - s) by Lemma 30: that is 10 for s = 5 and 9 for s = 6, an element of rank 7 gives
dim V <= 8, and rank 8 does not occur, since A_h sigma(h) = 0 with sigma(h) != 0 for h != 0.

(b) Lemma 30 for u reads 11 <= 8 + A(3) = 11, so this is the tight case dim V = t + k - 1 with
k = 4. Proposition 26 needs A(s) = s for s <= 3. Its part (d) rules out Case A, since
2 rho = 8 < t + 1 = 9. So u is in Case B, and Proposition 26 (a), (b) and (e) give the rest.

(c) Now the largest rank is 3, so Corollary 38 of [a10.md](a10.md) applies to u. If x0 were in P,
it would give c >= c0 = ceil((9 - 6) / 2) = 2 and dim V <= t - c0 + A(k - 1) = 8 - 2 + A(4) = 10,
which is less than 11. So x0 is not in P, and U+ = ker phi. Lemma 30 gives
dim ker phi <= 1 + A(4) = 5, so dim P = 11 - dim ker phi >= 6, and dim P <= 7 as P != X. These are
(B7)_8 and (B6)_8. The last sentence is the argument of Corollary 39, as in Proposition 29: by (3)
of a24.md, A_n x0 = 0 and A_u sigma(n) = 0, and by (2) with h_1 = n, A_n phi(h) = A_u beta(n, h)
lies in R. QED

The same steps at (9, 13) give its three configurations: rank 4 tight with k = 5 and Case A ruled
out (8 < 10), and at rank 3 c0 = 2 and dim V <= 9 - 2 + A(5) = 12, so x0 is outside P and
dim P = 8 or 7. That is not stated as a proposition here; a83_targets.reduction(9, 13) is the count.

What Proposition 36 uses: A(s) = s for s <= 4 (Theorems 26 and 27) in (b) and (c); in (a), the
terms of Lemma 27(c) for d = 1 and d = 7 use M(7) <= 8 and A(7) <= 8 (Theorem 33); Theorem 29's
hand bound of 9 gives 10 there, still below 11, so (a) does not need the computer-assisted
Theorem 33. The terms for d = 2 and d = 6 use A(6) = M(6) = 6 (Theorem 31).

## What A(8) <= 10 still needs

- **Rank 4 in Case B at t = 8.** Theorem 30 ([a7.md](a7.md)) rules out dim V = 12 at t = 8 by
  hand; there Lemma 30 leaves only rank 3, in the tight case with k = 5, and Lemma 57 forces shape
  (c), which rank 3 cannot have. At dim V = 11 the tight element has rank 4 and k = 4 instead. The
  first step is to read which of Lemmas 57, 58 and 60 carry over to it, and which shapes of
  [case-b-shapes.md](case-b-shapes.md) are left.
- **(B7)_8 and (B6)_8.** The analogue of Lemma 83 (the orbits of (ker A_u, Im A_u) with
  dim ker A_u = 5) and of job m42 (the classes of A_u), then the solver. At t = 7 these steps gave
  68 + 420 classes and, after the second-matrix splits, about 90,000 instances; at t = 8 the
  instances are larger (t = 8, dim V = 11 against t = 7, dim V = 9), and nothing here predicts
  whether the solver will decide them. That is the main risk of the plan.

## Limits

- Proposition 36 is a reduction by hand, in the pattern of Proposition 29, and no human has
  checked it. It proves nothing about A(8) by itself.
- The pairs above come from the count of Corollary 120 only. A bound proved some other way
  (as Theorem 32 was) could settle more.
- a83_targets.reduction lists what Lemma 30 and Corollary 38 leave. Its tests check it against
  Proposition 29 and the setting of Theorem 31, not against an independent derivation.
