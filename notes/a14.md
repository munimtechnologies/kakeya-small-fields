## Setting

As in [a10.md](a10.md): X = F_2^t, sigma : V -> X quadratic with polar form beta, h -> A_h linear,
and A_(h') sigma(h) = (I + A_h) beta(h, h') (*) for all h, h'. For u in V put x0 = sigma(u),
phi = beta(u, .), P = phi(V), p = dim P, X_1 = ker A_u and R = Im A_u. Always x0 lies in X_1
(A_u sigma(u) = 0, Lemma 21(b)). Shape A means x0 in P, shape B means x0 not in P. In Corollary 39,
(A4) is shape A with p = 5, (B4) is shape B with p = 4 and (B5) is shape B with p = 5, all with
t = 6, dim V = 7 and rank 3.

## Lemma 67 (the normal form of phi, and the split by (X_1, R))

**Lemma 67.** Let (sigma, alpha) be a system with dim X = t and dim V = d, and let u have
x0 = sigma(u) != 0 and dim P = p, with d - p >= 1. Then after a change of basis of X (g in GL(X):
sigma -> g sigma, A_h -> g A_h g^(-1)) and a choice of basis h_0 = u, h_1, ..., h_(d-1) of V:

    sigma(u) = e_0,   phi(h_i) = 0 for i < d - p,   phi(h_(d-p+j)) = b_j  (j = 0, ..., p - 1),

where b_0, ..., b_(p-1) = e_0, ..., e_(p-1) in shape A and e_1, ..., e_p in shape B. Let
G = {g in GL(X) : g e_0 = e_0, g P = P}, with P = span(b_j). If a system in this normal form has
ker A_u containing X_1 and Im A_u in R, then for every g in G there is a system in the normal form
with ker A_u containing g X_1 and Im A_u in g R. So the pairs (X_1, R) may be taken up to G, and
it is enough to search one pair from each G-orbit, with the linear conditions A_u X_1 = 0 and
Im A_u in R.

*Proof.* u is in ker phi (beta is alternating), so ker phi has a basis h_0 = u, h_1, ..., h_(d-p-1).
Choose a basis of X that starts with x0 and continues with a basis of P (shape A: x0, then a basis
of P containing x0 completed; shape B: x0, then a basis of P, independent of x0), then complete it.
In these coordinates sigma(u) = e_0 and P = span(b_j); pick h_(d-p+j) with phi(h_(d-p+j)) = b_j.
For g in G, the system g sigma, g A g^(-1) satisfies (*), has the same u, and g sigma(u) = e_0, and its
phi is g phi, with image g P = P. The vectors g b_j are a basis of P (in shape A, with g b_0 = e_0);
writing b_j = sum c_(jl) g b_l and replacing h_(d-p+j) by sum c_(jl) h_(d-p+l) gives the normal form
again, with ker (g A_u g^(-1)) = g ker A_u and Im (g A_u g^(-1)) = g Im A_u. QED

The group has the order of a block-triangular group: in shape A, with diagonal blocks of sizes 1,
p - 1 and t - p, |G| = 2^((t - 1) + (p - 1)(t - p)) |GL_(p-1)| |GL_(t-p)|; in shape B, with blocks
GL_p, 1 and GL_(t-p-1), |G| = 2^((p + 1)(t - p - 1)) |GL_p| |GL_(t-p-1)|. At t = 6: (A4) 10,321,920,
(B4) 645,120, (B5) 9,999,360. The elementary transvections g e_j = e_j + e_i that lie in G
generate it (each diagonal block GL_n(F_2) is generated by its elementary transvections, and so is
the unipotent part), but the search does not depend on this: it takes the closures of the pairs
under these transvections, each of which lies inside one G-orbit, and together they cover every
pair. If they were smaller than the G-orbits there would only be more instances.

At rank 3 and t = 6 there are 155 choices of X_1 (3-spaces through e_0) and 1395 of R, so 216,225
pairs for each shape. The orbit count is not computed by hand; job m27 reports it.

**Hand check (t = 4, shape B with p = 3, rank 2).** X_1 = span(e_0, l) with l a non-zero vector of
P (7 choices, one orbit; the stabiliser of l in G = GL(P) has order 24). Up to that stabiliser, R
is a plane of P through l (3) or not (4), or R is not in P, R n P = m and R = span(m, e_0 + q):
with m = l, q = 0 (1) or q != 0 modulo l (3); with m != l (6 lines), q = 0 (6), q = l (6) or q one of
the two other classes modulo m (12). So 7 orbits, of sizes 7 x (3, 4, 1, 3, 6, 6, 12) =
21, 28, 7, 21, 42, 42, 84, which add up to 7 x 35 = 245. The selfcheck of job m27 compares this with
the machine count.

## What a result would mean

A SAT answer at t = 6 is checked by brute force on all pairs (h, h') and would be a system with
dim V = 7, so A(6) = 7. UNSAT for every orbit of a shape means that no system with t = 6 and
dim V = 7 has an element of rank 3 in that configuration; this does not use the hypothesis "no
element of rank 4" of Corollary 39. If all three shapes come back UNSAT and jobs m25/m26 close
Case B at rank 4, then A(6) = 6, computer-assisted. A partial answer (for example (A4) UNSAT and
(B5) undecided) narrows Corollary 39 to the undecided configurations.

## Results of job m27

Job m27-a6r3-v1 ran on one CPU with 1 GB from 2026-10-18 23:06Z to 2026-10-19 01:10Z (7,465
seconds). Every step exited 0. The grade (out/grade.json) reports all 47 graded checks true and no
ungraded check:

| part | prediction | result |
|---|---|---|
| unit tests | pass | pass |
| selfcheck | subspace counts, group orders, generators in G, totals, divisibility, hand orbits | all true |
| controls, t = 2, ("A", 1) | SAT for both orbits, verified | SAT, SAT, both verified (0.2 s each) |
| controls, t = 2, ("B", 1) | SAT exactly for R = span(e_1) | 3 orbits: UNSAT, SAT (verified), UNSAT, as predicted |
| controls, t = 4, rank 2 | every instance UNSAT | ("A", 3) 12 orbits, ("B", 2) 19 orbits, ("B", 3) 7 orbits: all 38 UNSAT, 0.2 to 0.8 s each |
| t = 6, ("A", 5) = (A4) | not graded (A(6) = 6 predicts UNSAT) | 22 orbits: 19 UNKNOWN at 3 minutes, 3 SKIPPED |
| t = 6, ("B", 4) = (B4) | not graded | 39 orbits: 23 UNKNOWN at 2 minutes, 16 SKIPPED |
| t = 6, ("B", 5) = (B5) | not graded | 11 orbits: all UNKNOWN at 2 minutes |
| any SAT at t = 6 | verified if any | none |

So the orbit counts at t = 6 are 22, 39 and 11 (no hand count was made), and the encoding is
consistent with every known answer at t = 2 and t = 4, but **it decided nothing at t = 6.** The
exploratory guess that (A4) would behave like Case A (seconds per instance, as in job m24) was
wrong: no ("A", 5) instance finished in 3 minutes. Fixing sigma(u), phi and the pair
(ker A_u, Im A_u) leaves too much freedom in the other six maps; in Case A at rank 4 (job m24) the
normal forms of Lemma 61 also fixed A_u itself.

What this changes: nothing is ruled out, and Corollary 44's route to q2_t6_i03 through its element
u + v of rank 3 stays open (its element v of rank 1 is searched by job m28). The next search, job
m29 ([a16.md](a16.md)), runs the same 72 orbits in the same order with the facts of Lemma 69 added:
what (*) and Lemma 64 say about the maps of U+ in the normal form.
