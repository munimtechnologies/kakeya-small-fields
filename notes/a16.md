## Setting

As in [a14.md](a14.md): X = F_2^t, sigma : V -> X quadratic with polar form beta, h -> A_h linear,
and A_(h') sigma(h) = (I + A_h) beta(h, h') (*) for all h, h'. For u in V, x0 = sigma(u),
phi = beta(u, .), P = phi(V), p = dim P, X_1 = ker A_u, R = Im A_u, rho = rank A_u, k = t - rho and
U+ = {h : phi(h) in span(x0)}. The normal form of Lemma 67: sigma(u) = e_0, a basis
h_0 = u, h_1, ..., h_(d-1) of V with phi(h_i) = 0 for i < d - p, and phi(h_(d-p+j)) = b_j, where the
b_j are e_0, ..., e_(p-1) in shape A (x0 in P) and e_1, ..., e_p in shape B (x0 not in P). Put
kd = d - p. Then

    U+ = span(h_0, ..., h_kd) in shape A,     U+ = ker phi = span(h_0, ..., h_(kd - 1)) in shape B,

since phi maps h_(kd+1), ..., h_(d-1) to vectors that are independent modulo span(x0). In shape A,
v = h_kd has phi(v) = x0.

## Lemma 69 (the facts of U+)

**Lemma 69.** Let (sigma, alpha) be anisotropic and in the normal form of Lemma 67 for u, with
X_1 = ker A_u and R = Im A_u. Then:

(a) A_(h_i) e_0 = 0 for 0 < i < kd, and in shape A, A_v e_0 = e_0;

(b) A_(h_i) P lies in R for 0 < i < kd;

(c) in shape A, (I + A_v) P lies in R;

(d) if k is 2 or 3 and dim U+ = k, every A_h with h in U+ maps X_1 into X_1;

(e) under the hypothesis of (d), for some non-empty set S of indices i >= 1 with h_i in U+, the sum
    m = sum of the h_i for i in S has A_m X_1 in span(x0).

*Proof.* (a) is Lemma 30(a), A_h x0 = phi(h) for h in U+ ((3) in [a10.md](a10.md)). (b) is (2) of
a10.md with h_1 = h_i in ker phi: A_u beta(h_i, h) = A_(h_i) phi(h) + A_h phi(h_i) = A_(h_i) phi(h),
which lies in R. (c) is Lemma 62(a) with v = h_kd. (d) is Lemma 64(a) of [a11.md](a11.md). For (e),
Lemma 64 gives an element m' of U+ outside span(u) with A_(m') X_1 in span(x0). Write m' = c h_0 +
m with m a sum of h_i, i >= 1, in U+; m != 0, because m' is not in span(u). A_(m') = c A_u + A_m, and
A_u vanishes on X_1, so A_m X_1 = A_(m') X_1 lies in span(x0). QED

In coordinates every fact says that A_m x + c x lies in a subspace W for a fixed sum m of basis
indices, a fixed vector x, c in {0, 1} and a fixed W (0, R, X_1 or span(e_0)), that is,
dim X - dim W linear equations on the entries of the matrices A_i. Fact (e) is one of
2^(dim U+ - 1) - 1 choices of S; the search encodes it with one selector variable per choice.

(a) to (c) follow from the coefficient equations (L) and (Q) of `cube_groups/sat_aniso.py` with
one index equal to 0, so the solver of job m27 had them in principle, but only through XOR chains that a CDCL
solver does not combine well. (d) and (e) are not consequences of a few equations: their proof
passes to a quotient system of dimension 2 on U+ / span(u) and uses Lemma 63, which rests on the
anisotropy of all of U+.

**The configurations at t = 6.** In (A4) = ("A", 5) and (B4) = ("B", 4), dim U+ = 3 = k (Corollary
39, whose counting uses only dim V = 7 and Lemma 30, not the hypothesis "no element of rank 4"), so
(a) to (e) all apply; in (A4), kd = 2, U+ = span(u, h_1, v), and in (B4), kd = 3, U+ = span(u, h_1,
h_2). In (B5) = ("B", 5), dim U+ = 2 < k and only (a) and (b) apply, with (b) saying that Im A_(h_1)
lies in R (P + span(x0) = X).

**Rank exactly 3.** (d) and (e) use X_1 = ker A_u. An instance of the search asks for A_u X_1 = 0
and Im A_u in R, which a system whose u has rank below 3 can also satisfy; for such a u the facts
need not hold. So an UNSAT answer for every orbit of a shape now means: no anisotropic system with
t = 6 and dim V = 7 has an element of rank exactly 3 in that configuration. That is what Corollary
39 and Corollary 44 ([a15.md](a15.md)) need.

## Job m29

`cube_groups/a6_r3h.py` imports `a6_r3.py` of job m27 unchanged (orbits, encoding, child runner,
brute-force check of any SAT answer) and adds the facts. Instance r3h_t6_r3_A5_o007 is m27's
r3_t6_A5_o007 plus the facts, and so on. Per instance the facts add, at t = 6:

| shape | (a) | (b) | (c) | (d) | conditions | equations | choices in (e) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ("A", 5) | 2 x 6 | 5 x 3 | 5 x 3 | 6 x 3 | 18 | 60 | 3, each 3 x 5 |
| ("B", 4) | 2 x 6 | 8 x 3 | - | 6 x 3 | 16 | 54 | 3, each 3 x 5 |
| ("B", 5) | 1 x 6 | 5 x 3 | - | - | 6 | 21 | none |

The job runs, on one CPU:

1. the unit tests; the selfcheck: a6_r3.py's own selfcheck, the fact counts above and at t = 2 and
   t = 4, the CNF of the facts against a direct check on 400 random cases, and the facts
   themselves, in coordinate-free form, on all 24 anisotropic systems with t = dim V = 2 (Lemma 63:
   216, 180, 36, 36 and 36 tests of (a) to (e), worked out by hand) and on random systems from
   sat_aniso.py;
2. the controls of m27 (t = 2, where both SAT and UNSAT answers are known, and t = 4, all UNSAT by
   A(4) = 4), now with the facts, and one new control: t = 2 at rank 0 in shape ("A", 1), where (d)
   and (e) apply (dim U+ = k = 2) and Lemma 63 (ii) with u = a + b is a model, so the answer must be
   SAT;
3. ("A", 5) with 2.5 minutes per instance (55 in all), ("B", 4) with 1.5 (60 in all), ("B", 5) with
   2 (22 in all);
4. the grade.

What a result would mean: all ("A", 5) and ("B", 4) instances UNSAT rules out (A4) and (B4) for
elements of rank exactly 3, and so rules out q2_t6_i03, the first of the four instances of
Corollary 43, without job m26 (its element u + v has rank 3, Corollary 44). All three shapes UNSAT
rules out rank 3 altogether at t = 6 and dim V = 7, and then A(6) = 6 needs only Case B at rank 4
(job m26, or job m28 with this one). A SAT answer would be checked by brute force and would give
A(6) = 7.

## Results of job m29 (ran 2026-10-19, 03:57Z to 06:20Z, one CPU, 1 GB)

**Graded: 53 of 53 checks held.** The unit tests passed. The selfcheck was all true: a6_r3.py's
own selfcheck, the fact counts of the table (and those at t = 2 and t = 4), the CNF of the facts
agreeing with the direct check on all 400 random cases with both outcomes occurring, the 24
systems at t = dim V = 2 with 216 / 180 / 36 / 36 / 36 tests of (a) to (e), and no fact failing on
any of them or on the random systems. The controls came out as predicted: at t = 2 and rank 1,
("A", 1) SAT for both orbits and ("B", 1) SAT exactly for R = span(e_1); the new control at t = 2
and rank 0, ("A", 1), SAT; every SAT model verified by brute force, in normal form and satisfying
the facts; at t = 4 all 38 instances UNSAT within 0.3 to 0.8 s. No instance at t = 6 was SAT.

**At t = 6 nothing was decided**, as in job m27:

    shape      orbits   run   UNKNOWN            SKIPPED   budget per instance   group
    ("A", 5)     22      22         22               0          2.5 min           55 min
    ("B", 4)     39      10         10              29          1.5 min           60 min
    ("B", 5)     11       8          8               3          2 min             22 min

The exploratory guesses were **wrong** where they were optimistic: not one ("B", 4) instance was
UNSAT within 1.5 minutes (guess: at least half of 39), not one ("A", 5) instance within 2.5 minutes
(guess: at least a third of 22); the guess that ("B", 5) stays mostly UNKNOWN was right for the
wrong reason, since every shape stayed UNKNOWN. So, at a budget of 1.5 to 2.5 minutes, the 60 or 54
linear equations and the three alternatives of Lemma 69 make no visible difference to the solver.
This is a negative result about the search design, not about the mathematics: the facts are true
(the selfcheck tested them on every system at t = dim V = 2 and on random systems), they just do
not shorten the solver's work enough at this budget.

The ("B", 4) group lost about 37 minutes to a host stall between about 05:15Z and 05:57Z (the same
stall as in job m28, [a15.md](a15.md)): instances o003, o005 and o008 reported 486, 236 and 240 s
against a 90 s budget, and about 2,000 s of the group budget passed outside any instance, so 29
instances were SKIPPED instead of about 8. SKIPPED and UNKNOWN answers carry no information
either way.

## Job m31 (the same search with twelve minutes per instance)

Three searches with this encoding (m27, m28, m29) have now run 214 instances at t = 6 with 1.5 to
3 minutes each and decided none. The one search that did decide instances at t = 6 and dim V = 7,
job m26 ([a13.md](a13.md)), needed 7 to 18 minutes each (740 s for q2_t6_i07) with a 35-minute
budget. Before a new split, the cheaper lever is time, as it was for jobs m23 and m26. Job m31
(`m31/predictions.md`) runs `a6_r3h.py` unchanged on the 11 orbits of ("B", 5) only, with 12
minutes per instance and a 140-minute group budget; its graded predictions are those of m29 (unit
tests, selfcheck, controls, verification of any SAT answer, the same 11 instances by name). The
decision rule was written down before it ran: if none of the 11 is decided, the bare (X_1, R)
encoding is abandoned at t = 6 in favour of instances with the matrix A_u fixed (Lemma 70,
[a17.md](a17.md), job m30) and budgets of 30 minutes or more; if most are decided, the same budget
goes to ("A", 5) and ("B", 4) in two or three further jobs. ("B", 5) was chosen because it is the
smallest shape and the one where the hand facts are weakest.
