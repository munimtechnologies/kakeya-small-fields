## Setting

As in [a14.md](a14.md) and [a16.md](a16.md): X = F_2^t, sigma : V -> X quadratic with polar form
beta, h -> A_h linear, and A_(h') sigma(h) = (I + A_h) beta(h, h') (*) for all h, h'. For u in V,
x0 = sigma(u), phi = beta(u, .), P = phi(V), p = dim P, X_1 = ker A_u, R = Im A_u, rho = rank A_u.
The normal form of Lemma 67: sigma(u) = e_0, a basis h_0 = u, h_1, ..., h_(d-1) of V with
phi(h_i) = 0 for i < d - p and phi(h_(d-p+j)) = b_j, the b_j being e_0, ..., e_(p-1) in shape A and
e_1, ..., e_p in shape B; G = {g in GL(X) : g e_0 = e_0, g P = P}.

## Lemma 70 (the matrix A_u up to the stabiliser of the pair)

**Lemma 70.** Let (X_1, R) be a pair with dim X_1 = t - rho, e_0 in X_1, dim R = rho, and let
H = {g in G : g X_1 = X_1, g R = R}. Suppose an anisotropic system in the normal form of Lemma 67
for u has ker A_u = X_1, Im A_u = R and A_u = A. Then for every g in H there is an anisotropic
system in the normal form of Lemma 67 for u, with the same shape and the same dimensions, in which
ker A_u = X_1, Im A_u = R and A_u = g A g^(-1). Consequently, if M(X_1, R) is the set of matrices
with kernel exactly X_1 and image exactly R (there are |GL_rho(F_2)| of them: an ordered basis of R
as the images of a complement of X_1), and M(X_1, R) is partitioned into sets each of which lies
inside one H-orbit, then every anisotropic system with an element of rank exactly rho whose pair
is in the G-orbit of (X_1, R) is conjugate, by an element of G that keeps the normal form, to one
whose A_u is the chosen representative of one of the sets.

*Proof.* As in the proof of Lemma 67: for g in G the system g sigma, g A_h g^(-1) satisfies (*), is
anisotropic, has the same u with g sigma(u) = e_0, and its phi is g phi with image g P = P; after
replacing h_(d-p+j) by the combinations of the h_(d-p+l) that restore phi(h_(d-p+j)) = b_j (u = h_0
is untouched) it is in the normal form again. Its A_u is g A g^(-1), with kernel g X_1 = X_1 and
image g R = R when g is in H. For the consequence: by Lemma 67 the system may be taken in the normal
form with (ker A_u, Im A_u) = (X_1, R); then A_u is in M(X_1, R), so in one of the sets, so in the
H-orbit of that set's representative A', that is, A_u = g A' g^(-1) for some g in H, and
conjugating by g^(-1) gives a system in the normal form with A_u = A'. The facts of Lemma 69 hold
in the conjugate system because they hold in every anisotropic system in the normal form for an
element of rank exactly rho. QED

**How the classes are computed (`a6_r3m.py`).** The pair orbit of (X_1, R) under the transvections
of G is traversed breadth first with a spanning tree; t(x) denotes the product of the tree
generators from the root to the pair x. For a pair x and a generator s, the Schreier generator
t(s x)^(-1) s t(x) fixes the root pair, so lies in H; by Schreier's lemma all of them together
generate H (the transvections generate G: Lemma 67). The program takes all of them when the pair
orbit has at most 400 pairs and otherwise the ones at the root plus 600 random ones (seeded), and
checks each against the root pair. The classes are the closures of M(X_1, R) under conjugation by
the generators found, so each class lies in one H-orbit and the partition is complete either way;
if the sample missed a generator there are only more classes. At t = 2 and t = 4 the group G is
small (orders 2, 1, 192, 168, 48 by Lemma 67's formula; the closure of the transvections is checked
to reach them), so H is enumerated and the exact H-orbits are compared with the classes: they must
agree, and |H| x (orbit size) must be |G|.

## Job m30

`cube_groups/a6_r3m.py` imports `a6_r3.py` (job m27: normal form, pair orbits, encoding,
brute-force check) and `a6_r3h.py` (job m29: the facts of Lemma 69) unchanged. For every pair
orbit of m27 at t = 6, it builds the classes, saves them, and runs one instance per class: m29's
CNF plus 36 unit clauses fixing A_u. The index is the same as m27's: instance
r3m_t6_r3_A5_o007_m003 is pair orbit 7 of ("A", 5), class 3. The instances of a shape run round
robin over the pair orbits (every orbit's first class, then every second class, ...), one minute
each. The group budgets are 50, 55 and 30 minutes for ("A", 5), ("B", 4) and ("B", 5); whatever
is not reached is SKIPPED.

The controls fix A_u, so they ask for rank exactly rho, and two of job m27's control answers
change. At t = 2 and rank 1, shape ("A", 1) was SAT in m27 and m29 because A_u = 0 was allowed.
The systems with t = dim V = 2 (Lemma 63, 24 of them) have no element of rank 1 in shape A: their
36 elements of rank 1 all have phi(V) = span(f), which misses x0. Directly, a rank-1 element with
phi(V) = span(x0) would give A_(h_1) x0 = x0 and sigma(h_1) = x0 from (*), against
A_h sigma(h) = 0. So both instances must now be UNSAT. The others are as before: ("B", 1) at
rank 1 is SAT exactly for R = span(e_1), ("A", 1) at rank 0 is SAT, and every instance at t = 4
is UNSAT.

What a result would mean: if every class of every pair orbit of a shape is UNSAT, no anisotropic
system with t = 6 and dim V = 7 has an element of rank exactly 3 in that configuration. All three
shapes UNSAT, with Corollary 45, gives A(6) = 6, computer-assisted; a verified SAT answer would
give A(6) = 7. A pair orbit is closed when all of its classes are UNSAT here or when job m29's
single instance for it is UNSAT, whichever comes first.

**Corollary 46 (computer-assisted).** Let an anisotropic system have t = 6 and dim V = 7. Then
(i) no element of rank exactly 3 is in the configuration (A4) = ("A", 5); and (ii) no element of
rank exactly 3 is in (B4) = ("B", 4) with its pair (ker A_u, Im A_u) in any of the 37 pair orbits
of job m27 other than o001 and o026.

*Proof.* Take such an element u of rank exactly 3 in shape ("A", 5). By Lemma 67, the system may
be put in the normal form for u with (ker A_u, Im A_u) equal to the representative of one of the
22 pair orbits. By Lemma 70, A_u may then be taken to be the representative of one of that orbit's
classes. The facts of Lemma 69 hold there. So the system is a model of one of the 141 instances
of job m30, and all of them are UNSAT. The same argument with the 37 pair orbits of ("B", 4) all
of whose classes are UNSAT gives (ii). QED

The proof relies on 532 of the SAT solver's UNSAT answers: 141 for (i), and for (ii) the 391
classes of the 37 closed pair orbits. They were saved without proof certificates (cadical195
through python-sat). Like the earlier corollaries at t = 6, it is
computer-assisted, not checked by a human.

Left open by m30: in ("B", 4), class 0 (the only class) of pair orbit 1 and class 8 of pair orbit 26,
both UNKNOWN at one minute. In ("B", 5), 25 classes UNKNOWN at one minute in pair orbits 0, 1, 2,
3, 4, 6, 7 and 8, and 24 SKIPPED in pair orbits 5, 7, 8, 9 and 10. No pair orbit of ("B", 5) is
closed yet.

**The guesses, graded honestly.** "5 to 40 classes per pair orbit, a few hundred in all for
("A", 5) and ("B", 5)" was wrong: 1 to 27 per orbit, 141 and 66. "More for ("B", 4)" was right
(401). "At least 80 percent of the instances that get their minute are decided" was right overall
(557 of 584, 95 percent) and per shape for ("A", 5) (100 percent) and ("B", 4) (99.5 percent), but
wrong for ("B", 5) (17 of 42, 40 percent). "The budgets will probably not reach every class of
("B", 4)" was wrong: all 401 ran, most in under a second. "The last merge falls in the first
three quarters of the sample" was right for every pair orbit. Fixing A_u made the difference that
fixing only (ker A_u, Im A_u) and adding the facts did not (jobs m27 and m29: 0 of 72 at 1.5 to
3 minutes). This matches what job m24 showed at rank 4.

**Two things checked before trusting the fast UNSAT answers.** First, a contradictory encoding
would make every instance die at once. Here the answers took from 0.4 to 53 s, the controls with
the same code include SAT answers at t = 2, and the t = 2 instances also fix A_u. Second, the bit
layout of the unit clauses was checked by reading the code. Entry (row, col) of the fixed matrix
is bit 6 row + col in `a6_r3m.encode_r3m`, in `sat_aniso.decode` and in `sat_aniso.mat_apply`, and
the clauses A_u X_1 = 0 use the same orientation. Even so, every A_u in the t = 2 controls is
symmetric, so a transposed reading would not have been caught by them. Job m32 therefore adds a
control at t = 6 with a non-symmetric A_u (below). If that control fails, Corollary 46 is
withdrawn.

One cosmetic bug: the `stats.class_sizes` field of m30's class files lists 2 for every class,
which is the length of the pair (representative, members), not the class size. The sizes used by
the program and by the selfcheck (`reps`, `sizes_add_up`) are right. For example, pair orbit 0 of
("A", 5) has classes of 96 and 72. The line is fixed in the copy used by jobs m32 to m34; nothing
else changed.

## Jobs m32, m33 and m34 (the 51 open instances, and a positive control at t = 6)

`m32.py` re-runs exactly the 51 open instances with a6_r3m.py. The classes are rebuilt with the
same seeds, and each instance's A_u is compared with the value job m30 used before it runs. The
budgets are 10x to 20x m30's minute, following the lesson of jobs m23 and m26:

    job   instances                                             minutes each   group
    m32   ("B", 4) o001_m000, o026_m008; the 24 SKIPPED of ("B", 5)    20; 3          130 min
    m33   UNKNOWN of ("B", 5) in pair orbits 0 to 4 (11)                12             140 min
    m34   UNKNOWN of ("B", 5) in pair orbits 6 to 8 (14)                10             145 min

**The control at t = 6.** Take three copies of the system of Lemma 63 (ii). Each has
sigma(a) = sigma(b) = E, sigma(a + b) = F, A_a = A_b = the projection onto span(F) along span(E),
and A_(a+b) = 0; (*) holds in each. Their direct sum is an anisotropic system with
t = dim V = 6: (*) holds block by block, and the cross terms of beta vanish. In it,
u = a_1 + a_2 + a_3 has x0 = E_1 + E_2 + E_3 and P = span(F_1, F_2, F_3), which does not contain
x0, so shape ("B", 3). A_u is the sum of the three projections, of rank 3. Lemma 69's facts hold
for it. Written in the normal form of Lemma 67, it is a model of the instance (t, d) = (6, 6),
("B", 3), with A_u = diag(0, 1, 1, 1, 0, 0) fixed. That instance must therefore be SAT, and so
must a second one in which e_4 = E_2 + F_1, where A_u gets an off-diagonal entry. The transpose
of that second matrix has the wrong kernel, so its instance must be UNSAT. The predictions,
written before the jobs were queued, are in `m32/predictions.md`, with a decision rule: if every
class of ("B", 5) is UNSAT (counting m30, m32 to m34 and job m31's per-orbit answers), the A(6) = 6
note follows; if at most 10 stay open, one more job at 40 minutes each; otherwise a split by a
second fixed matrix, designed in a note first.
