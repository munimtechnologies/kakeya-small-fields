   which uses no fronts and none of the sticky-h4r-v1 enumeration, proved lambda_3 = 31 (OPTIMAL in
   1,424 s). With Lemma 167 and Proposition 92 this gives kappa_4 = 31 again, so the a196 tree is optimal
   at height 5 by two separate computations. The same job proved that every height-4 tree whose root map
   is a translate of x^2 has kappa >= 31 (OPTIMAL in 675 s).
2. **Height 6 is undecided.** Neither job decided whether kappa_5 <= 61 (which would beat a196). Known:
   60 <= kappa_5 <= lambda_4 <= 62, and beta_s <= 31^(1/5) = 1.98734 stands.

   exactly instead of the full problem.

## Where height 6 stands

- kappa_5 >= 60 (pi_6 >= 216,000, sticky-h4r-v1) and kappa_5 <= lambda_4 <= 62 ((H, H)).
- A value <= 61 at height 5 would give beta_s <= 61^(1/6) = 1.98407.
- Hand analysis of the nested family that extends a196 (F0 in prereg-sticky-nest.md): the relaxed
  slice-size bookkeeping over the exact E3 front allows 60-61, but the two natural explicit sub-cases
  (fixing Y to a (5, 9, 15) tree; the "cube" variant) both come out at 62, by a column lemma (if a slice
  of a height-2 tree lies in a column, every other slice meets all three columns) and a square lemma
  (no height-2 tree has |S_0| = 3 with S_1 inside a 2 x 2 square). These are hand arguments, not
  computer-checked.
