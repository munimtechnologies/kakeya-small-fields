## Lemma 95 (the space of M_n, and what each type of A_u asks of it)

Setting of Corollary 121 and Lemma 94 ([a84.md](a84.md)): t = 8, dim V = 11, u of rank 4 in Case
B, shape (c), with X_1 = ker A_u, R = Im A_u, N = U+ (dimension 4), Y = P n X_1 (dimension 3),
N_Y = sigma^(-1)(Y) n N (dimension 3), the matrices M_n on X/X_1 = F_2^4 of Lemma 38, the vectors
q(y) = [sigma(h_y)] of Lemma 38(d), and D = T^(-1)(X_1 n R) of dimension d in {3, 4}. Put

    W = { M_n : n in N_Y }.

**Lemma 95.** (a) W is a 3-dimensional space of pairwise commuting 4 x 4 matrices over F_2 that does
not contain I, and y -> M_(n_y) is a linear bijection from Y onto W.

(b) For every g in GL_4(F_2) the system has an element of rank 4 in the normal form of shape (c)
whose space is g^(-1) W g. Each condition in (c) and (d) is a property of W alone that is kept by
conjugation; so the rank-4 case at (8, 11) splits by the type of A_u and the orbit of W under
conjugation.

(c) In type (N4): every matrix of W \ {0} is singular, and one can pick for each M in W \ {0} a
vector q_M != 0 with M q_M = 0 such that the seven q_M span a space of dimension at least 3.

(d) In types (N3) and (F3): D is a hyperplane of F_2^4 with M D in D for every M in W, and the q(y)
span D. In type (F3) moreover every matrix of W \ {0} is singular and the q_M of (c) can be picked
inside D so that they span D.

*Proof.* (a) n -> M_n is linear (Lemma 38(a)) and one-to-one on N (Corollary 121: rank M_n =
rank A_n >= 1 for n != 0), and N_Y has dimension 3, so W has dimension 3; its matrices commute by
Lemma 38(b). sigma is linear on N and maps it onto X_1 (Proposition 27(c1)), so y -> n_y is linear
from Y onto N_Y and y -> M_(n_y) is a linear bijection onto W. If I = M_n with n in N_Y, then
M_(n + u) = M_n + M_u = 0 with n + u != 0 (u is not in N_Y, as sigma(u) = x0 is not in P), which
contradicts injectivity.

(b) Lemma 39 ([shape-c.md](shape-c.md)) with k = 4 and t - k = 4 gives, for every g in
GL_4(F_2) and c in GL_3(F_2), a normal form with matrices M'_a = g^(-1) (sum over b of c_ab M_b) g;
the recombination c does not change the span, so the new space is g^(-1) W g. In the new basis of
X/X_1 every vector v becomes g^(-1) v, so M' q' = g^(-1) M q and the q_M and D of (c) and (d)
become g^(-1) q_M and g^(-1) D; the conditions are stated with existential quantifiers over
vectors and hyperplanes, so they hold for W exactly when they hold for g^(-1) W g.

(c) In (N4), Lemma 94(b) gives M_(n_y) q(y) = 0 for every y in Y, and q(y) != 0 for y != 0
(Lemma 38(e)); so every M = M_(n_y) in W \ {0} is singular, and q_M = q(y) is a vector of its
kernel (by (a), each M in W \ {0} is M_(n_y) for exactly one y != 0). By Lemma 38(e), which uses
A(s) = s for s <= 3 (Theorem 26), the q(y) span a space of dimension at least k - 1 = 3.

(d) For d = 3, D has dimension 3. By Lemma 38(e), every q(y) lies in D and the q(y) span a subspace
of D of dimension at least 3, so they span D; and M_n q(y) lies in D for all n in N and y in Y, so
M_n D lies in D. In (F3), Lemma 94(b) adds M_(n_y) q(y) = 0, so q_M = q(y) lies in ker M n D, and
these vectors span D. QED

What Lemma 95 uses: Corollary 121 and Lemma 94 (so Proposition 28, Lemma 60, Corollary 35,
Theorems 29 and 31, and through Theorem 31 the 775 solver answers at t = 6, [a23.md](a23.md)),
Lemmas 38 and 39, Proposition 27 and Theorem 26. It does not use Theorem 33.

Two remarks. q is not linear in y (it is a value of the quadratic map sigma), which is why (c) and
(d) pick one vector per matrix instead of asking for a linear map. And none of (c) or (d) is
sufficient: they are necessary conditions, to be used as a filter before a search.

## Job m93: the orbits of W

Job m93 (cube_groups/m93.py) enumerates the 3-dimensional commutative subspaces of M_4(F_2) without
I, one orbit under conjugation at a time: the 34 similarity classes of M_4(F_2) and their
centralisers by brute force, every subspace that contains a class representative built inside the
representative's centraliser, and each new subspace swept with all 20160 elements of GL_4(F_2). For
each orbit it records the conditions of Lemma 95: singular (every non-zero matrix singular), n4
(singular and (c)), f3 (singular and (d) with picks in D) and hyper (an invariant hyperplane, for
(N3) and (F3)), and descriptive invariants (ranks, common kernel, sum of images, invariant
hyperplanes, closure under products, nilpotent matrices, and whether W is a compression space).
Its checks do not depend on the sweep: 34 classes and 14 invertible ones (the generating functions
prod 1/(1 - 2x^i) and prod (1 - x^i)/(1 - 2x^i)); the number of commuting ordered pairs against
Feit and Fine's formula; the 2-dimensional commutative subspaces (I allowed) against
(commuting pairs - 196606) / 6; and the 3-dimensional orbit sizes against a double count over
class representatives. The predictions and the decision rule were written before the job file
(cube_groups/m93-predictions.md).

What its answer is for: if the orbits that meet n4, f3 or hyper are few, the next step is the
shape (c) search at (t, k) = (8, 4) with A_u of the given type and W fixed to an orbit
representative, as job m44 did at t = 7 (Lemmas 85 and 86), with ONE pilot instance first (1 GB,
short cap); if they are many, a hand lemma that removes whole families comes first (for example,
a common kernel vector of W, which Lemma 94(c) might rule out).

## Limits

- A VERIFIED certificate covers one CNF only. The encoding (m57.py on sat_aniso.py), the hints,
  Lemma 93's unit clauses, the orbit representatives of Propositions 30 to 34 and the reductions
  are code and hand arguments that no human has checked.
- Theorem 33 stays computer-assisted. It rests on many thousands of solver answers, of which 2149
  now carry checked certificates.
- Lemma 95 is a proof by hand with no computer check of its own; no human has checked it. It proves
  nothing about A(8) by itself, and m93 will only count cases.
- No human mathematician has checked Theorem 33 or any lemma it uses. Until someone does, read it
  as a claim with its evidence, not as an established result.

## Prior work

As for the earlier notes, searches on 2026-10-15 to 2026-10-23 found nothing on A(t), M(t) or
Question R other than MV2's own posts. CaDiCaL (A. Biere et al.) is a standard SAT solver; DRAT
proofs and the drat-trim checker are due to M. Heule, W. Hunt and N. Wetzler. The count of
commuting pairs is W. Feit and N. J. Fine (1960); commuting tuples of matrices over a finite field
up to simultaneous similarity are counted in arXiv 1409.2698 ("Simultaneous similarity classes of
commuting matrices over a finite field"), which counts ordered tuples, not subspaces, and not with
the conditions of Lemma 95; commutative matrix algebras are classical (D. A. Suprunenko and R. I. Tyshkevich,
"Commutative matrices", 1968); spaces of singular matrices and compression spaces go back to
J. Dieudonne (1949) and M. D. Atkinson and S. Lloyd (1980), and over F_2 to C. de Seguins Pazzis
(arXiv 1405.1575). A search on 2026-10-23 found no published list of the orbits m93 counts. Please
open an issue at https://github.com/munimtechnologies/kakeya-small-fields if you find an error.
