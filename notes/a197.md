## 1. Theorem 80: U*(4) = 30

*Upper bound.* a187 (Theorem 76). The solver also returned its own 30-point set (re-checked in pure
Python: sticky maps, 30 points, a line in all 40 directions):

    plane 0 (16): 0000 0020 0021 0022 0100 0112 0121 0200 0201 0202 0210 0211 0212 0220 0221 0222
    plane 1  (7): 1110 1111 1112 1120 1201 1210 1221
    plane 2  (7): 2001 2011 2020 2200 2220 2221 2222

*Lower bound.* By Theorem 74 (a186), U*(n) = Psi(n-1), the minimum over sticky Kakeya sets K' in
F_3^(n-1), full trees T of height n-1 and hidden slices h of |K' u S_h(T)| + sum over t != h of
|S_t(T)|. By Lemma 159 the slices of a full tree can be permuted as sets, so h = 0 suffices, and
the construction in the proof of Theorem 74 with h = 0 is: root map (0, 0, 0), subtree K' under
x = 0, a tree T' (= T + w) under x = 1, and its relabelled copy P_2(d'') = P_{T'}(-d'') under x = 2.
The "reduced" CP-SAT model of `sticky_exact.py` minimises |K| over exactly this family (with one
translation fixed per depth), so its optimum is U*(4). CP-SAT (OR-Tools 9.x, 4 workers) reported
**OPTIMAL, objective 30, bound 30** after 787.6 s. The unreduced model (no use of Theorem 74) found a
30-point set and a bound of 27 in its 3,000 s limit, consistent with this. ∎

Status: the lower bound rests on Theorem 74 (hand proof, a186) and on CP-SAT's optimality claim,
which is not an independently checkable certificate. A DRAT-certified re-proof would make it
fully checkable; we have not done that yet.

## 2. Proposition 93: 56 <= U*(5) <= 61

*Upper bound:* Theorem 77 (a187, colour lift of the 30-point set); the reduced CP-SAT run at n = 5
also returned a verified 61-point set (bound 33 after 3,600 s). *Lower bound:* Corollary 74.1
(a186), U*(5) >= min over a in A_4 and h of max(U*(4), a_h) + sum_{t != h} a_t, evaluated over node
outputs of the Pareto front of A_3 (112 vectors) by `sticky_exact.g5`: **56** with U*(4) = 30
(55 with the old bound 29). ∎

The general (non-sticky) Kakeya sets in F_3^5 include a 56-point one, so the pre-registered
"S5-SEPARATION" (U*(5) >= 57, sticky sets strictly worse already at n = 5) was **not** reached.

## 4. Lemma 166 (removing the offsets from kappa)

kappa(P) = min over i, w of |S_i| + |S_{i+1} u (S_{i+2} + w)| (a195). The rotation
rho_i(P) = P + i d has S_t(rho_i P) = S_{t+i}(P). For e in F_3^h, R(d) = P(d + e) + e is again a
sticky tree and S_t(R) = {P(d') + t d' - t e + e} = S_t(P) + (1 - t) e, so
|S_0(R)| + |S_1(R) u S_2(R)| = |S_0(P)| + |S_1(P) u (S_2(P) - e)|. Taking e = -w after rho_i gives
every (i, w). Hence **kappa_h = min over P of |S_0| + |S_1 u S_2|**. ∎
(`test_sticky_kexact.py` checks the identity on random trees of heights 1-3 for every i and w.)

## 5. Lemma 167 (kappa_4 is a pair problem)

For height-h trees A, B put lambda(A, B) = |A_0| + |B_0| + |B_1| + |B_2 u A_1 u A_2| and let
N = root x^2 over (A, B, B(-e)). Since S_t(B(-e)) = S_{-t}(B), the slices of N in the planes
u = x^2 + t x are N_0 = {0} x A_0 u {1} x B_0, N_1 = {0} x (A_1 u B_2) u {2} x B_1,
N_2 = {0} x (A_2 u B_2) u {2} x B_1, so |N_0| + |N_1 u N_2| = lambda(A, B) and kappa_{h+1} <= lambda_h.

Conversely, let P have height 4 and kappa(P) <= 31. By Proposition 92 we may take root x^2, i = 0
and w = (0, w'); the map R(d) = P(d + e) + e with e = (0, -w') keeps the root x^2 and gives w = 0.
With children A, B, C under x = 0, 1, 2 the value is
|A_0| + |B_0 u C_0| + |A_1 u C_1 u A_2 u B_2| + |B_1 u C_2| >= lambda(A, B). Since lambda_3 <= 31
(the a196 pair), **kappa_4 = lambda_3**. ∎

So kappa_4 = 30 (sticky base <= 30^(1/5) = 1.97435, and then pi_5 = 27,000 exactly) holds iff two
height-3 trees reach lambda = 30. The annealing job `sticky-pair-v1` (queued) searches this
heuristically; `sticky-kexact-v1` (pre-registered today) solves it exactly by CP-SAT, together with
a Prop-92-free direct model of kappa_4 and hinted searches for lambda_4 <= 61 and kappa_5 <= 61
(either would give beta_s <= 61^(1/6) = 1.98407).
