## Shard 30 third 1: why it is counted as an outside stop, not a hard case

The first case of the third, r3b_t8_B7_o011_c001_t3_m031_036 (ring type F2[x]/x3; 906,081
clauses, 142,855 variables; CNF SHA-256 335fa98a...a5742d), is recorded as CaDiCaL UNKNOWN after
**19,834 s**. The other 9 cases never started, because m134 starts no new case once 12,000 s of the
cert step have passed (NO_START_AFTER). The grade therefore failed 2 of its 23 checks
('all_started' and 'fresh_all_verified'); the other 21, including all controls, no SAT and no
error, passed.

We read this as the job being stopped from outside, not as a hard case, for two reasons:

1. **The cap is a wall-clock timeout held by the Python parent.** m104.run_cadical runs CaDiCaL
   through subprocess.run(..., timeout=1500) and measures time.time() around it. That timeout
   fires within moments of 1,500 s whenever the parent process is running, so it cannot overshoot
   by 18,334 s unless the parent itself was not running, i.e. the container (or the machine under
   it) was paused or suspended for about five hours.
2. **The proof is far too small for 1,500 s of solving.** CaDiCaL wrote 141,164,544 bytes
   (0.14 GB) of proof. The ten cases of third 0, on the same machine, wrote 0.9 to 2.3 MB of proof
   per CaDiCaL second, which puts this case at roughly 60 to 160 s of actual solving before it
   stopped.

The job's own logs do not record what paused it. Nothing about the case's satisfiability follows
from this attempt. Its files are kept, unchanged, under results/m134/attempt1/ (as
m134k30p1_attempt1_*.json), so the ordinary file names stay free for the fresh run.
