## Why this case

A(6) <= 7 holds if and only if no anisotropic system with t = 6 and dim V = 8 has an element u of
rank 3 in Case B of shape (c) (Corollaries 18 and 22). In shape (c), d = dim(X_1 n R) is 2 or 3,
where X_1 = ker A_u and R = Im A_u (Proposition 27(c4)). Job m13 (shape-c.md) split shape (c) into
38 orbits of commuting pairs (M_(v_1), M_(v_2)) and decided none of them in 4 minutes each. It did not
separate d = 2 from d = 3, and it used none of the later hand results.

When d = 3, R = X_1, so A_u^2 = 0 and the subspace L = [R] of X/X_1 in Lemma 46 is 0. Then three of
the hand results become **linear** conditions in the coordinates of the normal form, which a SAT
solver handles well:

- **(D)** A_u e_c lies in X_1 for c = 3, 4, 5 (R = X_1). Nine unit clauses.
- **(W)** For y = e_1, e_2, the element v_(2+j) of W with phi = e_j maps X_1 into X_1 (Proposition
  27(c2)) and induces exactly M_(v_j) on X/X_1 (Lemma 46(a) with L = 0). This fixes the last three
  rows of A_(v_3) and A_(v_4): 36 unit clauses.
- **(K)** M_(n_y) q(y) = 0 for y = e_1, e_2, e_1 + e_2 (Lemma 46(c) with L = 0; at t = 6 this is part
  of Corollary 23(a)). Here q(y) is the class modulo X_1 of sigma(v_3), sigma(v_4) and
  sigma(v_3 + v_4) = sigma(v_3) + sigma(v_4) + beta(v_3, v_4). Parity equations.

Since q(y) != 0 for y != 0 (Lemma 38(e)), (K) also says that M_(v_1), M_(v_2) and M_(v_1) + M_(v_2) are
all singular. Whether a non-zero element of span(M_(v_1), M_(v_2)) is invertible does not change under
the symmetries of Lemma 39, so the search keeps only the orbits whose span has no invertible element.
These include none of the 5 field orbits of Corollary 23(d). Of the 14 orbits whose pair spans at
most a line, it keeps 8: the pair (0, 0) and the 7 non-zero singular similarity classes of
M_3(F_2).

Every condition above follows from (*) by the hand proofs cited, so adding them removes no model in
the case d = 3. The case d = 2 is searched in [a6-d2.md](a6-d2.md) (job m19).

## The search (job m18-d3-v1)

`shape_c3.py` takes the CNF of `shape_c.encode_split` for the orbit's least pair (the CNF of job m13)
and appends (D), (W) and (K). It works for any (t, k) with t = 2k, so the case R = X_1 can also be run
at (4, 2) as a control. Each instance gets 7 minutes, under a total of 150 minutes for (6, 3).

The predictions, written before the job was queued (m18/predictions.md):

- the selfcheck passes: 3 kept classes of 2 x 2 matrices, 8 of 3 x 3 matrices, 8 kept pair orbits
  spanning at most a line, and every orbit with an invertible element dropped;
- (4, 2), controls: every kept orbit UNSAT, since A(4) = 4;
- (6, 3), exploratory. If every kept orbit is UNSAT, then shape (c) at t = 6 has d = 2, and
  A(6) <= 7 comes down to the case d = 2. That would rest on solver answers without proof
  certificates and on the hand proofs of Proposition 27(c2), Lemma 38(e) and Lemma 46. A SAT answer
  counts only if the model passes the brute-force check of (*), anisotropy, the normal form, the
  equations (S) of shape-c.md and (D), (W), (K). It would be an anisotropic system with t = 6 and
  dim V = 8, so A(6) = 8, and we would publish it with its matrices.

## Computation

The unit tests of `shape_c3.py` check the orbit filter against the hand counts above; that the
filter gives the same answer on every element of an orbit; that it drops exactly the orbits whose
span contains an invertible matrix; that the appended clauses accept exactly the coordinates that a
direct check of (D), (W) and (K) accepts, on random coordinates at (4, 2) and (6, 3) built to satisfy
them, on random ones that do not, and on one-bit changes of both; and that flipping any bit fixed by
(D) or (W) is rejected.

## Results of job m18 (added 2026-10-18)

Job m18-d3-v1 ran on 2026-10-18 from 10:04 to 10:09 UTC on one CPU (cadical195 through python-sat):

    (t, k)   kept orbits   prediction          result
    (4, 2)        3        UNSAT (control)     3 UNSAT, 0.3 s each
    (6, 3)       16        exploratory         16 UNSAT

At (6, 3) the kept orbits are 0 to 7, 14, 15, 19 to 22, 28 and 33 of the 38. Orbit 0, the pair
(0, 0), took 134 seconds; each of the other 15 took under 2 seconds. The selfcheck (3, 8 and 16
kept orbits for (2, 1), (3, 1) and (3, 2); 8 kept orbits spanning at most a line) and the 7 unit
tests passed, every graded prediction held, and no instance was left undecided.

So **shape (c) at t = 6 has d = 2**: no anisotropic system with t = 6 and dim V = 8 has an element of
rank 3 in Case B of shape (c) with R = X_1. This is a computer result without proof certificates
(no DRAT proof was saved), and it rests on the hand proofs of Proposition 27(c2), Lemma 38(e) and
Lemma 46 behind (W) and (K), on Corollary 23(a) for the orbit filter, and on the normal form. Job
m19 ([a6-d2.md](a6-d2.md)) found the case d = 2 UNSAT too, which gives A(6) <= 7 (Theorem 28,
[a6.md](a6.md)). Job m20 (a6.md, "Robustness") repeats these 38 orbits with (D) alone and no filter.
